## Main result

This tranche sharpens one cross-cutting result: **an entity, constitution, succession clause, mediator, or exit promise is only a safeguard when practical authority, money, evidence, and timing make it usable against the person or faction it is meant to constrain.**

1. **A spiritually ratified capture label is not a conduct record.** August Jacobi was admitted on probation, appointed to teach, and expelled six months later after what the membership record called a “Godly disclosure.” Leaders described falseness, pride, deception, and a ravenous wolf, but the source says the actual “trouble” is not recorded. The author's suggestion that Jacobi sought power is speculation. Probation and usefulness do not prove safety, but neither do confident deception labels establish a reviewable offense. (F-091)
2. **A guaranteed buyout can still make exit impracticable.** Moora Moora offered entry financing and guaranteed a community buyout after a two-year wait. Its original participant nevertheless reported expensive financing, entrant-first priorities, and a payout that was not commensurate with the cost of moving elsewhere. A real outward door needs a deadline, usable value, liquidity, hardship support, and household-scale transition capacity. (F-092)
3. **Outside mediation can arrive too late.** Moora Moora used an outside facilitator and a cooperative-wide circle, yet the author says conflict was hard to resolve after it exploded and informal supporters reinforced camps. Unresolved conflict ended in departures and repeated rebuilding. The account does not prove that earlier intervention would have solved a particular dispute, but it shows why an outside route needs pre-crisis legitimacy and an early trigger. (F-093)
4. **An outside agency may correct one legal fact while leaving the safety system unresolved.** After Edith Peshak died, Marie Ogden concealed the death inside the Home of Truth while attempting resurrection. Information apparently reached the sheriff from an unknown source as many members left. Officials inspected the remains but found no immediate health reason to require burial. Two years later, after a defector alleged secret cremation, Ogden signed a death certificate under pressure; authorities did not require disclosure of the remains or an answer to the allegation. The source does not show pre-death denial of medical care or prove cremation. The transferable issue is scope: inspection, record correction, evidence preservation, internal disclosure, and accountability are different functions. (F-094)
5. **A constitution becomes an override when a leader's proposed change can actually fail.** Shaker covenants were accessible, reread, and changed through member assent. An 1877 ministry proposal was not adopted; 1957 closure and asset powers required written approval from affected members; and Sabbathday Lake could proceed with admissions despite central disapproval. The article also says the leader expected obedience, so signatures cannot be assumed to prove independent consent. Even with that caveat, the failed amendment and local refusal are rare tests of practical constraint. (F-095)
6. **The purpose behind a protective entity form is itself a capture test.** Rodger McAfee proposed a perpetual land trust, a cooperative community, and a religious organization. He explicitly described the religious form as a way to control membership and expel people he perceived as secret agents. The source is sympathetic, mostly preserves McAfee's perspective, and does not show that the church was formed or used this way; it also does not validate his claims about the law or infiltrators. The durable design lesson is to audit who will operate a land lock or exclusion power, under what evidence standard, and whether an independent body can bind the founder. (F-096)
7. **A succession system the leader may refuse is no safeguard.** Laura Kohl Johnston reports that discussion of Jim Jones's declining health was taboo and that he flatly rejected members' proposal for a governing triumvirate. Rumored unhappiness led to public confrontation, while defectors eventually left with Congressman Leo Ryan. The catastrophe cannot be reduced causally to the refused triumvirate, and the source does not show that the proposed body was adequate. It does show why incapacity, dissent, and exit routes must activate outside the leader's veto. (F-097)
8. **A named successor needs real delegated practice before the crisis.** Cyrus Teed wrote a hierarchy around Victoria Gratia while casually disregarding it and denying her independent leadership experience. After his death, material and doctrinal crises produced conflict, departures, noncompliance, her removal by directors, redirection of contributions, a board election, and revision of founder doctrine. The reorganized Koreshan community then entered a comparatively stable period. The removal may have been factional or gender-biased and is not a fair danger adjudication; the useful mechanism is the combination of practiced delegation, independent financial power, election, amendment, and room for dissenting continuities. (F-098)
9. **A constructive exit can preserve both material runway and relationship.** A review of Robert Rhodes's memoir says Starland colony gave his departing family a vehicle and money and that a senior minister blessed him as a brother-at-large. The amounts, terms, and later outcomes are absent, and the reviewer calls the blessing likely unprecedented. Even as a thin positive lead, it identifies three useful parts of an outward door: household support, transition assets, and a non-governing alumni identity. (F-099)

## Dangerous-child result

The separate child-as-dangerous-actor search covered every extracted text in the 64-PDF unit. Discovery counted six substantive term families—danger, sanction, governance, child, exit, and clinical—and five process families—allegation, assessment, intervention, review, and outcome. It inspected child-danger terms in proximity and close-read 25 child candidates; the score was a reading-priority device, not an evidence weight.

The apparent child hits resolved into other categories:

- FLDS, Children of God, Peoples Temple, and related review passages concerned children as victims, dependents, or people governed by adults.
- *Wet Hot and Wild* described a child presented as a guru under adult pressure and later shunned, while a younger brother's suicidal crisis was spiritualized; neither child was a persistently dangerous actor.
- *Soul To Keep* concerned a child's suicidal crisis and an admission decision, not a complete child-safeguarding process with review and later outcome.
- *A Third Way* described community-wide adult discipline of children, monitoring, and excommunication but no case in which a child posed grave persistent danger.
- Other hits were childhood biography, ordinary discipline, aggregated “rebellious” adult children, adult war or conflict terms near child terms, children present at founder-death scenes, metaphor, or OCR proximity.

No source documented the requested sequence: **a persistently dangerous child as actor → allegation → assessment → intervention → review → later outcome**. F-100 records a bounded null for volumes 29-30. Together with F-031, F-048, F-064, F-076, and F-090, it extends the completed journal null through volume 30 without converting it into a historical claim of absence.

## Source-role and inference limits

- F-091 is strong evidence of a missing conduct record, not evidence of Jacobi's innocence or capture attempt.
- F-092 and F-093 are first-person organizational reflections; the buyout instrument, payout histories, and particular mediation cases remain unverified.
- F-094 does not establish denied medical care before Peshak's death, the identity of the first reporter, or the truth of the cremation allegation.
- F-095 is a corrective and advocacy-style historical argument grounded in cited documents; expected obedience complicates any inference of independent consent.
- F-096 records a founder's proposed design and self-described intent. It does not adopt his legal assertions, accept that secret agents existed, or show that the proposed religious organization operated.
- F-097 is retrospective survivor evidence and partially reconstructs a day the author did not witness; no single failed override is presented as a sufficient cause of Jonestown.
- F-098 is a historical governance sequence, not proof that the successor's removal was fair, safe, or free of gender and factional bias.
- F-099 is a short review of a memoir and describes apparently exceptional support; it is a retrieval lead before it is a replicable policy.
