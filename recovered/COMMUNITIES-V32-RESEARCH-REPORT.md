## Main result

This volume sharpens one cross-cutting result: **a legal form is protective only when its operational duties, control rights, review triggers, and ending rules work under conflict.**

1. **Long probation is an observed practice, not a validated danger filter.** A seven-community sustainability study found applicant testing periods of roughly six months or more, but it selected communities that already matched preferred organizational traits, had more than twenty members, and had survived at least five years. It recorded no accepted-versus-rejected comparison, false-positive or false-negative rate, dangerous-conduct outcome, or causal test. The study supports probation as a candidate mutual-acquaintance practice; it cannot support the article's stronger suggestion that long exposure reveals con artists. (F-106)

2. **Nonresistance does not eliminate financial accountability.** An 1881 visitor account says a charismatic White Water preacher who also controlled village funds stole a large sum and fled with a member around 1829; residents reportedly did not pursue him because of nonresistance. A contemporary elder's letter independently described the likely man as corrupt and dishonest but did not record theft. The durable lesson is role separation, accounts, evidence preservation, restitution, and lawful reporting without vengeance—not proof of the historical allegation. (F-107)

3. **Perpetual ownership can preserve land while preserving dysfunction.** Olive Jones's comparative study found that charitable trusts protected four New Zealand community properties from privatization. At Renaissance, however, most founders and the productive core left, collective enterprise faded, assets deteriorated, and the legal structure made sale, subdivision, closure, or individual recovery exceptionally difficult. Legal longevity therefore needs to be measured separately from mission, safety, maintenance, member freedom, and usable exit. An asset lock needs periodic purpose review and a recharter, successor, or humane sunset route. (F-108)

4. **Ultimate legal power is not an operative safety override.** Jones reports repeated former-resident appeals to the Renaissance trustees, especially over violence and illegal activities, but no intervention. The deed omitted operational duties; trustees could not agree; some were resident beneficiaries; and the founding ideology defined them as land-holders rather than community governors. The allegations are not adjudicated in the article. The transferable mechanism is narrower: an override needs an affirmative duty, conduct-based trigger, recusal standard, decision rule, interim authority, independent evidence route, and external-reporting boundary. (F-109)

5. **Title transfer can leave founder control untouched.** Wilderland's founders transferred the land to a charitable trust but remained trustees for life and controlled election or re-election of the other trustees. Jones reports resulting inequality, insecurity, cliques, and turnover. After the founders died, a family effort to reclaim and dismantle the community failed in court and a new board began formal planning. The structure protected the land from hereditary reclamation, but the earlier control arrangement shows why a deed audit must include appointment, removal, terms, succession, information, and resident voice—not just whose name holds title. (F-110)

## Source-role and inference limits

- F-106 establishes the design and limits of one study, not that probation is ineffective. A controlled or well-tracked protocol could still prove useful.
- F-107 is low-confidence historical evidence. Carter wrote more than fifty years after the alleged event, residents supplied a likely wrong name, and the contemporary letter records character accusations rather than theft.
- F-108 through F-110 benefit from Jones's founder, trustee, and thirty-five-year access, but that access is not independent. Her participant perspective and current/former resident interviews should be paired with deeds, minutes, court records, incident evidence, and other resident perspectives before publication.
- The New Zealand trust account is a 2012 snapshot and its legal discussion is not current legal advice. Present deed terms, law, governance, conditions, and resident views require fresh authoritative verification.
- F-109 records appeals reported by Jones, not findings that named people committed violence or illegal acts.
- No historical actor is diagnosed with ASPD or psychopathy. Conduct, structure, and response mechanisms remain separate from clinical diagnosis.

## Context retained without new findings

- Oneida's 1880 transition shows an institution preserving relationships, welfare commitments, housing, and common facilities after abandoning its original communal form. That materially deepens adaptive-dissolution evidence without adding a new principle.
- The kibbutz-of-kvutzot model embeds an individual in a primary small group, lets the group accompany a member in dealings with the wider institution, disperses committee work, and gives constituent groups blocking power. It is a useful design candidate, but the source does not test it against a malicious actor or later capture.
- Euree Street offers an independent instance of debt pressure overriding compatibility criteria: a mortgage required fast replacement of departing rent-payers. F-033 already documents the same mechanism more directly.
- Camphill's B.A.C.E. program reportedly produced more than one hundred participant research projects that informed internal debate and outside collaboration. It is a promising internal research function, but the review also notes unsupported assertions, underspecified methods, and overbroad claims.
- The Hutterite, Jonestown, and Children of God reviews add corroboration and verification leads, not sufficiently independent process-outcome evidence for separate findings.
