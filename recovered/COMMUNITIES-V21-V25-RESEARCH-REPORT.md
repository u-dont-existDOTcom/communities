## Main result

This chunk strengthens the distinction between removing a dangerous person and merely enforcing conformity. It also adds concrete ways to keep conflict reversible before it becomes expulsion, but still supplies no documented dangerous-child sequence.

1. **Delayed founder removal can become civil conflict.** At Icaria, a founder dispute escalated into building seizures, the near-strangling of an opponent, a school confrontation that terrified children, sheriff intervention, collective expulsion, separation, and dissolution litigation. The majority also denied necessities and used force, so this is not a simple heroic-expulsion story. It shows how late correction can leave no safe internal remedy. (F-065)
2. **An adjudication can protect the accused and endanger reporters.** Icaria's Assembly acquitted two people accused of violating sexual rules, then evicted the two accusers for slander and restricted broad applicant categories. The source does not let us decide whether this was justified sanction or retaliation. It does show why reporter protection, appeal, and proportionate consequences belong in the same procedure as protection against false allegations. (F-066)
3. **A warning-and-expulsion rule is not voluntary when the institution controls necessity and salvation.** The Jesus Fellowship handled unacceptable conduct through a warning and ultimately required departure, while community membership supplied food, shelter, and clothing and departure was framed as leaving the faith for evil “Babylon.” No case-specific evidence standard or transition process appears. (F-067)
4. **Graduated belonging can reduce the cost of exit.** The same fellowship developed several membership styles, so people could reduce residential commitment without leaving the church, and the article says capital placed in trust could be refunded on exit. The actual terms and contested-use performance still require verification. (F-068)
5. **High-need recruitment needs individualized support and conduct-specific assessment.** The Jesus Fellowship recruited heavily among homeless and poor young people. The author describes weak integration, theft, disruption, temporary use of community necessities, and high turnover, but uses stigmatizing categories and supplies no individual assessment-to-outcome sequence. Poverty, substance use, emotional distress, and opportunism must remain separate from danger. (F-069)
6. **A whole-group process can normalize bullying while a protected subgroup produces correction.** At Black Bear Ranch, insiders described coercive relationship experiments, hidden noncompliance, and “group decision making” in which the loudest faction often prevailed. Separate women's meetings built enough backing for previously deferential members to challenge gendered labor and materially change practice. The correction was real but incomplete. (F-070)
7. **Family belonging does not require inherited governance rights.** Grasmere made adult children preferred guests rather than automatic members, avoiding both compulsory acceptance by existing adults and a later personalized rejection. New families moved through interview, visit, and six-month mutual probation. The design does not prove that probation catches skilled bad-faith applicants. (F-071)
8. **Conflict can be made reversible before it becomes a verdict.** Grasmere used a full go-round, deferral, or a time-limited experiment with later evaluation when consensus failed. A separate living-conflict meeting, rotating facilitation, and written minutes addressed hurt and exclusion. Most routine conflicts reportedly cleared; dissolution bitterness did not. (F-072)
9. **Formal democracy can sit beneath a shadow government.** Ananda had an elected council and committees, while an informal council directed both legal entities and the founder retained exceptional spiritual authority. Longevity, service, positive attitude, spiritual progress, and attunement with him were pathways to power. A later harassment scandal is mentioned without an allegation-response account and must not be causally attached to this arrangement without further evidence. (F-073)
10. **Peer therapy needs a firewall against emotional authority and victim-blaming.** Morehouse trained non-therapists to lead intense encounter exercises and manipulate emotional highs and lows while teaching that people create their experience and adopt a victim stance when they perceive deprivation. The source provides no direct adverse-event study, so the material finding is the unguarded combination of mechanism and authority, not proof of abuse. (F-074)
11. **Low visible violence does not validate punishment-centered child discipline.** The Hutterite article reports serious youth violence and drug use as practically nonexistent, while adult authority used praise, punishment, and sometimes corporal discipline. Some outside teachers described detection-centered lying, cheating, and theft. The evidence does not establish a causal relationship or a dangerous-child case. (F-075)

## Dangerous-child result

The separate child-as-dangerous-actor search covered every extracted text in the 97-PDF unit. It paired child, children, boy, girl, youth, juvenile, minor, and adolescent terms with violence, aggression, assault, attack, killing, threats, harm, arson, delinquency, theft, weapons, coercion, punishment, exclusion, and related inflections, then inspected relevant hits in context and close-read the strongest sources.

The apparent hits resolved into other categories:

- Hutterite youth were described in aggregate as showing ordinary covert rule-breaking while serious violence was practically nonexistent.
- Grasmere's adult-child membership rule anticipated the possibility that a grown child could be disruptive; it documented no actual case.
- Icaria's children were frightened and endangered by an adult factional struggle; they were not the dangerous actors.
- Camphill and Catholic Worker sources concerned child autonomy, outside schooling, adult role models, and voluntary return.
- Fundamentalist Mormon book-review material described girls and children as victims of adult violence and coercion.
- Shaker sources discussed ordinary school discipline, affection control, spiritual excitement, and young adult apostasy.
- Other hits were about dangerous travel, metaphor, bibliography, adult theft near youth terms, or OCR proximity.

No source documented the requested sequence: **a persistently dangerous child as actor → allegation → assessment → intervention → review → later outcome**. F-076 records a bounded null for volumes 21-25. Together with F-031, F-048, and F-064, it shows repeated absence in processed journal chunks, not absence from history or the unprocessed literature.
