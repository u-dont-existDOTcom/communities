### 1. Peer competence / referral

### 2. Adulthood exit / freedom
Owner objection: r01's neutral transition package was too pie-in-the-sky and implicitly assumed communes could fund exploration. Ordinary families generally prepare children for a viable path rather than financing unrestricted exploration. Poor or money-free communes may have higher-priority needs. The deeper problem is the unavoidable path dependence of any upbringing plus the risk that children raised outside the money economy lack the practical knowledge to survive it and may be easily exploited/scammed.

Disposition: r02 rejects the guaranteed resource package. It explicitly states that every childhood narrows options; food, medical care, and basic survival may rightly outrank a travel/launch fund; the realistic standard is not unlimited freedom but avoiding artificially impossible departure. It adds outside-world competence (jobs, rent, contracts, banking, scams, identification, transport), portable skills/education, outside relationships, records/documents, and at least one plausible next path. Material transition help is conditional on capacity rather than an entitlement.

### 3. Federation accounting without money
Owner direction: explain how the federation logic works when communities do not use money.

Disposition: r02 generalizes the transfer taxonomy to food, medicine, tools/vehicles, labor, emergency housing, shared projects, and learning/relationship visits. Some create future obligations; some do not. Purpose-specific records do not require pricing everything or parity accounting. Cash categories remain only at the external interface when cash is actually used.

### 4. Finances / money-free communities
Owner direction: wherever new repair prose assumes money/finance, include communities that do not use money.

Disposition: r02 renames `Money, Land, and Exit Terms` to `Resources, Land, and Exit Terms` and recasts the repair around scarcity, bottlenecks, allocation authority, use rights, common stores, in-kind exit rights, and outside obligations. External accounts/debt remain visible only where they exist.

### 5. Move the whole article toward the author's goal of money-free societies
Owner direction: money-free society is not a local caveat; it is part of the article's desired direction.

Disposition: r02 adds the explicit long-term aim of no internal money under `4. The container`, while stating that abolishing money does not abolish scarcity or economic power. It updates the repair's resource/control language in the container, medical interface, federation, founder-power audit, capacity budget, land/resource section, membership, premove simulation, boundary standing, removal, dissolution, and Black Tax boundary. Existing autobiographical/historical references to money remain when money is the actual subject.

A new Black Tax boundary paragraph explains that internal life can be cashless while taxes, hospitals, visas, school fees, family obligations, imported tools, and other outside relations still use money; the design question is how to govern that interface without re-importing individual cash inequality/status.
