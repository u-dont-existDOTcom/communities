## G-021 — B — Experiment before fission: institutional R&D as a normal function

**Current article root:** Forks/fission are treated as an immune system and relationships may outlive institutions.

**Missing residual:** Institutional variation should often begin before crisis or social divorce. Use a gradient:

`microexperiment inside parent → semi-autonomous project using parent infrastructure → nearby residential seed with selected shared services → independent sister community in a local ecology/federation → distant branch only when needed`

The Louisa County Twin Oaks/Acorn/Living Energy Farm/Mimosa cluster is an empirical analogue, not proof of one deliberate incubation protocol. Preserve the C003 warning: some valuable institutional packages require land, scale, or complementary changes before a cheap prototype can look good.

**Why it matters:** A movement that makes full fission the main variation mechanism raises the social and capital cost of experimentation and may destroy relationships merely to test a rule.

**Likely destination:** forks / worldwide communal innovation / replication architecture.

**Evidence/provenance:** Batches 62–63; Louisa cluster reconstruction.

## G-022 — C — Boundary egalitarianism: standing should follow exposure, not only membership

**Current article root:** The member pipeline governs progress toward full internal voice.

**Missing residual:** A commune can be egalitarian among full members while volunteers, employees, interns, guests, dependents, renters, contractors, or sister-community workers become a low-voice service class.

Do not give nonmembers general political power merely because they interact with the community. Give **role-specific standing over decisions that materially affect them**: workplace safety/scheduling/discipline/compensation, rental access/maintenance/notice, childcare safeguarding/reporting, shared-enterprise duties and appeal, relevant records, etc.

Target: **shared infrastructure without exported disenfranchisement.**

**Why it matters:** Federation can recreate hierarchy in the spaces between egalitarian communities.

**Likely destination:** membership/labor architecture; federation/shared infrastructure.

**Evidence/provenance:** Batch 63; refines G-014/G-011 and the broader standing corpus.

## G-023 — C — Federation anti-starvation: translocal maintenance is productive capacity

**Current article root:** Federation, shared infrastructure, and movement continuity are valued, but federation upkeep is not treated as a separate scarce productive function.

**Missing residual:** The 2024–2026 FEC near-collapse/rebuild shows that member communes can remain viable while the federation layer loses liveness. Participant/rebuild records describe neglected legal/financial maintenance, delegate loss without replacement, unattended calls, and local crises absorbing the people who had carried federation work.

Reserve real labor/money for:

- delegate/officer succession and backups;
- legal/tax/compliance maintenance;
- records and finances;
- meetings/communications;
- minimum-operating-state checks;
- degraded mode and restart.

### Dispute-capacity update

FEC history also shows pressure for the federation to receive intercommunity/harm disputes even though it was not designed as a general government. Mediation/harm-response ideas did not become reliably durable when nobody had standing capacity. If a federation is expected to receive such cases, jurisdiction, staffing, handoff, recusal, evidence, and remedy cannot remain an aspirational volunteer project.

### Digital continuity/decommissioning update

Federation maintenance includes institutional identity:

- registrar/domain/DNS ownership;
- redirects and canonical-current-site notices;
- credential succession/revocation;
- read-only archival snapshots;
- retirement of obsolete forms/contact routes;
- policy version/effective dates;
- explicit deprecation of stale policy URLs;
- monitoring of retired official domains/pages long enough to detect misleading reuse.

The old FEC site and current official site now expose materially different membership-policy wording, illustrating why searchable stale policy must not masquerade as current federation law.

**Why it matters:** Healthy local communities do not automatically maintain the institution that lets them cooperate, and institutional dormancy can corrupt both operational continuity and public policy provenance.

**Likely destination:** federation/shared infrastructure; practical capacity budget; movement continuity.

**Evidence/provenance:** Batches 64–67.

## G-024 — B — Modularize federation services and separate service authority from financial-risk authority

**Current article root:** Bounded authority, selected couplings, independent review, and function-specific layers are already valued.

**Missing residual:** Do not make every critical federation function depend on one general-purpose federation body. Use overlapping layers where appropriate:

1. bilateral/local-cluster relationships;
2. function-specific institutions;
3. the general federation/corporate coordination layer.

PEACH is an architecture lead because current FEC material describes it as independently governed from general FEC delegates. This is **not** causal proof that governance separation produced its continuity.

Louisa evidence also shows useful bilateral/local ties surviving formal federation weakness or membership rupture; the federation entity is not the whole network.

### Financial-risk boundary from the Israeli cooperative crisis

A second communal-federation comparison strengthens the modularity rule. Israeli higher-order supply/service cooperatives expanded into credit intermediation backed by mutual liability. World Bank analysis describes control problems and cases where failing regional enterprises pulled service/supply cooperatives into financing operating losses and debt service. Crisis restructuring proposed returning supply cooperatives to direct supply/marketing functions and removing financial intermediation.

For each function-specific federation body, distinguish authority over:

- service operations;
- ownership/capital commitments;
- ordinary accounts payable;
- lending/borrowing;
- guarantees/surety;
- pooled-risk claims;
- emergency grants.

A health pool, transport service, purchasing cooperative, archive, training network, or review body should not acquire movement-wide credit/guarantee powers by institutional drift. If broader financial powers are needed, treat them as a separately governed risk-bearing function with caps, reserves, visibility, succession, and failure isolation.

### Shared-enterprise exit

For shared enterprises, distinguish already-committed fixed costs from future variable/use costs. Define owner exit/termination/buyout consequences before investment; neither the leaver nor remaining nodes should discover the liability during crisis.

**Why it matters:** Federation failure is often partial. Useful functions should be able to survive general dormancy, while failure in a risky function should not automatically take down unrelated services.

**Likely destination:** federation/shared infrastructure; function-specific subsidiarity; movement living-will architecture.

**Evidence/provenance:** Batches 64–68; FEC PEACH/rebuild; Louisa bilateral ties; Kislev/Lerman/Zusman 1989; Rosenthal/Eiges 2014.

## G-025 — C — Federation membership needs a lifecycle, including stable non-convergent affiliation

**Current article root:** Individuals have membership stages; communities have succession/fission/dissolution concepts, but federation member communities do not have a comparable accreditation lifecycle.

**Missing residual:** Accreditation must not attach permanently to a name, site, corporation, or historical reputation. Track separately:

- people/member composition;
- land/site;
- legal entity/control;
- governance/economic rules;
- mission/constitutional commitments;
- federation accreditation.

Material change should trigger review, not punishment. Useful states include:

`forming → in-dialogue → full → re-forming → dormant → dissolved`

But the FEC history/current rebuild adds an important correction: **not every non-full relationship should be transitional toward convergence**.

Current FEC relationship categories distinguish Full Members, Communities in Dialogue working toward full membership, and Friends that either do not meet full criteria or choose not to pursue membership. Historical Allied status served a similar need for communities such as Ganas/Living Energy Farm.

Therefore also preserve a **stable non-convergent friend/ally state**. A movement that values experimental diversity should not quietly make every cooperative relationship an assimilation ladder.

Current FEC status transitions are active rather than merely archival: Alpha Farm moved from Community in Dialogue in the 2025 rebuild update to Full Member on the current site.

**Why it matters:** A federation needs to preserve trust after major institutional change and preserve cooperation with communities that remain valuable without becoming institutional copies.

**Likely destination:** federation membership architecture; succession/fission; lineage/outcome measurement.

**Evidence/provenance:** Batches 64–65; FEC Re-forming policy; current Friends/CID/full-member categories.

## G-026 — B — Federation mobility needs a usable interface, not merely permission

**Current article root:** G-012 already owns portable exit benefits; G-016 already names intercommunity migration as continuity infrastructure.

**Missing residual:** A federation can formally permit people to move while autonomous communities use incompatible labor, accounting, health, tax, housing, benefit, status, and record systems that make movement administratively expensive or unsafe.

Historical FEC/Twin Oaks dual-membership material is unusually direct: residence changes required cross-community labor, accounting, tax, health, and PEACH coordination, and the policy allowed communities to limit switching frequency if labor/money accounting became too burdensome.

Operationalize:

- primary/secondary responsibility during transition;
- status recognition/probation;
- housing/queue/notice rules;
- labor balance/obligation settlement where relevant;
- health/medical and pooled-benefit responsibility;
- tax/accounting responsibility;
- consent-bounded record transfer;
- reimbursement;
- grace periods preventing coverage/basic-needs/standing gaps;
- transition-specific dispute forum;
- mobility-friction metrics such as administrative hours, delayed/aborted moves, benefit gaps, and switching restrictions.

### 2026 correction: interoperability can be a thin adapter

The current FEC Exchange Program shows that temporary mobility does **not** require harmonizing member-community labor systems. It replaced the old LEX/LETS architecture with a bounded interface: sending/host consent, coordinator/host roles, federation travel funding, host accommodation, simple host-work expectation, full home-community labor credit, no intercommunity labor-debt ledger, and assigned emergency/problem handling.

Core rule:

> **Standardize the minimum interface necessary to make movement usable; preserve local systems unless deeper portability actually requires harmonization.**

Temporary exchange is not an entitlement to residential membership; local admission/capacity remains selective.

**Why it matters:** Institutional diversity does not produce practical member choice if switching between experiments is procedurally expensive.

**Likely destination:** federation/shared infrastructure; forks/continuity; exit and member mobility.

**Evidence/provenance:** Batches 65–66; historical FEC/Twin Oaks/East Wind mobility rules; current 2026 FEC Exchange Program.

## G-027 — B — Do not force every intercommunity transfer into one parity ledger

**Current article root:** Federation, mutual aid, shared infrastructure, and movement are already supported.

**Missing residual:** Different transfers have different economic meanings. Historical FEC LEX treated labor as reciprocal and balance-sensitive. In a 2010 East Wind stress episode, FEC used a distinct direct-aid budget. The current 2026 FEC Exchange Program explicitly says it replaced LEX/LETS because the old arrangements were transactional and falsely assumed parity between communities; the replacement has no formal labor debt/tracking and includes skill-sharing, mutual aid, relationship-building, renewal, and exploration.

Classify the transfer **before** choosing the accounting:

1. grant/direct aid;
2. pooled reserve/insurance-like protection;
3. loan;
4. guarantee/surety;
5. shared-enterprise capital/fixed-cost commitment;
6. reciprocal exchange with actual settlement expectation;
7. non-transactional training/exploration/relationship transfer.

Use a debt ledger only when repayment is genuinely part of the agreement. Do not silently turn emergency aid or federation-funded exploration into bilateral debt.

### Solidarity is not joint liability

The Israeli kibbutz/moshav cooperative crisis provides the opposite boundary. Movement-wide mutual guarantees helped pool risk and widen credit, but also transmitted liabilities across nodes when macroeconomic conditions shifted and heavily indebted members/regional organizations failed.

Therefore:

- `mutual aid` must not silently authorize guarantees;
- guarantee/surety authority should be explicit, separately approved, visible, and bounded;
- publish gross and contingent exposure;
- stress-test simultaneous guarantee calls against liquidity;
- do not retroactively convert grants into debt;
- distinguish committed shared-enterprise fixed costs from future-use obligations.

The target is **purpose-specific accounting and liability**. FEC's old system could be too transactional for aid; broad mutual guarantees can make solidarity too financially coupled.

**Why it matters:** A distressed/smaller community should not become least eligible for support merely because it has already received more, but neither should vague solidarity expose every community to everyone else's debt.

**Likely destination:** federation/shared infrastructure; mutual aid; experimental-ecology movement.

**Evidence/provenance:** Batches 66 and 68; historical/current FEC exchange policies; 2010 East Wind aid; Israeli cooperative-finance crisis literature.

## G-028 — B — Federation principles need a constitutional casebook, not only abstract clauses

**Current article root:** The article has constitutional/value commitments and federation standards. Strict Creative-Tail survivor C001 already supplies the underlying normative edge-case/regression architecture.

**Missing article application:** A FEC historical reconstruction reports that Short Mountain Sanctuary left the federation in 1987 when its queer-sanctuary admission policy was treated as conflicting with the federation anti-discrimination principle. Later FEC practice distinguished blanket identity restrictions from some demographic-balancing preferences. Current September 2025 full-member criteria explicitly permit affinity around a specific oppressed identity under stated conditions while retaining an anti-discrimination rule.

Do **not** infer that the 2025 revision was caused by Short Mountain, and do not use the evidence to declare the 1987 decision obviously right or wrong.

The institutional residual is interpretation memory. For each high-information constitutional edge case, preserve:

1. concrete facts and actual decision;
2. principles plausibly in tension;
3. independent pre-deliberation judgments where feasible;
4. final decision/rationale;
5. dissent/minority reasoning;
6. affected-party input/material consequences;
7. scope and limits of the precedent;
8. later outcomes if observable;
9. exact constitution/policy version;
10. whether later rule changes would change the result.

When a constitutional rule is revised, rerun the stored case suite and explicitly record changed outcomes.

This does not automate the substantive morality. It stops a federation from forgetting the hard question and rebuilding its interpretation under each new crisis.

**Relationship to other gaps:** G-025 supplies stable non-full affiliation when constitutional criteria diverge; G-023 supplies policy-version provenance. C001 is the underlying strict Creative-Tail mechanism; G-028 is the federation/article application, not a new strict survivor. C012 can supplement case precedent with relation-level tests.

**Why it matters:** A federation can lose a useful community even when both sides endorse equality if its abstract rule has no durable treatment of the hard boundary case.

**Likely destination:** federation constitutional floor; membership architecture; preserved variation/experimental ecology.

**Evidence/provenance:** Batch 67; FEC/Short Mountain history; historical FEC criteria; East Wind demographic-balancing policy; September 2025 current FEC full-member criteria.
