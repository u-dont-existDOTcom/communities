## Main result

Volume 33 adds three linked design lessons: **usable exit must exist before a rupture, protected dissent needs a channel with actual voice rather than nominal access, and an independent leaver network can supply support and counter-accountability without repairing the parent institution.**

1. **Dependency can turn dissent into reliance on an untested rescuer.** The Count Leon study says unhappy Harmonists depended on the Rapps, lacked outside survival skills, and had surrendered earnings and inheritances into accounts controlled by Friedrich Rapp. A society document listed petition signers by family and noted vulnerabilities such as poverty, parents remaining, or a spouse still obligated; the author says it appears to have been used to pressure reconsideration. After a failed leadership challenge, Leon's party financed legal representation and offered a new communal project, and 176 adults with 75 children left together. The source does not prove coercive use of the list or deny dissident agency. The transferable point is to provide protected organizing, independent advice, an exit reserve, portable skills, family-contact protection, and neutral separation support before a member bloc must choose between staying and trusting a charismatic outsider. (F-112)

2. **A shared dissent forum can preserve conflict while distributing voice unevenly.** During the long Shaker progressive-conservative dispute, the official monthly carried competing views. The Central Ministry defended the freedom to express differences, pre-screened submissions, rejected some material as too controversial, and refused to silence progressive leader Frederick Evans. Yet progressives dominated early issues, Evans later became editor, and many conservatives were reluctant to publish. The article describes continued argument without formal rupture, but it does not establish that the journal caused unity or treated viewpoints equally. A modern channel needs viewpoint-neutral rules, transparent editorial allocation, minority access, appeal, and durable records—not merely permission to submit. (F-113)

3. **A leaver network can be exit infrastructure and a counter-archive while remaining a weak reform mechanism.** Bruderhof leavers created the KIT newsletter, gatherings, the Hummer email network, memoir publishing, archives, a limited assistance fund, and later educational, financial, health, and relational support. KIT kept public and private subscriber lists because some participants feared identification could jeopardize remaining family contact. The authors report strong results for contact and heritage, mixed healing, and little success changing the Bruderhof or securing unrestricted family access. This supports an optional, independently controlled alumni channel with confidentiality and practical transition support; it does not validate every participant allegation or establish outcomes for nonparticipants. (F-114)

## Source-role and inference limits

- F-112 combines archival sources with the author's reconstruction. The source itself says the vulnerability list *appears* to have been used for pressure; that inference is not converted into an adjudicated fact.
- F-113 documents a forum and its asymmetries. The continued Shaker organization does not establish that the journal prevented rupture or that a similar forum would withstand a malicious actor.
- F-114 is written by long-term KIT participants and uses about twenty self-selected focus-group participants from a purposive sample. It is a case study of a participant network, not a representative outcome study of all Bruderhof leavers.
- F-114 does not independently verify wiretapping, litigation, abuse, retaliation, or family-contact allegations. Those remain in the verification queue and are not the basis of the promoted mechanism.
- No historical actor is diagnosed with ASPD or psychopathy. Conduct, institutional design, source role, and response process remain separate from clinical diagnosis.
