## Promoted findings

### F-163 — response repertoire plus failed separation authority (C)

Zablocki's Table 6-11 reports methods ever used across 120 communes: discussion by the people involved (88 percent), group meeting (76 percent), mutual criticism (75 percent), expulsion or threat (44 percent), removal from office (40 percent), membership tribunal (31 percent), therapeutic counseling (24 percent), temporary exile (4 percent), activity exclusion (4 percent), and privilege withdrawal (1 percent). The table is not an effectiveness measure.

The same source reports that about one-quarter of rural communes denied any authority to expel and describes one rural anarchistic case in which an adult's drinking, bullying, theft, abuse, and occasional violence coexisted with fear, weak requests to leave, assaults by other men, mockery by children, and cruel practical jokes. The adult remained. The conduct is retained; the source's diagnostic and scapegoat speculation is not.

The bounded design implication is advance definition of the conduct threshold, immediate protection, decision authority, evidence review, recusal, appeal, and fair property settlement. Discussion and informal pressure cannot be assumed to handle every dangerous case.

### F-164 — staged access to childcare during provisional membership (B)

One Atlanta commune participant reported that a new resident did not initially attend meetings or perform childcare while the group observed the resident's relationship with children. Access later expanded when the group judged the relationship and long-term commitment acceptable.

This establishes only that residence and sensitive-role access were separated in one case. It provides no written criteria, child or parent input, observation method, time limit, appeal, error rate, incident, or safety outcome. The transferable design question is whether childcare, governance, finances, transport, records, and other sensitive roles should each have explicit provisional gates.

### F-165 — decoupling productive skill from allocation authority (B)

Lee reports that 34 percent of hunters accounted for 79 percent of recorded kudu kills. Formal meat-distribution authority belonged to the owner of the first arrow, not necessarily the hunter, and arrows circulated through hxaro exchange. In a check of four men's quivers, three held arrows from four to six other men and two held none of their own.

The source therefore documents partial separation of productive skill, formal allocation authority, credit, and distribution burden. It does not establish a causal effect on dominance, violence, equality, or nutrition. Ridicule, food taboo, kinship exchange, and arrow ownership are not modern prescriptions; only the structural separation question transfers for further analysis.

### F-166 — interruption, cooling, fission, and outside adjudication (C)

Lee recorded 58 arguments and fights during three years of fieldwork, including 34 with blows. He also assembled retrospective accounts of 22 confirmed homicides and at least 15 woundings from 1920-1955, with the strongest cases corroborated by multiple informants.

The documented response repertoire includes third-party restraint after blows, principals leaving for cooling periods, group splitting and later recombination, long separation between n!ores, and increasing use of a Tswana court for serious disputes. The internal mechanisms exposed bystanders and sometimes failed. Outside law did not eliminate violence and existed within a colonial and coercive setting.

The transferable design question is a nonlethal ladder separating immediate interruption, voluntary cooling space, reversible housing separation, and independent adjudication. Community-sanctioned killing, feud retaliation, summary execution, banishment, and colonial coercion are explicitly excluded.

### F-167 — graduated internal discipline with a captured-review weakness (C)

Kraybill describes a sequence from report or observation through a private leader visit, a public members' meeting for serious matters, an opportunity for the accused to explain, a minister-proposed sanction, a congregational vote, a six-week temporary ban, and review for reinstatement. Refusal to submit could lead to excommunication and shunning.

The same chapter says congregations generally affirmed the bishop's proposal and reports a member pressing action against a bishop's son, followed by the bishop threatening that member with excommunication over a telephone. The source offers no recusal, independent appeal, fairness denominator, safety outcome, or correction of the conflict.

The bounded lesson is that graduated steps, a time limit, review, and reintegration do not by themselves create independent adjudication. Public humiliation, compelled confession, shunning, and conformity sanctions are not endorsed.

Across all eight sources, the recorded search and close-read method found no intentional-community case containing the required sequence: a child who remained gravely dangerous, a conduct-specific allegation, assessment, intervention, review, and later outcome.

## Contested-source control: *The Mountain People*

Turnbull's reported child conduct was not promoted because the case sequence is incomplete and the work's generalized account is disputed. Bernd Heine later described the account as biased and incomplete and called for more comprehensive study ([Heine 1985, DOI 10.2307/1159836](https://doi.org/10.2307/1159836)). A later study using participant observation and Dictator Games with a convenience sample of 120 Ik adults reported generosity within the range found in other populations and did not support a culture-wide selfishness characterization ([Townsend et al. 2020, DOI 10.1017/ehs.2020.22](https://doi.org/10.1017/ehs.2020.22)). That later study has its own sample limits and does not adjudicate every historical incident.

Turnbull's proposed military roundup, random separation of men, women, and children, forced language loss and assimilation, infant removal, and hoped-for disappearance of the Ik are unethical, coercive, unsupported, and rejected. They are not an intervention candidate.

## Other explicit non-promotions

- Kanter's 9 long-lived versus 21 shorter-lived community comparison uses longevity and retention as its outcome. Its source-availability, selection, and functionalist limits prevent conversion of commitment mechanisms into safety evidence.
- Oakley's system-level observations about opacity and checks do not cure the retrospective diagnostic framing or create a community protocol. No historical actor is diagnosed in this checkpoint.
- Wrangham's review explains a proposed evolutionary mechanism and explicitly notes testing and modeling gaps. It is retained as theory only; lethal punishment and conformity pressure are not design recommendations.
- Zarpentine's discussion distinguishes psychopathy from antisocial personality disorder and notes construct and instrument disputes. It does not authorize lay screening or retrospective diagnosis.
- Zablocki's post-exit changes and charismatic-survival analysis either have response-bias limits or duplicate existing findings and were not promoted.
