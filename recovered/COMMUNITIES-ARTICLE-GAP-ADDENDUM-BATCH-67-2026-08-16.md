## G-028 — B — Federation principles need a constitutional casebook, not only abstract clauses

**What the article currently says:** The article has strong values/constitutional commitments and increasingly treats the federation as a real governance layer. Existing Creative Tail survivor C001 already establishes the value of active normative edge-case search and durable regression cases. The article-facing federation architecture does not yet apply that mechanism to federation membership standards.

**What the FEC evidence adds:** A later FEC history reports that Short Mountain Sanctuary entered the federation orbit in 1984 and dropped membership in 1987 when its queer-only sanctuary policy was treated as conflicting with the FEC anti-discrimination principle.

FEC practice later made finer distinctions. An older full-member criteria page says blanket identity restrictions are not acceptable while demographic quotas may be acceptable; historical East Wind policy used a three-to-one housing preference for the minority gender during imbalance.

The current full-member criteria, revised September 12, 2025 and linked from the official FEC policy page, explicitly say the criteria are guides involving judgment calls in situations that cannot all be anticipated. The current discrimination criterion allows communities to be formed around affinity for a specific oppressed identity provided they do not discriminate against other oppressed identities, while also requiring efforts to reduce structural access barriers.

Do **not** infer that the 2025 revision was caused by the Short Mountain case. The evidence establishes a salient historical edge case plus later policy evolution, not a causal drafting history.

**Residual:** Abstract principles such as `oppose discrimination` do not uniquely decide hard cases involving sanctuary, affinity, remedial demographic preference, and federation membership. If those cases are not preserved as precedent, the federation is forced to reconstruct its constitutional interpretation under crisis conditions and later policy revisions can silently change the answer.

### Operational requirement

Maintain a versioned federation constitutional casebook/regression suite. For each high-information edge case, preserve:

- concrete facts and the actual institutional decision;
- the constitutional principles plausibly in tension;
- independent pre-deliberation judgments where feasible;
- final decision and rationale;
- minority/dissent reasoning;
- affected-party input and material consequences;
- the scope/limits of the precedent;
- later outcomes if observable;
- the exact policy/constitution version used;
- whether a later rule revision changes the result.

When the federation revises a principle or membership rule, rerun the stored cases. Publish changed outcomes rather than allowing the old precedent and new rule to coexist implicitly.

The casebook does not resolve the substantive morality automatically. It preserves the hard question in a form that can be compared, challenged, revised, and taught to daughter/friend/member communities.

**Why a reader would care:** A federation can lose a useful community not because either side rejects equality, but because an abstract equality rule has no agreed treatment of a hard boundary case. Institutional memory should make the next disagreement more informed than the last one.

## G-023 additional digital-version update

The old FEC full-membership page and the current official 2025 criteria now expose materially different discrimination/admission wording. This is more consequential than simple archival clutter: search can surface an obsolete federation rule as though it were current.

Add to G-023:

- every externally visible policy needs a version/effective date;
- obsolete policy URLs should carry an explicit deprecated banner or redirect to the current canonical version;
- a policy revision should preserve the old text as a labeled historical snapshot rather than leave two unlabeled operational-looking rules;
- high-consequence membership/safety/financial policies should have a current-policy index that can be audited against search-visible stale copies.
