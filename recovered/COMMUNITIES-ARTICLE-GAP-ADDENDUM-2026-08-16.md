## G-021 — B — Experiment before fission: institutional R&D as a normal function

**What the article currently says:** Forks are an immune system; a community can end or divide while relationships persist. The article already recognizes experimentation, replacement, and the possibility that relational continuity outlives an institution.

**What the newer work adds:** Do not make full fission, crisis, or incompatibility the default entry point for institutional variation. Where feasible, allow a small group to test a different practice or institutional package inside the parent community or immediately beside it while retaining selected shared infrastructure, relationships, services, and resources. Treat autonomy as a gradient rather than a binary event:

`microexperiment inside parent → semi-autonomous project using parent infrastructure → nearby residential seed with shared services → independent sister community inside a local ecology/federation → distant branch only when needed`

The Twin Oaks / Acorn / Louisa evidence lead is materially closer to this architecture than classical fission. Twin Oaks helped establish nearby Acorn; the communities retained dense social and labor ties, and later nearby experiments received labor, land, business, or other interfaces from older communities. The evidence does **not** establish a single deliberate incubation protocol or prove that clustering caused survival, so this remains an empirical analogue and design lead rather than validation of the full model.

A proof-first rule also needs a limit: some institutional variants require land, scale, or several complementary changes before any component can look good. Do not systematically starve variants merely because they are less cheaply prototypeable; this connects to the existing C003 asset-gated cultural-mutation concern.

**Why a reader would care:** The article currently makes variation look too much like something that happens when a community needs to fork. A mature communal system could instead make institutional experimentation routine, cheap, reversible, and socially non-destructive. That is a different growth model: innovation without requiring social divorce or rebuilding every shared service from zero.

**Likely destination:** Forks / worldwide communal innovation / formation and replication architecture.

**Evidence/status:** Batch 62 operational synthesis and Louisa empirical leads; existing G-016 provides the partial article root. Classification is **B**, not C, because the article already owns forks and continuity.

## G-022 — C — Boundary egalitarianism: standing should follow exposure, not only membership

**What the article currently says:** Members progress through a pipeline toward full voice and the internal community is designed around egalitarian membership.

**What the corpus and Louisa extension add:** A commune can be internally egalitarian while recreating hierarchy at its boundary. Volunteers, employees, interns, guests, dependents, renters, contractors, and participants from sister communities may materially support the system while lacking meaningful voice over the decisions that affect them.

The nearby-cluster model makes the problem sharper. A person can be a full member/citizen in Community A while working, renting land, providing childcare, or using infrastructure in Community B. They should not automatically receive full governance power in B, but they need **role-specific standing that follows exposure**:

- workplace participants need voice/review over safety, scheduling, compensation, discipline, harassment, and relevant records;
- renters/land users need standing over access, maintenance, notice, liability, and dispute resolution;
- childcare-exchange participants need safeguarding, reporting, and review rights even when they are not members of the host community;
- shared enterprises need named operational ownership and an appeal route that is not defeated by the worker belonging politically to another commune.

The design target is **shared infrastructure without exported disenfranchisement**.

**Why a reader would care:** A federation or experimental cluster can appear egalitarian inside each member commune while quietly creating a low-voice interstitial labor/service class. Auditing equality only among full members misses where a growing communal ecosystem may reintroduce the hierarchy it claims to escape.

**Likely destination:** Membership/labor architecture; federation/shared-infrastructure section.

**Evidence/status:** Refines existing G-014 (class C), G-011, T-01/S-01, and Batch 63. This is an article-facing synthesis, not a new empirical finding.

## G-023 — C — Federation anti-starvation: translocal maintenance cannot be leftover labor

**What the article currently says:** The article values federation, shared infrastructure, intercommunity support, and movement-level continuity, but does not treat upkeep of the federation itself as a scarce productive function with its own failure mode.

**What the newer evidence adds:** The Federation of Egalitarian Communities provides a direct warning. In 2024 the general federation layer became close to nonfunctional while major member communes continued to exist. A participant account described legal and financial trouble after required paperwork was not maintained, delegates leaving without clear replacements, later conference calls with effectively nobody attending, and local community work/crises absorbing the attention of people who had previously maintained the federation. The FEC's current official site describes the organization as being in an active rebuilding phase; a May 2025 delegate update listed corporate registration/tax filing, policy review, and recovery of website control among the central rebuilding priorities.

The relevant design lesson is not merely `do the paperwork`. Federation work is a **second-order commons**: each local community receives benefits from a functioning translocal layer, while the immediate cost of delegate time, compliance, records, finance, succession, meetings, and maintenance competes against urgent local work. If federation labor is the residual claimant on communal attention, simultaneous local stress can starve the layer precisely when shared coordination becomes most valuable.

Treat a minimum federation operating capacity as real production rather than volunteer overhead. At minimum define:

- reserved labor/financial capacity for federation maintenance;
- primary + backup ownership for secretary/treasurer/compliance/records/delegate functions;
- automatic replacement/escalation when a delegate or officer becomes inactive;
- a minimum viable operating-state check for meetings, filings, records, finances, communication, and critical shared services;
- explicit degraded-mode and restart procedures when the general federation layer falls below that floor.

This connects to the existing governance-liveness work, but the article does not currently apply liveness to **translocal maintenance capacity itself**.

**Why a reader would care:** A network of healthy communes can still lose the institution that lets them cooperate. If nobody owns the boring meta-work robustly, federation benefits can disappear without any member commune actually failing.

**Likely destination:** Federation/shared infrastructure; movement continuity; practical capacity budget.

**Evidence/status:** Post-corpus empirical lead from FEC 2024–2026 rebuild history. This is an article-facing operational gap, not a strict novelty claim.

## G-024 — B — Modularize critical federation services away from general federation dormancy

**What the article currently says:** The article already favors bounded authority, selected couplings, independent review, and function-specific institutional layers.

**What the newer evidence adds:** During the FEC rebuilding phase, the current official FEC site describes PEACH—the catastrophic-health mutual-aid program—as governed independently from the general FEC by representatives of participating communities. Older FEC documentation likewise distinguishes PEACH delegates from federation delegates and says the federation delegates have no authority over PEACH.

That architecture suggests a useful resilience principle: **do not make every critical shared function depend on the liveness of one general-purpose federation body**. Health risk pooling, records, safeguarding review, loan funds, training, dispute review, or other high-consequence services may warrant their own bounded governance, succession, accounts, and restart plans, with explicit interfaces to the federation rather than being mere subcommittees whose operation stops when federation attention collapses.

The evidence does **not** establish that PEACH's institutional separation caused its continuity through the FEC's 2024–2025 dysfunction, nor does it establish uninterrupted performance. Treat PEACH as an architecture lead, not a causal validation.

**Why a reader would care:** A federation can fail partially. Critical services should be able to remain live when the general coordination layer is dormant, just as a community's housing or medical continuity should not depend on preserving a failing legal entity.

**Likely destination:** Federation/shared infrastructure; legal pluralism / function-specific subsidiarity; movement living-will architecture.

**Evidence/status:** Partial gap (**B**) because the article already owns function-specific authority and continuity concepts; this adds the federation-service modularity application.

## G-025 — C — Communities need a federation-membership lifecycle, not permanent accreditation

**What the article currently says:** Individuals move through visitor/provisional/full membership, and the article discusses succession, fission, dissolution, and continuing relationships. It does not give federation-member **communities** a comparable lifecycle after initial accreditation.

**What the newer evidence adds:** FEC created a `Re-forming Community` status after several kinds of institutional discontinuity: one full member community underwent effectively complete turnover and was taken over by a new group; another was expected to shrink to a three-person family unit, raising concerns that later applicants would enter an inegalitarian power structure; another had drifted substantially away from the income-sharing requirement that originally justified full membership. FEC explicitly noted that major turnover can make re-forming more like starting a new community than simply returning to compliance.

A federation therefore needs **material-change requalification**, not only one-time admission and eventual expulsion. Community identity should be tracked across several distinct continuities:

- people/member composition;
- land/site;
- legal entity and asset control;
- governance and economic rules;
- mission/constitutional commitments;
- federation accreditation.

A site or corporation can persist while the community that earned federation trust has materially changed. Conversely, change should not automatically mean expulsion. Use statuses such as forming / in-dialogue / full / re-forming / dormant / dissolved, with rights, obligations, support, and review appropriate to each state.

Potential material-change triggers include major membership turnover, merger/fission, core governance/economic-rule change, land/control transfer, population falling below a safe minimum, or extended inactivity. The trigger should initiate review rather than presume wrongdoing.

**Why a reader would care:** Without requalification, federation legitimacy can become hereditary to a shell—land, name, corporation, or tiny residual group—even after the people and institutions that earned trust are gone. A scalable communal movement needs a way to preserve relationships and support experimentation without pretending institutional identity never changes.

**Likely destination:** Federation membership architecture; succession/fission; outcome/lineage measurement.

**Evidence/status:** Post-corpus empirical gap from FEC `Re-forming Community` policy. Classification **C** because the individual-membership and succession roots are present, but federation-level requalification is not.
