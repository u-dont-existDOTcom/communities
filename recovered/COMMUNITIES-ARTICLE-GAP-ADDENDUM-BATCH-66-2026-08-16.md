## G-026 correction / evidence update — interoperability can be a thin interface, not system harmonization

The June 2026 `FEC Exchange Program Policy` supplies a current operational counterexample to an overbroad reading of G-026.

The FEC did **not** make the labor systems of its member communities identical. For temporary Exchange Trips it instead standardizes a boundary protocol:

- sending-community and host-community consent;
- coordinator and host roles;
- FEC travel funding;
- host accommodation expectations;
- approximately three hours/day of host labor by travelers;
- full labor credit/compensation from the traveler's home community;
- no formal intercommunity labor-debt tracking;
- defined responsibility for travel emergencies and unexpected costs;
- problem reports that can affect future approval.

The policy explicitly says the simplified labor expectation helps avoid the logistical complications of trying to match different community labor systems.

**Correction to G-026:** `interoperability` means standardize the minimum interface required for usable movement. Do not harmonize underlying community systems unless a deeper form of portability actually requires it.

This does not create a general entitlement to admission or residence. Current Exchange Trips require consent, and full dual membership/residential migration remains a separate problem with housing, legal, health, tax, status, and accounting consequences.

Classification remains G-026 **B**.

## G-027 — B — Do not force asymmetric intercommunity support into a parity ledger

**What the article currently says:** The article/corpus already supports federation, mutual-aid agreements, intercommunity movement, and shared infrastructure. It does not distinguish the accounting semantics of different kinds of intercommunity help.

**What the FEC operating record adds:** Historical Twin Oaks LEX treated labor exchange as reciprocal: labor was expected to come back within the planning period, and credited outgoing LEX could be restricted when the destination community already owed Twin Oaks a large labor debt.

A 2010 FEC Assembly report then supplies a concrete asymmetric-stress case. East Wind had recently suffered several member deaths plus business/regulatory difficulties. Delegates discussed special LEX opportunities and subsequently created a special FEC budget for direct aid to East Wind rather than treating every supportive flow as ordinary bilateral labor debt.

The current FEC went further. Its Exchange Program Policy, approved January 2026 and amended June 2026, explicitly replaces old LEX/LETS because those programs were `somewhat confusing, under-developed, and based on a transactional system that falsely assumed parity between communities`.

The replacement system:

- is explicitly non-transactional;
- creates no formal labor debt or tracking;
- prioritizes skill-sharing, time-sensitive mutual aid, and intercommunity connection;
- also supports cultural cross-pollination, communard renewal, and exploration;
- gives travelers full home-community labor credit while using a simple host-labor expectation;
- avoids attempting to reconcile different internal labor systems;
- uses a federation travel fund and assigned host/sending-community responsibilities;
- tells delegates to seek balanced distribution while considering differences in community population.

**Residual:** A single bilateral parity ledger is the wrong accounting model for every kind of intercommunity transfer. Routine reciprocal exchange, emergency solidarity, training, exploration, and relationship-building have different purposes and should not silently create identical obligations.

### Operational requirement

Before recording an intercommunity transfer, classify it:

1. reciprocal exchange with expected settlement;
2. mutual aid / grant after asymmetric need;
3. shared-service contribution;
4. training / skill transfer;
5. exploratory, renewal, or relationship-building travel.

Then:

- use a debt/balance ledger only when repayment is genuinely part of the agreement;
- do not silently convert emergency aid or federation-funded exploration into bilateral debt;
- preassign travel, accommodation, home labor credit, host burden, emergency cost, and coordination responsibility;
- preserve local labor/economic systems when a small interface is sufficient;
- track distribution across member communities and normalize by relevant capacity/population so `non-transactional` does not become invisible permanent extraction from one node;
- if an aid/grant relationship later becomes reciprocal, make that a new explicit agreement rather than retroactively reclassifying past aid as debt.

**Why a reader would care:** If every supportive flow must balance bilaterally, a smaller or distressed community can become least eligible for additional help precisely because it has already needed more. Conversely, removing all accounting can hide chronic extraction. The FEC's redesign suggests the solution is not `no accounting`; it is **purpose-specific accounting instead of false parity**.

**Relationship to G-026:** G-026 asks whether people can move administratively between autonomous systems. G-027 asks what transfers between the communities mean economically and whether they should create reciprocal obligations at all.

**Likely destination:** federation/shared-infrastructure section; experimental-ecology architecture; intercommunity movement/mutual aid.

**Evidence/status:** Historical Twin Oaks LEX rules; 2010 FEC direct-aid response to East Wind stress; current January/June 2026 FEC Exchange Program redesign. Classification **B** because federation mutual aid already exists in the article/corpus, while the parity-ledger distinction is missing.

**Strict Creative-Tail status:** Not promoted. Adjacent mutual-credit/cooperative-finance literature already contains related countercyclical and non-bilateral accounting ideas, and mandatory Parallel Task is not executable in the current harness.
