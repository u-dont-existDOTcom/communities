## Main result

Volume 45 adds three source-bounded lessons: **health claims need subgroup and exposure outcomes rather than communal reputation; a pooled catastrophic-health fund can make its attachment, payout, reserve, and waiting rules explicit; and planned fission can divide people and assets before scale pressure becomes a crisis.**

1. **Communal medical knowledge did not prevent a harmful work-and-housing pattern.** Sargent reports that nineteenth-century Shaker tuberculosis mortality was significantly higher than non-Shaker mortality, that young indoor workers died at a higher rate than outdoor workers, and that sisters, who mostly worked indoors, died at a much higher rate than brothers, who mostly worked outdoors. He attributes the difference most likely to failure to use established isolation and rest practices: sick Shakers continued working and remained in ordinary, often overcrowded dwellings. This is a synthesis of John E. Murray's study, not a new causal reanalysis, and the same page notes conflicting ventilation evidence. The transferable point is procedural: evaluate health claims with subgroup outcomes, exposure and housing conditions, protected rest and isolation, independent medical access, and an external public-health route rather than treating community care or expertise as evidence of safety. (F-159)

2. **A cross-community health reserve made several risk-allocation rules legible.** In a footnote synthesizing reports by Kat Kinkade and Laird Schaub, Sargent describes PEACH, a catastrophic-health fund of the Fellowship of Egalitarian Communities. Communities initially contributed $10 per member per month, later $12.50 and then $15; the fund began helping after $5,000 per incident, paid 90 percent of a covered expense, never paid more than half of the fund, and imposed a two-year wait for a new member's full coverage. A cited 2009 account said that after 23 years the fund held more than $500,000 and had paid every submitted claim in full. Sargent could not determine its current status. The account supplies an explicit historic mechanism and claimed result, not audited current performance, claim definitions, rejected-claim data, or proof of legal insurance status. (F-160)

3. **Fission was planned as a multiyear continuity process rather than improvised after rupture.** Becker-Kilian reports that Hutterite colonies typically begin dividing at roughly 120 to 150 people. The daughter-colony process can take years; at completion, human and material assets are divided as equitably as possible, approximately half the members relocate, and mother and daughter become independent, self-sustaining financial entities. After the daughter opens with modern facilities, the older mother colony enters renovation intended to reduce disparities in living standards, although rising costs can impede that work. The article does not provide individual consent procedures, valuation formulas, dissent handling, debt allocation, comparative outcomes, or proof that the practice is universal. It nevertheless supplies a distinct fork design: a scale trigger, capital runway, planned member and asset division, financial independence, and a parity obligation to the originating community. (F-161)
