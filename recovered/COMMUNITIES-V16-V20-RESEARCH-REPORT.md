## Main result

This chunk sharpens the capture and correction problem. It also supplies several external remedies, but still no documented dangerous-child sequence.

1. **Oneida embedded adult authority in sexual access to girls and younger members.** The source says Noyes initiated girls, older men became their initial partners, and elders controlled younger members' education, work, social lives, and pairings. Some young women later refused sex and sought monogamy, but no independent contemporary check is described. The source does not supply each girl's age or a modern legal finding. (F-049)
2. **Exit becomes corrective only when a leaver can reach evidence, regulators, and valuation outside the group.** After Damanhur and an inner-circle leaver could not agree on compensation, litigation and disclosure exposed a secret, noncompliant construction project; a court expert later supplied an independent valuation. The author is sympathetic and the civil case was still pending. (F-050)
3. **Expulsion can be compounded by family-contact severance.** A source-limited review of former-Bruderhof-member memoirs describes wider cleansing, blocked spousal contact, an expelled daughter denied contact with her mother, and a second family rebuilding outside after prolonged rupture. The mechanism is material; the case details require balanced and primary corroboration. (F-051)
4. **Crisis can strengthen the layer associated with prior failure.** A kibbutz study says economic crisis increased dependence on technocrats despite leadership failure and rank-and-file unhappiness; those experts became the primary sponsors and carriers of change. This is a structural dependency finding, not proof of misconduct. (F-052)
5. **Founder allegations and factional assets may reach a boundary only outside the group.** A brief House of David review says Benjamin Purnell disappeared after being charged with statutory rape, was found hiding, and was tried; a court later divided factional property. The review states no verdict and cannot establish the charge. (F-053)
6. **A communal promise is not exit security unless the entitlement is portable and vested.** Most kibbutz members mistook pooled provident funds for pensions; crisis withdrawals preceded their dissolution. At Beit Oren, elderly members were reportedly advised to leave after being told the kibbutz could not provide for them, though the exact wording was disputed. Individual pensions remained valid across exit and then spread rapidly. (F-054)
7. **Post-exit narratives can disable institutional learning.** Shakers circulated tales of apostate ruin, exchanged legal intelligence, and tracked a prominent former member. The author says the narratives strengthened identity but caused the movement to ignore mixed praise and constructive criticism from leavers. The tales are evidence about social control, not proof of apostates' fates. (F-055)
8. **A participatory discipline can still deny reply and appeal.** Oneida's Mutual Criticism used many critics and sometimes rotating committees, but the subject had to remain passive, crying and outbursts were unacceptable, outsiders could be treated as contaminating, and some sessions were mandated. The favorable interpretation in the article does not remove those procedural facts. (F-056)
9. **Peer-spiritual care needs an independent medical boundary.** During a diphtheria outbreak, Oneida called a physician late, dismissed him after alleged disrespect toward Christ, and substituted criticism and ice under Noyes's authority. The reported post-treatment survival pattern is not clinical efficacy evidence. (F-057)
10. **Closed threat systems can metabolize disconfirmation.** At Royal Teton Ranch, an apocalyptic warning mobilized nearly one thousand arrivals and months of sixteen-hour workdays; after no attack, most members treated prayer as having averted it. The author describes a closed-information environment that quelled dissent, while former staff later attributed defections and budget problems partly to the episode. (F-058)
11. **Exclusion can stabilize a group and suppress needed correction at the same time.** Comparative and participant evidence links stricter selection and banishment with stability, while the authors explicitly warn that broad labels such as disruptive can remove innovation, dissent, or political opposition. Their stagnation thesis remains a question, not a measured outcome. (F-059)
12. **Longevity is not a safety metric.** A methods article notes that scholarship often treated survival as a proxy for a well-functioning community. In its 408-community exploratory study, centralization correlated positively with survival, while other structures produced context-dependent or reversed effects. None of those measures establish autonomy, exploitation, correction, or safety. (F-060)
13. **Late challenge may produce mass exit instead of internal correction.** In the Love Israel Family, two elders challenged the leader's domineering role; widening dissent became departure and litigation, at least two-thirds of members left, and property was jeopardized. The article does not reconstruct an independent internal adjudication. (F-061)
14. **Ordinary law and evidence standards are a better external boundary than categorical labels.** Grangeville heard serious claims and abundant hearsay, commissioned an inquiry after a tied vote, referred investigation to police, and required compliance with ordinary registration, licensing, building, and zoning rules. That process did not prove either safety or danger. (F-062)
15. **A truth-telling ritual fails when the founder is exempt.** A Synanon insider wrote that members were meant to break group contracts but no one could break a contract held by Dederich. The Berlin branch refused a 1978 alcohol-policy reversal and survived to the article's date. The memoir and editor connect concentrated power to collapse and violence, but those facts and the Berlin trajectory require independent verification. (F-063)

## Dangerous-child result

The separate child-as-dangerous-actor search covered every extracted text in the 88-PDF unit. It paired child, children, boy, girl, youth, juvenile, minor, and adolescent terms with violence, aggression, assault, attack, stabbing, killing, threats, harm, arson, delinquency, theft, weapons, unruliness, and related inflections, then inspected each proximity hit in context.

The apparent hits resolved into other categories:

- Twin Oaks material placed “children's issues” near petty crime without attributing the crime to children.
- One review described an adult founder's childhood abuse; the child was a victim.
- Student-cooperative evidence concerned threats against young residents, not dangerous students.
- A general overview mentioned child-abuse allegations against communities; children were alleged victims.
- Zoar material described ordinary discipline and rhetoric about idleness or malice, not persistent grave danger.
- Love Israel material recorded parents' fears and adult or external allegations, not a child perpetrator.
- Other matches were bibliographic, metaphorical, or OCR proximity artifacts.

No source documented the requested sequence: **a persistently dangerous child as actor → allegation → assessment → intervention → review → later outcome**. F-064 records a bounded null for volumes 16-20. Together with F-031 and F-048, it shows repeated absence in the processed journal chunks, not absence from history or the unprocessed literature.
