## Main result

This volume sharpens one cross-cutting result: **an override is only as real as its reach into the institution carrying the power—title, debt, public office, enforcement, residence, or legal standing.**

1. **A founder-removal vote can succeed while the asset override fails.** Zion's members deposed John Alexander Dowie and ninety-five percent voted to accept Wilbur Glenn Voliva. But Dowie had held the church and industry assets in his own name, so he could contest their transfer and the businesses entered receivership. Members later donated jewelry, mortgaged homes, and helped the church assume a million-dollar mortgage to reacquire the institutions; that debt remained a vulnerability when the businesses returned to receivership in 1933. A tested removal clause needs automatic control of title, accounts, debt, contracts, and litigation—not merely a new officeholder. The allegations surrounding Dowie's removal remain allegations in this source. (F-101)

2. **Private sanctions can migrate into captured public institutions.** Christian Catholic Church members voted as a bloc; Voliva selected electoral slates without holding office; and by 1914 Theocrats controlled the school board, city court, mayoralty, and nearly every local seat. Municipal ordinances then enforced church-derived restrictions. An observer reported little routine immediate coercion, yet described Voliva's advice as functioning like commands and anticipated expulsion for critical disloyalty. Independents obtained an injunction against the tobacco rule, but Theocratic leaders left it in the code where it still operated on members. Later records showed police fining outsider motorists for smoking while avoiding resident confrontations. Public status, elections, sincere belief, and incomplete enforcement did not make the system independent. (F-102)

3. **The most effective counter-power came from dissenters who could remain.** Some opponents left the church but not their homes, jobs, families, or city. They formed a parallel congregation and an Independent coalition. In 1934-1935 residents used a school-board election, petition, judicial election, municipal election, and church vote to defeat Theocratic control. Voliva retaliated after the school-board loss by closing the parochial school to flood the public system, then reversed. His loss did not erase inherited norms: blue laws and other restrictions persisted and an ordinance was later turned against his own film screenings. The transferable mechanism is not factional conflict; it is the separation of membership from residence and livelihood, plus parallel association and neutral civic routes that a leader cannot veto. (F-103)

4. **A community needs verified child-custody authority before admission or relocation.** A review of *The Great Divorce* says James Chapman removed his children with Shaker assistance after Eunice rejected Shakerism. The Shakers hid and moved the children while Eunice had almost no legal standing. Her five-year campaign restored civil standing through private legislation, organized public pressure, recovered one child amid a threatening crowd, and ended with the other two returned. Mother Lucy Wright later directed that children not be admitted unless both parents joined. This is not a model for crowd confrontation or a universal modern both-parent rule. It is evidence for custody verification, preserved contact and location records, independent child representation, and an external route before a community becomes a party to a custody conflict. (F-104)

## Source-role and inference limits

- F-101 is high-confidence evidence of a mismatch between removal authority and asset title, not proof of the sexual rumors or a single-cause account of either receivership.
- F-102 does not classify every Zion ordinance as unlawful or every voter as coerced. It identifies overlapping institutional control, leader-selected slates, strategic nonlitigation, and differential enforcement.
- F-103 does not attribute Voliva's fall only to elections or dissenters. Debt, depression, generational change, attendance decline, rival leaders, and elite defections also mattered.
- F-104 is a short review of a book based on historical sources. It does not resolve the indentures, legal custody, children's independent wishes, or leaders' reasons for return and rule change.
- The Ave Maria coda is a 2011 comparison. It was not promoted as evidence of current governance, policy, finances, leadership, or resident opinion.
- No historical actor is diagnosed with ASPD or psychopathy. Conduct, structure, and response mechanisms remain separate from clinical diagnosis.

## Context retained without new findings

- Amana's environmental sustainability coexisted with social and economic strain. Leaders found expulsion disproportionate as an individual cost mechanism and relied on admonition; low-voice hired workers filled undesirable labor; outmigration and closed admissions drove demographic decline; and a large-majority 1932 redesign introduced stock, wages, an outside manager, and institutional separation. These details materially deepen existing findings but do not create a new principle.
- Israeli city communes show one way to scale through small units, federations, shared learning, financial cooperation, and service organizations rather than simply enlarging one residential body. The article is a 2009 scene map and does not test anti-capture governance or later outcomes.
- Zion's Ave Maria comparison raises a useful verification lead about permit-granting authority, developer-run special districts, church hierarchy, board removal, and resident opposition. Because the account ends in 2011, it cannot support a present-tense claim without fresh authoritative research.
