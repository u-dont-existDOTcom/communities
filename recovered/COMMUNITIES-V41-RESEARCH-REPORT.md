## Main result

Volume 41 adds one narrow lesson: **a complaint forum and an outside evaluator do not provide independent evidence when the person being challenged controls the forum and members cannot answer safely or candidly.**

1. **A mandatory conflict forum can become a founder's discipline channel and contaminate an outside evaluation.** Coulthard reports that Kerista required every member to participate in Gestalt-o-Rama, a forum intended to air complaints, resolve conflict, and support personal growth. She says that founder Jud Presmont and more confrontational members instead used it to bully others into submission. She also reports that Jud targeted attractive younger men in the sessions while expressing sexual insecurity, and that women described related accusations in private relationships. A psychologist later interviewed members for a book presenting Kerista as evidence that people could move beyond jealousy; former members subsequently said they had lied to the psychologist about their feelings. The article does not supply session transcripts, the psychologist's interview protocol or notes, a participant denominator, or comparable accounts from every member. It cannot establish that every session was captured, that every interviewee lied, or that the psychologist knowingly presented a managed account. It does establish a paired design risk: a nominal complaint process can manufacture outward conformity, and an evaluator can inherit that conformity unless participant selection, private access, confidentiality, anti-retaliation, discrepant accounts, and later correction are independently protected. (F-147)
