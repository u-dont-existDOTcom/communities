## Source-level findings

### F-183 — A rapid leaving fund can coexist with a nonvested or discounted later benefit

The Mothership text allows voluntary withdrawal for any reason or no reason on 30 days' written notice. It requires a Leaving Fund for each member, payment within two business days after withdrawal or expulsion, and enough cash to cover every member's Leaving Fund. It also provides member access to books and internally prepared financial statements and a negotiation-to-mediation-to-arbitration dispute path.

The instrument keeps a later Retirement Fund separate. That fund is expressly nonvested, need not be held in cash, and may be paid over ten years or as a lump sum worth half its stated value. This proves a design point rather than an outcome: a prompt ring-fenced payment can still leave the larger source of transition security delayed, contingent, or discounted.

The source page labels the document “DRAFT 03/20/2016,” contains sample names and blank signatures, and supplies no executed agreement, reserve statement, withdrawal, payout, dispute result, relocation record, family-contact record, or later wellbeing outcome. Draft is not adoption. The amounts also cannot be assumed sufficient for a household, and a 30-day notice term cannot override immediate lawful safety action.

### F-184 — Institutional equity in a pooled fund is not an individual claim right

The current FEC page says PEACH has governance separate from ordinary FEC delegates: one representative from every participating community and a technical administrator. In the 2013 administrator account, communities rather than individuals participate. The home community decides, pays the medical expense first, and submits the claim. The account describes 90 percent reimbursement above a $5,000 community threshold, a formula-based annual cap, and a withdrawing community's receipt of 90 percent of contributions net of reimbursements.

The same account expressly says the agreement gave no direct right to an individual. It reported roughly one claim a year among about 200 people in eight communities and said all but one filed claim had been paid. That assertion is historical and has no public claim ledger or audit in the inspected material. A high payment rate among claims that reached PEACH says nothing about needs a home community declined or could not afford to submit.

The current official page corroborates the organizational governance layer, not the 2013 balance, payment history, coverage terms, or legal status. No public current rules, reserve audit, denial record, individual notice, direct independent intake, hardship bridge, medical appeal, portability record, or later patient outcome was located. “Self-insurance” is the source's terminology, not an independent legal conclusion.

### F-185 — Aggregate parity, judicial allocation, and individual choice are separate

In *Walter Estate v. Walter*, the Alberta Court of Appeal affirmed allocation after the Felger Colony partnership had dissolved. Nine partners were allocated shares according to length of service, and one estate's share was reduced for funds found to have been diverted to a breakaway colony. This is a post-breakdown legal allocation, not a planned-fission agreement.

Hutterites.org separately describes a customary planned response when a colony reaches about 30 families or 150 people: a daughter site is prepared and aggregate assets are valued and divided. People may volunteer, or be divided into groups and assigned by lot. In its Lehrerleut description, all families pack before the lot and the selected group can move the same day. The public account itself recognizes that the process can be painful.

Together, the records show why an apparently fair division between institutions cannot be used as evidence of individual consent. A court may settle beneficiary interests after collapse; a planned split may create two capitalized successors; neither establishes a household refusal right, child or spouse voice, neutral valuation and debt review, protected family contact, or comparative later viability. The underlying 1972 trust agreement, trial reasons, signed planned-division schedule, dissent records, and both-successor accounts were not obtained.

### F-186 — Court-supervised finality is not a standing fair-exit mechanism

The four-page Schedule 1A Notice records a High Court order dated July 25, 2025. For specified claims outside the Trust Deed and listed-beneficiary position, a claimant had to give identity, factual and legal basis, evidence, and amount or calculation by October 16, 2025. Trustees would classify timely claims as rejected or pending; the notice says covered claims not preserved on time could be ignored in distribution and claimants would fund their own legal advice or litigation.

The current closure records separate initial transition distributions, redress and claims, historic requests, and residual apportionment. Some later eligibility depends on institutional records, deadlines, and votes of Listed Beneficiaries. Trustees reported the court claims procedure complete on January 23, 2026. A March 2026 deed amendment opened two additional Phase 3 categories, whose registration window closed April 30, 2026.

This is a useful record of asset preservation and procedural finality after institutional breakdown. It is not a pre-crisis member exit right. The full Order and complete Trust Deed were not public in the inspected pages, final distributions and closure remain incomplete, and the record supplies no complete housing, pension, family-contact, survivor, child, or later-wellbeing outcomes. Redress, civil claims, historic requests, and residual beneficiary status must remain separate.

## Is exit usable before crisis?

Only E-001 presents an ordinary withdrawal mechanism before collapse, and it is an unsigned draft. Its two-business-day Leaving Fund is the clearest rapid-liquidity control in the unit, but no record shows that the amount enabled relocation or supported a household. The later nonvested benefit and discounted lump-sum option show why “some money quickly” cannot stand for usable exit.

E-002 protects a participating community's pooled equity, not a member's independent access to care. E-003 describes a planned institutional fork but can allocate families by lot. E-004 begins after institutional failure and puts some rights behind records, deadlines, classification, advice costs, and votes. None establishes a complete pre-crisis outward door.

A usable exit therefore needs, before conflict: a direct right to leave; an emergency safety exception; sufficient household liquidity; separately stated and vested benefits; portable identity, education, health, and financial records; housing and care continuity; protected family contact; and affordable independent review.

## Neutrality test

None of the four records places every contested function under a neutral controller.

- E-001 exposes books to members and names mediation and arbitration, but does not specify independent valuation, escrow, former-member records, or cost support that the departing person controls.
- E-002 separates PEACH governance from FEC delegates, yet the home community remains the individual claimant's first gatekeeper and payer.
- E-003 supplies a court for dissolution allocation but no documented neutral choice, valuation, debt, or appeal route for a planned household assignment.
- E-004 supplies court supervision for defined claims, while record access, claim preparation, legal cost, trustee classification, deadlines, and later beneficiary votes can still determine practical access.

The transferable control is functional separation: no person or body challenged in the dispute should control the records, valuation, escrow, necessities, family contact, claim intake, or appeal. External arbitration or a court answers only the dispute placed before it; neither supplies missing housing, medical, family, or safeguarding functions.

## Planned-fission choice and viability

A planned fork needs two ledgers, not one.

The institutional ledger records land and equipment valuation, cash, liabilities, mortgages, working capital, guarantees, transfer dates, and both successors' runway. The individual ledger records opt-in or refusal, household and child voice, identity and benefit portability, schooling and care continuity, family contact, housing, work obligations, independent advice, and appeal.

The Hutterite public account addresses scale, a prepared site, aggregate valuation, and the creation of two operating colonies. It does not establish the individual ledger when households are assigned by lot. *Walter* addresses a judicial allocation after dissolution and therefore cannot fill the planned-consent gap. Both-successor viability must be reported after subsidy, debt, shared-service, and transition arrangements change; legal existence alone is not viability.

## Later-outcome rule

For each instrument or fork, report separately:

1. instrument status: draft, executed, amended, funded, and currently operative;
2. eligible people and whether the right belongs to the individual or an institution;
3. reserve balance, valuation method, debt, escrow, record access, and actual payment timing;
4. claims or exits initiated, rejected, delayed, appealed, withdrawn, and completed;
5. safety exceptions, housing, care, school, family contact, and dependent continuity;
6. household choice and both successors' financial and operational position;
7. fixed later measures of material security, wellbeing, relationships, recurrence, and institutional performance; and
8. missing, unreachable, or excluded people and records.

A draft clause is not adoption. A historical payment assertion is not a current audit. Aggregate asset parity is not consent. A judgment is not a preexisting fork protocol. A claims cutoff is not a fair exit, and legal survival is not a later human outcome.

## Transfer boundary

Potentially transferable design features are narrow:

- write an individual exit right and emergency exception before crisis;
- reserve enough liquid value for every eligible member and test household sufficiency;
- separate immediate funds from vested later benefits and publish every haircut or condition;
- preserve records for former members and place valuation, debt schedules, escrow, and review outside the challenged authority chain;
- give an individual a direct route around an institutional claim gate for urgent care and independent appeal;
- preserve refusal, household and child voice, necessities, care, schooling, and family contact during any fork; and
- audit actual exits, claims, distributions, both successors, and later human outcomes.

This evidence does not authorize forced relocation, lot assignment as a general consent rule, family division, shunning, banishment, withholding necessities, compelled treatment, community adjudication of medical need, private extinguishment of legal claims, retrospective diagnosis, or transfer of court, insurance-regulatory, medical, custody, or safeguarding power to a private community.
