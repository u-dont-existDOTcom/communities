## Main result

Volume 44 adds three source-bounded lessons: **a child-placement program needs a child-centered stop rule when its stated succession purpose repeatedly fails; therapy-controlled family separation needs a route to independent court and professional review; and an outside report can still fail when the intake standard excludes credible secondhand disclosure.**

1. **Child placement cannot be justified by demographic need alone.** Haagen reports that, after adult recruitment declined, Shaker leaders accepted hundreds of unaccompanied and indentured children in hopes that they would become adult Believers. The children also contributed substantially to agricultural and domestic labor. Some stayed, but the article says the vast majority ran away, were dismissed, left at adulthood, or were removed—sometimes against their will—by family. Haagen concludes that the strategy did not maintain membership and continued despite shortcomings obvious to many. The source does not provide a cohort denominator, age and custody records, child accounts, placement-by-placement welfare outcomes, or a counterfactual. The transferable point is therefore procedural: child admission needs lawful authority, a child-centered purpose, independent representation, capacity and education standards, protected family contact, cohort-level retention and wellbeing measures, and a recorded stop or redesign rule that does not treat labor or institutional succession as sufficient benefit. (F-155)

2. **Independent legal and professional routes can expose a therapy-controlled family narrative.** Brown's review of Stille reports that Sullivanian therapists directed family severance, separated mothers from babies, sent children to boarding schools, and portrayed resisting parents as dangerous. In the review's account of Marice Pappo, group members removed her baby's belongings, denied access, and circulated substance-use claims. With legal advice and outside help, Pappo took her daughter out of state. When the father sued for custody, testimony reportedly exposed false claims about Pappo's alcohol and drug use; he dropped the suit when the court appeared likely to favor her, and the parents reached a private partial-custody agreement. Publicity also preceded the loss of psychotherapy licenses by three senior therapists for boundary and sexual misconduct described by the review. This is review-level evidence, not an adjudication of every allegation or proof that publicity caused the licensing outcomes. It nevertheless supplies a distinct design contrast: care authority, custody narratives, evidence, and professional discipline must not remain inside the same therapist-leader chain. (F-156)

3. **A reporting route can exist while its evidence threshold still blocks action.** Metcalf directly recounts visiting the Cooperite precursor of Gloriavale in 1982, hearing women and girls report routine sexual and physical abuse, being expelled by Neville Cooper, and reporting the disclosures to authorities. He says nothing happened because he had not personally observed the abuse. The review then summarizes later conviction of Cooper, other convictions and charges, ongoing investigation, survivor organizing, and fear-related evidence withholding. These later details are the reviewer's account of Pratt's book and should not be treated as a current official case register without verification. The source establishes a reporting bottleneck, not that authorities acted unlawfully or that every later case corroborated the 1982 disclosures. A usable outside route needs documented intake of disclosed harm, lawful referral and preservation rules, protection from retaliation, trauma-informed survivor support distinct from evidence testing, and a way to record why a report is screened out and whether later information triggers review. (F-157)

## Source and interpretation cautions

- F-155 relies on Haagen's historical synthesis and cited Shaker sources. Inspect child-admission, indenture, census, family, labor, school, dismissal, custody, and departure records before estimating cohort size, retention, welfare, involuntariness, or causal effects.
- F-156 is a review of Alexander Stille's book. Inspect the book, interview method, custody pleadings and testimony, licensing files and dispositions, school records, participant accounts across positions, and contemporaneous press before naming individuals' conduct as adjudicated or claiming that publicity caused professional discipline.
- F-157 combines Metcalf's direct account of his 1982 visit and report with his review-level summary of Pratt's memoir and later legal developments. Verify agency intake, conviction and charging records, investigative status, survivor accounts, and Gloriavale's response before treating the later counts or procedural status as current or complete.
- Book reviews support their reviewers' direct reports, summaries, and evaluations until the reviewed books and underlying records are inspected. Reviews do not inherit the evidentiary scope of books they summarize.
- None of the sources supports a retrospective diagnosis of antisocial personality disorder or psychopathy. The reviewer's diagnostic labels in M-0093 are not repeated as findings.
