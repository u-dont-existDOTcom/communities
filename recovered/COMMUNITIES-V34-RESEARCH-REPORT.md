## Main result

Volume 34 adds two narrow design lessons: **a child-protection route must be able to override a leader's control of basic necessities, and a founder-capture audit must test whether family, property, and spiritual authority can be bundled while outside peer checks are severed.**

1. **Basic necessities need an operative outside override.** Goodwillie's Pilgrim history reports that the New Madrid County sheriff brought provisions to the island and stood guard while starving children ate against Isaac Bullard's orders. A later travel account, relayed by Goodwillie, said two other children had been taken from the remnant group because of their condition. The article does not identify the legal authority, assessment, duration, decision maker, children, or later outcomes. Nor is an armed historical intervention a modern template. The transferable point is narrower: food, urgent health care, confidential child reporting, and independent protective review must be non-waivable and usable even when a leader or authority chain objects. (F-116)

2. **Peer checks fail when a leader can reject the peer system and bundle several kinds of authority.** Neal's review of Donald Kraybill's *Renegade Amish* reports that Bishop Sam Mullet increasingly separated Bergholz from other Amish communities, excommunicated challengers, rejected other Amish leaders' authority, owned most community land, and led a group composed largely of his family. The review says this isolation and concentration prevented the usual checks on a bishop from operating. Internal confinement, paddling, and hair-cutting practices preceded five attacks on disapproving relatives and critics; sixteen defendants were convicted as of the review's publication. This does not make heterodoxy, kinship, isolation, or charismatic leadership danger proxies. It supports testing whether property, family, discipline, religious office, complaint channels, and reciprocal peer review can all be controlled or rejected by one person. The underlying book, federal docket, appeals, victims' accounts, and post-2014 outcomes require verification. (F-117)

## Source and interpretation cautions

- F-116 depends on Goodwillie's synthesis of Timothy Flint and Thomas Nuttall. The sheriff episode and child-removal report need the underlying texts and any surviving county or family record before they are used as legal or child-protection precedent.
- F-117 comes from a four-page review of a book whose author used legal records, interviews, and his own work as a prosecution expert. The book, trial record, victims' accounts, later appeals, and post-2014 outcomes are needed before naming final legal results or causal claims.
- The Ananda reflection is written by a community member, relies heavily on the founder, current leaders, community counsel, and community publications, and interprets litigation through the community's spiritual frame. Its reported civil verdict, admissions, departures, governance changes, and later outcomes remain a verification lead.
- The Las Gaviotas article intentionally presents the charismatic founder's public account. Consensus, finances, retention, education, security encounters, and environmental results require independent records and other resident perspectives before they are treated as governance outcomes.
- The Shaker print-history article demonstrates why allegation, counter-allegation, affidavit, suppression, and later recollection must be kept separate. It does not adjudicate the abuse claims it quotes.
- Broad book reviews can identify leads and contrasts, but their compression generally cannot establish a complete allegation, assessment, intervention, review, and later outcome.
