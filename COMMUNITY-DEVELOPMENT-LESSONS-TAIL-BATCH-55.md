# Main lesson — Share structured safety evidence, not portable moral labels

**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + FEDERATION SAFEGUARDING

A federation faces two opposite risks:
- keeping communities siloed so a genuinely dangerous pattern can follow a person across communities;
- turning one local allegation/conflict into a movement-wide informal blacklist.

Existing safeguarding systems already distinguish substantiated, unsubstantiated, unfounded, malicious and false allegations. A communal federation should use the same general discipline rather than portable labels such as `unsafe`, `psychopath`, `toxic`, or `bad fit`.

---

## B55-L01 — Portable records need explicit evidentiary states

Use controlled states such as:
- allegation pending;
- substantiated risk/finding;
- unsubstantiated / insufficient evidence;
- unfounded;
- false;
- malicious;
- resolved/expired restriction;
- corrected/overturned finding.

## B55-L02 — Separate facts, reports, interpretations and decisions

A shared packet should distinguish:
- source report;
- directly observed facts;
- corroboration;
- alternative account;
- finding/risk assessment;
- current restriction;
- appeal/correction state.

## B55-L03 — The observation regime must travel with the evidence

Connect to C021.

If extra incidents were found only after intensified monitoring, the receiving community should know:
- when monitoring changed;
- why;
- what evidence existed before the scrutiny;
- what ordinary observation baseline looked like.

## B55-L04 — Scope warnings to relevant roles

A substantiated concern may justify restricting:
- childcare;
- finances;
- private records;
- driving;
- medical/care authority

without establishing global unsuitability for every community role.

Prefer bounded role-specific controls where possible.

## B55-L05 — Receiving communities ordinarily make their own decision

Portable evidence informs local admission/role decisions.

Automatic federation-wide bars should exist only where the federation has explicitly established a binding safeguard for a defined risk/role.

## B55-L06 — Corrections and reversals must propagate

If a source finding is:
- corrected;
- overturned;
- reclassified;
- expired,

identify downstream recipients and push the update.

Do not allow stale copied warnings to outlive their source record.

## B55-L07 — Version and log portable records

Include:
- source;
- date/version;
- current status;
- review/expiry;
- correction history;
- who received it and why.

## B55-L08 — Preserve subject reply/correction rights where compatible with safety

Subject to victim/safeguarding confidentiality, people should generally know what portable finding/restriction exists and have a route to correct factual errors or appeal decisions.

## B55-L09 — Do not transport ordinary incompatibility as safeguarding data

Separate:
- political disagreement;
- relationship conflict;
- personality mismatch;
- ordinary poor fit

from evidence of portable safety/rights risk.

## B55-L10 — Count evidence lineages, not copied allegations

Repeated warnings copied from one originating conflict are not independent evidence.

Track provenance.

## B55-L11 — Strong direct safety evidence still needs rapid protective action

Due process should not become paralysis. A portable system exists partly because serious predatory behavior can exploit organizational boundaries.

Use proportionate interim protection while evidence/review proceeds.

## B55-L12 — Restrict access to people who need the record

Portable safeguarding information must not become searchable federation gossip.

## B55-L13 — Audit whether the system becomes a factional exclusion tool

Monitor disparate use and patterns involving:
- dissenters;
- unpopular members;
- demographic groups;
- local factional conflicts.

## B55-L14 — Private transfer infrastructure must not erase substantiated safeguards

Connect to Batch 46.

A member may explore transfers privately, but formal placement can still apply relevant verified role restrictions.

## B55-L15 — `Unsubstantiated` is not the same as `false`

Insufficient evidence does not prove guilt or innocence.

Do not present it as a proven fact, while separately assessing whether any narrowly justified temporary risk control remains necessary.
