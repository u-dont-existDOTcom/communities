# Main lesson — The cost of being screened is part of the admission filter

**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + MEMBERSHIP DESIGN

A visit/probation process does not merely reveal fit. It also determines who can afford to remain in the applicant pool.

Long visits, unpaid time, repeated travel, childcare, uncertain housing, waiting-list delays and invasive process can filter applicants by opportunity cost before communal fit is ever evaluated.

Twin Oaks already mitigates some of this by allowing split visits when three consecutive weeks are economically unworkable and by reducing visitor fees when needed. The broader lesson is to measure and design this explicitly.

---

## B52-L01 — Audit the whole applicant funnel

Track:
- inquiry;
- application;
- scheduled visit;
- visit completion;
- interview;
- acceptance;
- actual move-in;
- provisional completion;
- full membership.

## B52-L02 — Record why applicants withdraw

Where consensual, distinguish:
- changed interest;
- work/time;
- travel/cost;
- childcare/caregiving;
- health/accessibility;
- housing/visa;
- household constraint;
- process burden/invasiveness;
- community fit.

Do not code every withdrawal as failed fit.

## B52-L03 — Measure information gained per burden imposed

For each stage ask:
- what new predictive/reciprocal-fit information does this stage produce?;
- applicant time/cost/lost income;
- care burden;
- community staff time.

Remove or redesign expensive stages with little incremental information.

## B52-L04 — Preserve necessary observation while offering equivalent routes

Possible accommodations:
- split visit periods;
- sliding/waived fees;
- transport support;
- care support;
- accessible housing/tasks;
- remote orientation before travel;
- federation-coordinated visits.

Equivalent need not mean literally identical.

## B52-L05 — Do not use hardship tolerance as an accidental commitment test

Ability to abandon work or caregiving for weeks is not the same as communal commitment or fit.

If sacrifice is intentionally valued, make that norm explicit rather than embedding it invisibly in logistics.

## B52-L06 — Study people who never reach formal application

Ask interested non-applicants, where feasible, what blocked application.

The strongest selection effect can occur before the community has an applicant record.

## B52-L07 — Waiting lists create another selection pressure

Record:
- waiting duration;
- offers declined due timing;
- people lost from list;
- repeated re-visit burden;
- applicants who cannot keep plans flexible.

## B52-L08 — Household applicants face multiplied burden

Connect to Batch 45:
- multiple jobs/leave schedules;
- children/care;
- travel/housing cost;
- linked placement uncertainty.

## B52-L09 — Accommodations should not erase genuine safety/fit requirements

Reduce logistical inequity while preserving necessary:
- safeguarding;
- role competence;
- reciprocal informed consent;
- realistic communal-life experience.

## B52-L10 — Compare outcomes across accommodation routes

Test whether split visits/fee support/etc. materially alter:
- admission decisions;
- later retention;
- community outcomes;
- screening accuracy.

Do not assume accommodation weakens screening quality.

## B52-L11 — Federation pooling can reduce redundant applicant burden

Where baseline requirements overlap, consider portable:
- orientation;
- applicant-controlled records;
- verified basic checks;
- coordinated visit schedules.

Local fit decisions remain local.

## B52-L12 — Membership demographics can be pipeline-created

If the admission process selects for high schedule flexibility/low caregiving burden, the community may later misread the resulting demographic profile as evidence about who naturally `fits` communal life.

Research rejected/withdrawn/nonapplicant populations where feasible.
