# Main lesson — Best-practice discovery needs a discovery/confirmation boundary

**Status:** FOUNDATIONAL RESEARCH-CONTROL / KNOWN

When a federation research center searches many communities, practices, outcomes, subgroups and models, the most impressive discovered association is selected partly because its noise happened to be favorable.

This is the winner's curse. The raw effect of the selected winner is systematically too optimistic on average.

---

## B51-L01 — Separate discovery from confirmation

Use new data where feasible to estimate the effect after selection:
- later community/time cohorts;
- new daughter communities;
- community/lineage-level holdouts;
- staggered voluntary adoption;
- external movement replication.

Do not advertise the discovery estimate as though the practice had been preselected independently of the data.

## B51-L02 — Publish the search universe

For each highlighted `best practice`, record how many:
- candidate practices;
- outcomes;
- subgroups;
- specifications;
- time windows

were inspected before selection.

## B51-L03 — Distinguish confirmatory from exploratory outcomes

Exploration is legitimate. Label it honestly.

Do not retroactively present the outcome that happened to look strongest as the original primary outcome.

## B51-L04 — Use selection-aware uncertainty

Depending on design use appropriate:
- multiplicity control;
- shrinkage/hierarchical models;
- selected-best-policy inference;
- held-out/cross-fitted evaluation;
- replication.

No one ritual correction works for every communal dataset.

## B51-L05 — Replicate magnitude, not merely sign

If discovery says `+40%` and confirmation says `+5%`, saying `both were positive` hides material winner's-curse shrinkage.

Publish discovery and confirmation estimates separately.

## B51-L06 — Keep failed/null searches in the experiment ledger

The other candidate practices are part of the evidence context of the selected winner.

Do not let them disappear from institutional memory.

## B51-L07 — Avoid selecting communities and evaluating their unique practices on the same outcome noise

Bad loop:
1. identify highest-outcome communities;
2. find what they uniquely do;
3. call those features best practices;
4. validate with those same communities.

Use independent comparison/confirmation.

## B51-L08 — Regression toward the mean is not automatically implementation failure

When adopters fail to reproduce the original spectacular effect, first account for expected selection optimism before blaming them.

## B51-L09 — Annual rankings can repeatedly recreate the winner's curse

Choosing the current top practice/community each year from noisy results repeatedly selects positive noise.

Use appropriate longer horizons/shrinkage/predeclared rules or selection-aware sequential methods.

## B51-L10 — Distinguish `best observed` from `best expected`

Raw rankings answer a sample-specific question. Decision support should include uncertainty and selection effects.

## B51-L11 — Confirmation does not solve transportability

A replicated local effect can still fail when scaled or moved to communities with different context/values.

Combine with Batch 32 scaling controls and Batch 39 value pluralism.

## B51-L12 — Small-N communal research is especially vulnerable

Few communities per practice means noisy estimates and unstable selected maxima.

Use modest claims and independent confirmation rather than precision theater.

---

# Suggested finding lifecycle

1. exploratory signal;
2. selection-aware estimate;
3. independent confirmation;
4. transportability/value/scaling analysis;
5. provisional recommendation;
6. continuing outcome monitoring.

Do not jump from step 1 directly to `best practice`.
