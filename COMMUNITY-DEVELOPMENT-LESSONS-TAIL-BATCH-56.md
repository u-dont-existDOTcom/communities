# Main lesson — Give communities a resolution plan, not only a dissolution clause

**Status:** TAIL-PROVISIONAL + CRISIS/EXIT ARCHITECTURE  
**Creative Tail:** C025 — Community resolution / living-will architecture

A community can fail as an institution while members still urgently need the functions it supplied. Crisis planning should distinguish:

1. **recovery** — restore the existing community to viability;
2. **resolution** — preserve member-critical functions while the organization is restructured, transferred, or wound down if recovery is no longer desirable or feasible.

The key rule is:

> **Design failure so the people and critical functions do not have to fail with the institution.**

---

## B56-L01 — Maintain both recovery and resolution plans

Recovery can include temporary liquidity, staffing help, mediation, management support, debt restructuring, recruitment, or federation services.

Resolution asks a different question: if recovery fails, how do housing, food, medication, childcare, records, money, transport, and exit continue?

## B56-L02 — Define member-critical functions before crisis

Possible functions:
- shelter/housing;
- food/water;
- health/medication/care;
- childcare/education/safeguarding;
- cash/benefits/savings/payroll;
- transport;
- identity/legal/medical/education records;
- communication/family contact;
- safe relocation/exit;
- animal/land/infrastructure stewardship.

## B56-L03 — Give each function a continuity horizon

Some functions tolerate no interruption; others can be resolved over weeks or months.

Examples:
- food/water/medication: immediate continuity;
- emergency housing: immediate bridge;
- schooling: rapid continuity with transition;
- asset payout: longer timeline may be acceptable if necessities are protected.

## B56-L04 — Map provider and fallback provider

For each critical function record:
- current provider/system;
- key dependency;
- local fallback;
- federation bridge option;
- external provider where appropriate;
- activation authority.

A resolution plan that depends entirely on the failing system continuing to function is not a resolution plan.

## B56-L05 — Make records and benefits portable where feasible

Institutional collapse should not erase:
- personal records;
- vested benefits/savings;
- health/education records;
- work history/qualifications;
- federation rights where applicable.

## B56-L06 — Maintain limited bridge capacity

Possible federation supports:
- temporary housing;
- food;
- transport;
- childcare;
- document recovery;
- work placement;
- C013/C045/C046 transfer support.

The bridge protects people during transition; it need not recreate the failed community permanently.

## B56-L07 — Separate member rescue from institutional bailout

A federation can fund household transition, emergency necessities, portable benefits, and orderly asset/legal resolution without giving unrestricted rescue money to incumbent leadership.

This matters especially when mismanagement or capture contributed to failure.

## B56-L08 — Predefine recovery-to-resolution triggers

Possible dimensions:
- insolvency/runway;
- inability to supply necessities;
- governance incapacity;
- loss of legally required competence;
- severe population loss;
- failed recovery milestones;
- member vote;
- safeguarding/rights conditions.

No universal threshold is proposed.

## B56-L09 — Incumbent leadership should not be sole judge of institutional survival

Founders/leaders naturally have preservation incentives. Where the issue is whether leadership/control or the institution itself should continue, provide an independent review/trigger route while respecting legal/property/member rights.

## B56-L10 — Map functions independently of the legal entity

`Childcare`, `housing transition`, or `medication access` are functions. They may be continued by a sister community, federation bridge, selected households, or lawful external provider even if the old community entity ends.

## B56-L11 — Protect family/care continuity

Resolution should not scatter households or children according to asset convenience.

Map:
- households;
- coparents;
- dependents/caregivers;
- child/family contact;
- essential support ties.

## B56-L12 — Preserve evidence/records before liquidation or leadership change

If failure includes conflict or misconduct, records can disappear during wind-down.

Use preservation triggers, independent copies/custody, access/correction rights, and privacy controls.

## B56-L13 — Include animal, land, and ecological obligations

Agricultural/ecological failure can leave animals, crops, water systems, hazardous materials, or stewardship duties that need continuity.

## B56-L14 — Resolution can transfer viable functions into a successor

Possible structures:
- successor community with new governance;
- merger into sister community;
- bridge entity;
- household/project subdivision;
- federation takeover of one function only.

Institutional death and total functional loss are not the same thing.

## B56-L15 — Asset liquidation should not crowd out immediate function continuity

Legal creditor/member rights still govern assets. Internally, the resolution plan should separately show how urgent necessities continue while liquidation/settlement proceeds.

## B56-L16 — Tabletop-test the resolution plan

Ask:

> If this community could not operate next Monday, where do medication, food, children, records, cash access, animals, and housing go?

Test key-person loss, inaccessible accounts, absent leaders, and degraded records rather than assuming an orderly failure.
