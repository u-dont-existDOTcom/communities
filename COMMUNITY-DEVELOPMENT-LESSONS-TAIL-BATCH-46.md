# Main lesson — Give members a private federation-level transfer-exploration channel

**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + EXIT/RESEARCH INFRASTRUCTURE

A member may be unwilling to tell a survey `I am unhappy here` yet still privately explore whether another community fits better. A federation can support both autonomy and research by allowing nonbinding transfer exploration without requiring immediate disclosure to local leadership.

This is not a pure welfare measure. Transfer interest depends on available alternatives, family constraints, mobility costs, curiosity and search assistance.

---

## B46-L01 — Keep exploration private until voluntary disclosure

Local leadership should not automatically learn who:
- browsed openings;
- requested a conversation;
- joined a transfer-interest list.

## B46-L02 — Separate exploration from commitment

Possible stages:
1. private browsing;
2. anonymous/pseudonymous fit inquiry;
3. confidential conversation;
4. voluntary identity disclosure;
5. visit/trial;
6. formal transfer.

## B46-L03 — Do not treat transfer interest as a direct dissatisfaction score

Interest can reflect:
- values;
- location/climate;
- family;
- work/skills;
- romance;
- conflict;
- housing;
- curiosity;
- serious dissatisfaction.

Use it as one behavioral evidence channel.

## B46-L04 — Compare private transfer interest with survey outcomes

Possible patterns:
- high satisfaction + low interest;
- high satisfaction + high interest — possible reporting pressure or attractive alternatives;
- low satisfaction + low interest — possible poor alternatives/exit barriers;
- low satisfaction + high interest.

Interpret only with opportunity-set and mobility-cost data.

## B46-L05 — Record search exposure

A person cannot reveal preference for an alternative they never knew existed.

Track:
- openings shown;
- communities considered;
- search assistance;
- geographic constraints;
- household constraints.

## B46-L06 — Lower legitimate transfer barriers

Possible supports:
- visit/transport grants;
- federation transition housing;
- portable records/benefits;
- neutral exit-valuation help;
- childcare/logistics;
- C013/C045 matching support.

## B46-L07 — Protect against retaliation for good-faith exploration

The channel fails if discovery causes:
- housing/work retaliation;
- benefit loss;
- governance exclusion;
- social punishment.

## B46-L08 — Prevent the channel becoming a soft-expulsion system

Distinguish:
- member-initiated exploration;
- mutually agreed transfer;
- disciplinary/separation procedure.

Local communities should not quietly route inconvenient members into `voluntary transfer` to avoid due process.

## B46-L09 — Preserve household/couple constraints

Connect to Batch 45: some transfer interests require same-community or nearby-community configurations.

## B46-L10 — Publish only aggregate research outputs above privacy thresholds

Possible metrics:
- proportion exploring alternatives;
- reason categories;
- time from interest to visit/transfer;
- inbound/outbound interest;
- post-transfer outcomes.

Suppress small identifiable cells.

## B46-L11 — Follow people who explore and stay

Exploration can lead to:
- transfer;
- recommitment;
- local change/voice;
- no action.

All are informative.

## B46-L12 — Staying becomes more interpretable when exit is actually usable

A low exit rate means little where moving is materially/socially impossible.

As mobility support and information improve, staying behavior becomes more preference-informative—still not a complete welfare measure.
