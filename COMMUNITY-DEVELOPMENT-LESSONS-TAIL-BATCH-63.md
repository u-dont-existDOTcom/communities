# Community development lessons — boundary egalitarianism and cross-community labor

Date: 2026-08-16
Disposition: article-gap refinement / practical design lesson; not a new empirical finding

## Root article gap

The article's egalitarian architecture is defined mainly around full members. The existing gap bank classifies as missing the status and rights of volunteers, employees, interns, guests, dependents, and other low-voice participants (G-014). The completed corpus already shows that communities can blame or discipline low-voice residents while full members retain the formal equality structure.

Core risk:

> A commune can be internally egalitarian while recreating hierarchy at its boundary.

A member-owned, member-governed community can still externalize undesirable labor, risk, blame, or dependency onto people who contribute materially but do not possess the same voice, grievance access, records, or exit protections.

## Louisa-cluster extension

The nearby-network model developed in Batch 62 adds a second boundary case: members of one intentional community may work for, rent from, trade with, or provide labor to another community while remaining politically anchored in their home community.

This creates a cross-polity labor problem. A person may be:

- a full citizen/member in Community A;
- a worker, contractor, renter, visitor, or service provider in Community B;
- socially embedded across both;
- governed by neither community's ordinary membership rules for the interface dispute.

The relevant design object is therefore not `member versus nonmember` alone. It is **role-specific standing that follows exposure**.

## Practical rule

Do not grant every guest or outside worker full community governance power. Instead, attach voice, records, grievance access, safety rights, and appeal to the domain in which the person is affected.

Examples:

- a sister-community worker does not need a vote on the host community's spiritual doctrine;
- they do need a defined voice and review route for workplace safety, scheduling, compensation, discipline, harassment, and records that affect them;
- a renter or land user needs standing on access, maintenance, liability, notice, and dispute resolution affecting that tenancy/use;
- a childcare exchange participant needs role-specific safeguarding, reporting, and review rights even if they are not a member of the host community.

## Why this matters for experimental clusters

A network of nearby communities can lower startup costs by sharing labor, land, businesses, transport, childcare, tools, and expertise. But if shared infrastructure is governed only through home-community membership, the network can quietly create a class of people who are highly exposed to another polity's decisions while lacking standing there.

The stronger design target is:

> **shared infrastructure without exported disenfranchisement.**

This is an article-facing gap because the current text discusses membership progression, forks, shared life, and egalitarian governance but does not separately specify rights and voice for people who materially participate without full membership.

## Relation to existing corpus

This note does not create a new finding. It synthesizes:

- G-014: missing fair status for volunteers, employees, interns, guests, and dependents;
- G-011: cross-organization work needs named operational ownership and conflict-safe escalation;
- T-01/S-01: authority must be bounded by function;
- the Batch 62 Louisa-cluster lead on cross-community labor and resource exchange.

## Article implication

Add a short section or paragraph near the membership/labor architecture explaining that equality must be audited across the whole **participation boundary**, not only among full members. Define role-specific rights for nonmembers and sister-community participants before federated labor/resource sharing scales.
