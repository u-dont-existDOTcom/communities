## B26-L01 — Track which high-stakes decisions depend on which findings
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

For sanctions, role restrictions, child/family access, federation alerts, financial decisions, governance disqualification, safety plans, and exit/property consequences, record the material premises used.

At minimum retain:

- finding/evidence identifier;
- rule/policy version;
- date and decision maker;
- whether the premise was necessary, sufficient, or contextual;
- any independent alternative grounds.

The purpose is not bureaucratic completeness. It is to make later correction possible.

---

## B26-L02 — When an upstream finding changes, run derivative-decision impact review
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Correcting one record does not automatically repair consequences that already propagated from it.

If a finding is overturned, narrowed, disputed, or materially corrected:

1. identify decisions that relied on it;
2. mark affected decisions `needs re-review`;
3. preserve unaffected decisions with genuinely independent sufficient grounds;
4. reverse, narrow, compensate, or reaffirm each consequence through the appropriate process;
5. document what remains unresolved.

Do not default either to "everything stays" or "everything automatically disappears."

---

## B26-L03 — Corrections must travel to the recipients of the original alert
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

If a community sends a conduct/safety/eligibility alert to a federation, neighboring community, insurer, employer-like communal work unit, or other authorized recipient, the record needs a correction/revocation route that reaches the same authorized recipients when the underlying information changes.

A corrected local file is insufficient if the old information continues governing elsewhere.

Privacy and need-to-know rules still apply; do not create broad rumor-distribution systems in the name of correction.

---

## B26-L04 — Pin rule and evidence versions at decision time
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

A later reviewer should be able to reconstruct:

- which rule version applied;
- which evidence state was available;
- which exceptions/temporary powers existed;
- which external/federation policy version was used.

Without this, a current policy can be mistakenly projected backward onto an old decision, or an old standard can remain embedded in a current consequence.

---

## B26-L05 — Add a `stale / needs re-review` state
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

A downstream decision whose premise changed is not necessarily valid, and not necessarily invalid.

Use an explicit intermediate state such as:

- active;
- stayed pending review;
- stale / needs re-review;
- reversed;
- superseded;
- reaffirmed on independent grounds.

This is especially important when immediate reversal could create another rights or safety problem.

---

## B26-L06 — Preserve independent sufficient grounds
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

If a decision had several independent bases, record them separately.

Example: a role restriction was based on both a verified licensing problem and an allegation later overturned. Correcting the allegation should trigger review, but it should not erase the separate licensing basis.

Conversely, do not invent new justifications retrospectively merely to save the old outcome.

---

## B26-L07 — Appeals should clean up stale consequences across systems
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

When an appeal reverses or materially changes a finding, check more than the headline sanction.

Possible dependent artifacts include:

- keys and physical access;
- passwords/admin permissions;
- bank/signature authority;
- childcare or records restrictions;
- work assignments;
- federation flags;
- internal directories/status labels;
- housing conditions;
- benefit or exit calculations;
- notices already sent to authorized outside parties.

A successful appeal that leaves these derivative consequences untouched is incomplete.

---

## B26-L08 — When a policy basis changes, identify affected pending and historical cases
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

A repealed, invalidated, or materially reinterpreted rule can affect existing decisions made under it.

Do not assume either universal retroactivity or universal grandfathering. Define:

- which pending cases convert;
- which final cases remain final;
- which high-impact old cases get review;
- what rights/settlements cannot be reopened;
- what correction/compensation is available.

This pairs with Batch 25 governance migrations and tombstones.

---

## B26-L09 — Use dependency-aware review selectively
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Do not reopen the whole community every time one fact changes.

Trace only material dependencies and inspect those decisions first. This reduces both administrative burden and the temptation to ignore corrections because remediation looks impossibly large.

---

## B26-L10 — Predefine a safe route for contradictory authoritative records
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

If two authoritative records conflict and an irreversible decision depends on resolving the contradiction, predefine what happens:

- stay the action;
- preserve temporary safety conditions;
- route to independent/federation review;
- use a time-bounded emergency rule;
- preserve the status quo where appropriate.

Do not let the first actor to notice or control the database silently choose which record counts.

---

# Batch 26 disposition

No Batch 26 idea is being claimed as Creative Tail novelty. Truth-maintenance, reactive case-management, record-correction, administrative appeal, provenance and change-propagation systems already implement close structures.

The communal-development value is operational: **make correction propagate through consequences, not merely through prose.**
