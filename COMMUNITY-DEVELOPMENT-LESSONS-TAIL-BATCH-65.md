## Empirical findings

### 1. Cross-community mobility in the FEC required a real interoperability layer

Historical FEC/Twin Oaks dual-membership and labor-exchange documents show that movement between communes was not a simple matter of `you can live at either place`.

Twin Oaks' dual-member policy required a member to designate a primary community for tax, property, citizenship/membership, and related legal purposes. A change of official residence required notification/coordination across labor, tax, accounting, health, and PEACH functions. PEACH costs were reconciled between communities based on residence. The policy explicitly provided that if money or labor accounting became too burdensome, either community could limit how often a dual member switched community of residence.

FEC/Twin Oaks labor-exchange rules likewise tracked reciprocal labor balances. A Twin Oaks member's ability to take a labor exchange at another community could depend on whether the other community already owed Twin Oaks a large labor debt and whether repayment was expected.

East Wind dual-membership/economic-agreement material shows the same general architecture: home/secondary-community status, proportional cost allocation, health/emergency responsibility, work approval, and rules for people moving between statuses.

This is direct evidence that **mobility between autonomous communities depends on administrative compatibility and settlement rules**. A right to move can become practically throttled by reconciliation workload even where the communities are friendly and ideologically aligned.

Primary historical sources:

- FEC archive, `Dual Member Policy - Twin Oaks - 2003`.
- FEC archive, Twin Oaks labor system / labor exchange rules.
- FEC archive, East Wind dual-membership/economic agreements.
- FEC archive, `Labor Exchange` project documentation.

Current continuity signal:

- the 2026 official FEC initiatives page still lists Exchange Trips as an active initiative: members temporarily work/live at other FEC communities while remaining compensated by their home community and with FEC-paid travel.

### 2. The current FEC rebuild has a narrower visible operating surface than the historical federation

The old FEC archive described a wider bundle including labor exchange, PEACH, the Community Loan Fund, the Mutual Aid Scholarship Fund, outreach, and other projects.

The current official 2026 site publicly lists:

- monthly calls;
- annual assembly;
- PEACH;
- exchange trips;
- mini-grants;
- reduced outreach during rebuilding.

Targeted searches of the current official domain found no current page for the historical Community Loan Fund or Mutual Aid Scholarship Fund. **This is not evidence that those programs were formally terminated**; their current disposition remains unresolved. It is evidence only that the rebuilt public operating surface is narrower/changed and that service continuity cannot be inferred from the federation name persisting.

The May 2025 delegate update said the main rebuild priorities were corporate/tax registration, policy revision, and regaining website control. By 2026 the official site says the FEC remains in an active rebuilding phase.

### 3. Federation membership has evolved toward multiple durable relationship states

Historical FEC material shows repeated difficulty fitting every cooperative relationship into a single `Community in Dialogue -> full member` trajectory:

- Short Mountain left in 1987 when its queer-sanctuary membership policy was treated as conflicting with the FEC anti-discrimination rule.
- Ganas wanted a durable relationship with the FEC without becoming an FEC-style full member; the Allied Community category was created in the 2000s.
- Living Energy Farm later used allied status when it was not becoming an income-sharing full member.
- FEC created `Re-forming Community` status in 2017 for communities that had materially changed after full accreditation.

The current 2026 site now displays three relationship categories:

- Full Member Communities;
- Communities in Dialogue, explicitly described as working toward full criteria/membership;
- Friends of the FEC, described as like-minded communities that either do not meet full criteria or choose not to pursue membership.

This is important for G-025. A federation lifecycle should distinguish **transitional non-membership** from **stable non-convergent affiliation**. Otherwise a network that wants institutional diversity quietly turns every relationship into an assimilation ladder.

Current site evidence also shows Alpha Farm as a full member after being described as a Community in Dialogue in the May 2025 rebuilding update, demonstrating that status transitions are active rather than merely historical categories.

### 4. A coordination federation can accumulate dispute/jurisdiction expectations even when it was not designed as a government

A 2019 Commune Life account described the FEC as a network intended to connect communes, facilitate transportation/labor exchange, and fund/support projects—not a governing body able to order member communes around. Yet it also reported that people repeatedly approached the FEC over harms or disputes inside member communes. The federation discussed creating a mediation/conflict team and a separate harm/abuse response team, but the author noted that commune workload made such initiatives difficult to sustain without dedicated people/time.

The December 2018 FEC Assembly itself dealt with controversial intercommunity/internal-community questions. A later participant interview with JB, an East Wind member and FEC delegate at the time, is chaptered by its uploader as covering `conflict in community, conflict across communities, justice, living up to bylaws, member expulsions, member burnout, racism` and later `Learning about mediation through the FEC process`.

The exact participant interview transcript was not available through the current connector, so no disputed factual reconstruction is made from it.

By the 2024 near-collapse account, unresolved intercommunity grievances were still part of the federation-level legitimacy problem: some Acorn members were upset with Twin Oaks, and at least one East Wind delegate remained upset about the 2018 Assembly. Those grievances coexisted with the separate delegate-succession/compliance/capacity failures documented in Batch 64.

Disposition: this is **not** promoted as a separate new gap. It strengthens G-023/G-024 and the existing independent-review/jurisdiction findings: once a federation controls meaningful status, shared resources, cooperation, and reputation, `we are not a governing body` does not by itself specify where cross-community harms and disputes go.

### 5. The local intercommunity network can remain live when the formal federation layer is weak

The strongest counterweight to treating the FEC collapse as `the network collapsed` is the Louisa cluster itself.

During the March 2024 Twin Oaks wildfire, Twin Oaks evacuated members to nearby Acorn; a Twin Oaks first-person account describes Acorn members arranging rooms, food, and space for evacuees and animals. Earlier records document Twin Oaks labor/housing assistance after an Acorn fire, labor exchanges with East Wind, and dense Twin Oaks-Acorn business/labor ties.

Current Twin Oaks material still describes income from work with its sister community through Southern Exposure Seed Exchange even after Acorn left the FEC. Thus bilateral/local cooperation can persist despite failure or rupture at the federation-corporate layer.

This supports an important refinement of G-024:

**the federation entity is not the whole network.** Critical cooperation should not be forced through a single organizational choke point. Bilateral and local-cluster relations, function-specific institutions, and the general federation can be separate overlapping layers.

This is not a claim that bilateral ties are always safer; they can also carry unresolved conflicts. It is evidence against designing the movement so that loss of one federation body automatically severs every useful intercommunity relation.

### 6. Acorn's departure is a useful stress case, but the public causal record is too thin for blame attribution

Current official FEC pages no longer list Acorn as a member. Commune Life in 2026 states that Acorn left the FEC but says the author does not understand the reason. A later Allen Butcher paper says the departure followed an Acorn-originating conflict that became a Twin Oaks-Acorn conflict after several Acorn members moved to Twin Oaks.

Because the public record located here does not give a sufficiently complete multi-party account, this batch does **not** infer who was right, whether mobility caused the conflict, or whether FEC procedures worsened it.

It is retained only as a stress case demonstrating that individual movement, sister-community relations, dispute jurisdiction, and federation affiliation can become coupled in practice.

## Candidate dispositions after hostile veto + corpus collision

### Survives as article gap: B65-1 / G-026 — Federation mobility needs interoperability, not merely permission

The existing corpus already covers portable exit benefits (G-012) and names intercommunity migration as a continuity option (G-016). The residual is narrower and not redundant:

**A federation can formally permit movement while making it administratively difficult or unsafe because autonomous communities use different labor, accounting, tax, health, housing, benefit, membership, and record systems.**

The FEC dual-member policy is unusually direct empirical support because it explicitly says excessive labor/money accounting burden can cause communities to limit switching frequency.

Operational requirement:

- define primary/secondary responsibility during a move;
- specify recognition of membership/status and any probation/transition state;
- settle or explicitly carry labor balances/debts;
- assign health/medical and PEACH responsibility;
- define tax/accounting responsibility;
- transfer relevant records with consent and role limits;
- specify housing/queue access and notice;
- define reimbursement between communities;
- prevent temporary gaps in basic needs/coverage/standing;
- name the dispute forum for cross-community transition problems;
- track administrative hours, delayed/aborted moves, benefit gaps, and switching restrictions as mobility-friction metrics.

Classification: **B**. This is a substantial federation/experimental-ecology operational addition, not a reversal of the article's existing exit/continuity logic.

Strict novelty status: **not evaluated to completion** because mandatory Parallel Task is unavailable in the current harness. Do not put G-026 in strict Creative-Tail findings on the basis of this batch.

### Fold into G-024, not new gap — federation entity != network

Bilateral emergency aid and ongoing sister-community business ties survived periods when the FEC corporate/coordination layer was weak or when Acorn was no longer a member. This sharpens service modularity/multi-layer topology but does not justify a separate article gap.

### Fold into G-025, not new gap — stable non-convergent affiliation

`Friends of the FEC` / historical Allied status provides a stable relationship state distinct from both full membership and a transitional Community-in-Dialogue ladder. Add this to lifecycle architecture rather than rediscovering `preserve diversity` as a generic principle.

### Fold into G-023/G-024/G-019, not new gap — dispute role creep / jurisdiction ambiguity

The FEC's history shows repeated pressure to address harms/conflicts without durable authority/capacity. This is important evidence but collides with existing jurisdiction, independent review, and federation anti-starvation findings.
