## Main lesson — Test measurement comparability before pooling community outcomes
**Status:** TAIL-PROVISIONAL + RESEARCH-CONTROL  
**Creative Tail:** C022 — Cross-community measurement canaries

A movement research center should not assume that two communities use the same outcome label at the same threshold merely because both forms contain the same field name.

Before pooling or ranking outcomes that depend on local interpretation, periodically circulate a **small standardized synthetic case bank** across communities/research teams and record how each classifies the same controlled cases.

Possible constructs:
- serious conflict;
- coercion / consent violation;
- governance capture;
- failed exit;
- safeguarding event;
- privacy violation;
- labor unfairness;
- member wellbeing/autonomy;
- disciplinary or exclusion severity.

If identical cases receive materially different categories or severity ratings, the raw community metrics are not yet demonstrated to be commensurate.

### Minimum architecture

1. use identical controlled case facts where feasible;
2. collect each community/coder's answer independently before cross-community discussion;
3. record category, severity, reportability, process trigger, confidence, and rationale;
4. treat disagreement as **measurement evidence**, not automatically as one community being wrong;
5. pretest whether groups understand the vignette equivalently;
6. preserve raw community reports separately from any adjusted/calibrated value;
7. repeat a subset after major governance, membership, schema, or reporting-incentive changes;
8. block or qualify pooled comparisons when measurement compatibility is poor.

### Critical caveat

Anchoring-vignette and measurement-invariance research shows that standardized cases are not automatically valid anchors. Different groups can understand the supposedly identical vignette differently. Validate interpretation and response consistency; retire bad canaries.

## B38-L01 — Do not treat shared field names as shared measurements
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + RESEARCH-CONTROL

`Serious incident`, `successful exit`, `member autonomy`, `child wellbeing`, and similar labels can conceal different thresholds or definitions across communities.

Operational rule: compare the measurement rules before comparing the numbers.

## B38-L02 — Preserve raw and calibrated data separately
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Never overwrite a community's original self-report with a harmonized or modeled value.

Keep distinct:
- raw local report;
- measurement-canary profile;
- independent/audit classification where available;
- adjusted/comparability model;
- uncertainty and failed assumptions.

## B38-L03 — Missingness is data, not zero
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + RESEARCH-CONTROL

A community with no leaver outcomes, child follow-up, adverse-event records, or exit data has **missing information**, not a demonstrated zero-event record.

Report missingness pattern and likely missingness mechanism explicitly.

## B38-L04 — Run leave-one-community and leave-one-lineage sensitivity checks
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + RESEARCH-CONTROL

Before publishing a movement-wide conclusion, recompute it after removing:
- each individual community;
- each major lineage/branch where descendants are correlated.

Large conclusion changes indicate fragility to one institution or inherited cluster.

Do not use lineage balancing unless it matches the estimand; a resident-weighted estimand should not be silently converted into a lineage-weighted one.

## B38-L05 — Preserve rare severe events outside robust averages
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Robust estimators can correctly resist statistical outliers while accidentally suppressing substantively decisive catastrophes.

Maintain a separate non-nettable rare-severe-event register alongside central-tendency statistics.

## B38-L06 — Keep evidence channels separate before reconciliation
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Track separately where feasible:
- community self-report;
- affected-person/leaver report;
- independent audit/review;
- external legal/medical/administrative record;
- researcher observation.

Do not average disagreement away before asking what produced it.

## B38-L07 — Require denominator provenance for rates
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

A numerator without a trusted exposure denominator can produce a persuasive but meaningless rate.

Record who/what was included in the denominator, time at risk, missing exposure, member turnover, and denominator source.

## B38-L08 — Treat schema changes as possible breaks in the trend line
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

When an outcome definition, severity threshold, reporting duty, or data-collection system changes materially, do not continue one longitudinal line by default.

Version the definition and test whether old/new measurements are bridgeable.

## B38-L09 — Separate reporting quality from substantive performance
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

A community that reports more completely can look worse than one that suppresses or fails to detect incidents.

Track data/reporting completeness separately from actual safety/wellbeing outcomes. Do not create incentives to hide problems.

## B38-L10 — Model observation/administrative capacity where it differs greatly
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Communities with professional administrators, better records, more formal processes, or larger staff may generate more measurable events than informal communities with equal or greater underlying harm.

Where this difference is material, stratify/model measurement capacity or qualify comparisons.

## B38-L11 — Do not assign one global credibility weight to a source
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

A community may report finances accurately but underreport internal conflict; a leaver may have excellent first-person evidence for one episode but little knowledge of current governance; an external regulator may have authoritative legal findings but weak daily-life outcome data.

Reliability is claim- and channel-specific.

## B38-L12 — Use negative controls only as diagnostics, not truth machines
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + RESEARCH-CONTROL

Outcomes a proposed community practice should not plausibly affect can help reveal hidden confounding or measurement artifacts. But a negative-control result does not by itself prove the causal model.

## Relation to existing tail findings

- **C001** asks whether communities mean the same *normative principle* in hard cases.
- **C012** asks whether claimed constitutional relations hold across paired cases.
- **C021** asks how changing scrutiny changes what evidence becomes observable.
- **C022** asks whether different communities map the **same observed case** into comparable empirical categories.

Together they suggest a research-center rule:

> Before using cross-community data to rank or recommend a practice, test both **what was observable** and **how the observed facts were classified**.
