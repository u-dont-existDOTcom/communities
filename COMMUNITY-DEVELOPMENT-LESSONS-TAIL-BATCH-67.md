## Empirical sequence

### 1. Short Mountain joined the FEC and then left over a constitutional interpretation conflict

A detailed FEC history compiled from federation records/participant knowledge reports:

- Short Mountain, a queer community in Tennessee, entered the FEC orbit in 1984;
- in 1987 it dropped FEC membership when its policy of operating as a sanctuary for queer people only was treated as conflicting with the FEC's anti-discrimination principle.

The same history notes that in 1987 there was also discussion of acknowledging that communities sometimes fell short of federation principles while still trying to move toward them.

This source is a later historical reconstruction rather than the original 1987 assembly minutes, so it establishes a strong historical lead but not every detail of the deliberation.

### 2. FEC practice later explicitly distinguished blanket exclusion from demographic balancing

An older FEC full-membership criteria page states that communities should not have blanket restrictions on joining based on sex, gender, age, race, sexual orientation, gender identity, and similar categories. It explicitly says the FEC had previously rejected communities that were only one gender and acknowledges that this standing decision could be controversial.

The same criteria page says demographic quotas can be acceptable, citing gender balance and average-age limits.

Historical East Wind policy supplies a concrete example: when housing was scarce and one gender exceeded 55% of the adult population, the waiting-list system gave the minority gender available housing at a three-to-one rate until balance was restored.

Thus the federation's operative equality standard was never simply `identity-conscious admission is forbidden`. It already distinguished some forms of demographic preference from blanket exclusion.

### 3. The September 2025 criteria introduce a more explicit oppressed-affinity exception

The current full-member criteria document linked from the official FEC policy page says it was revised by FEC delegates on September 12, 2025.

The document begins by warning that the criteria are only a guide because many decisions will require judgment calls about specific situations that cannot be anticipated.

Its current criterion 9 says:

- member communities should not have discriminatory restrictions on joining based on class, sex, gender, age, race, sexual orientation, gender identity, and related categories of oppression;
- communities **may be formed around affinity for a specific oppressed identity if they do not discriminate against other oppressed identities**;
- communities should make an earnest effort to identify and reduce structural barriers to access.

This is materially more nuanced than the older publicly indexed `no blanket restrictions; demographic quotas are acceptable` rule.

Do **not** infer from the available record that the 2025 revision was caused by the 1987 Short Mountain rupture. The evidence establishes policy evolution and a historically salient edge case, not a causal drafting history.

### 4. The old and current policy surfaces now disagree

The old `thefec.org` full-membership page still exposes the earlier rule against blanket identity restrictions while the current official site links the September 2025 criteria with an oppressed-affinity exception.

This is another concrete example of the G-023 digital-continuity problem: stale historical policy pages can look operationally current in search unless version/deprecation provenance is explicit.

## Candidate disposition

### Survives as article gap: B67-1 / G-028 — Federation principles need a constitutional casebook, not only abstract clauses

This is **not a new Creative-Tail mechanism**. It is a direct empirical federation application of existing strict survivor C001, `Active normative edge-case search`.

C001's retained pipeline is:

> select concrete cases for their ability to discriminate plausible constitutional/value interpretations; collect independent answers before deliberation; preserve resolved cases as a durable regression-test suite for future interpretation.

The Short Mountain sequence shows why that architecture matters at federation scale. `Oppose discrimination` did not itself answer:

- whether a queer sanctuary could restrict admission to preserve its sanctuary function;
- why a blanket affinity restriction and a demographic-balancing quota should be treated differently;
- what later policy revision should do with the old case;
- whether a community that fails the full-member constitutional test should lose all federation relationship or move to a non-member/friend status.

A federation therefore needs a **constitutional casebook / regression suite** around its abstract principles.

### Operational requirement

For each high-information edge case, preserve:

1. the concrete facts and actual decision being considered;
2. which constitutional principles plausibly point in different directions;
3. independent pre-deliberation judgments from decision makers where feasible;
4. the final decision and rationale;
5. dissent/minority reasoning;
6. the scope of the precedent — what it does and does not decide;
7. affected-party input and material consequences;
8. later outcomes if observable;
9. the policy/constitution version under which the case was decided;
10. whether later rule changes would change the result.

When a constitutional rule is revised, rerun the stored edge-case suite. If the answer changes, record the change explicitly instead of allowing the old precedent and new text to coexist ambiguously.

The casebook does **not** decide the substantive morality automatically. It makes the hard tradeoff inspectable, repeatable, and revisable instead of rediscovering it during the next membership crisis.

## Rejected overclaims

Do not claim from this batch that:

- all identity-restricted communities are legitimate;
- oppressed-affinity restrictions are always equality-promoting;
- demographic quotas and sanctuary restrictions are morally equivalent;
- the FEC's 2025 rule was caused by Short Mountain;
- the 1987 FEC decision was clearly wrong or clearly right;
- an affinity community should automatically receive full federation membership.

The durable finding is the **need for explicit precedent and revision memory when abstract federation principles collide in real cases**.
