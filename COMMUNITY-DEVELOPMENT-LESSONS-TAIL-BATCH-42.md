# Main lesson — Treat constituency-mutating decisions as reflexive constitutional decisions

**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + GOVERNANCE CONTROL

Admissions, expulsions, voting eligibility, fission, merger, delegate-selection and similar decisions can change **who will later have authority to review or reverse the decision**.

That makes them different from ordinary policy choices.

---

## B42-L01 — Flag constituency-mutating decisions explicitly

Agenda materials should state:
- how the future decision-maker population changes;
- which later decisions become easier/harder;
- whether a pending dispute/vote is affected.

## B42-L02 — Freeze eligibility for pending contested decisions when appropriate

Define the voting/decision electorate before a contested process begins so a faction cannot strategically admit/remove decisive voters midstream.

Do not use this as a pretext for denying ordinary membership rights indefinitely.

## B42-L03 — Separate safety decisions from coalition arithmetic

A genuine serious conduct-based restriction must not be blocked just because it changes a factional balance.

Conversely, factional benefit must not become the hidden basis for a purported safety/disciplinary action.

Use conduct evidence, recusal and review.

## B42-L04 — Run a pre/post constituency counterfactual

For major structural changes ask:
- who decides before?;
- who decides after?;
- who gains/loses future reversal power?;
- would likely pending decisions differ under the new constituency?;
- does one faction predictably benefit?

Political benefit is a diagnostic, not automatic proof of illegitimacy.

## B42-L05 — Membership/governance rules deserve heightened procedure

Depending on context consider:
- supermajority;
- advance notice;
- independent facilitation/review;
- fixed transition date;
- later confirmation;
- affected-member reply;
- federation appeal;
- sunset/review.

## B42-L06 — Do not let newly created authority be the sole validator of its creation

Where possible, the legitimacy of a new review/governance body should trace to the previously valid constitutional process, not only to the authority of the body after creation.

## B42-L07 — Distinguish genuine growth from membership packing

Diagnostic questions:
- was the ordinary admission pipeline used?;
- were standards selectively changed?;
- were applicants recruited around a pending vote?;
- were dissenters simultaneously suspended/expelled?;
- did timing determine the result?;
- would the process look acceptable if the factional advantage were reversed?

## B42-L08 — Include constituency effects in fission/merger planning

Connect to C020.

A split/merge changes not only assets and population but potentially:
- local majority/minority status;
- federation voting power;
- appeal/review jurisdiction;
- who controls records/assets;
- future rule amendment.

## B42-L09 — Preserve membership-roll integrity where formal eligibility matters

For consequential formal votes keep a durable record of:
- eligible list/cutoff;
- additions/removals;
- legal/constitutional basis;
- conflicts/appeals;
- delegate/proxy status.

## B42-L10 — Review new-constituency legitimacy later

A structural change can be valid under the old constituency yet become dead-hand control over the new one.

For major transitions consider a later confirmation/review after the new body exists and has enough independence to assess the arrangement.

## B42-L11 — Practical constituency can differ from formal franchise

Housing, employment, benefits, relationships, childcare and social viability can determine who can realistically remain and exercise formal rights.

Connect membership-roll analysis with practical-power/dependency mapping.

## B42-L12 — Membership composition is endogenous in research models

A governance/labor/family system partly creates its own membership population through admission and exit.

Do not casually control away membership composition as if it were purely external background.
