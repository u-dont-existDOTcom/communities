# Intentional Communities Research Report

## What the evidence says about power, safety, exit, autonomy, and replication

**Research status:** completed bounded synthesis, August 15, 2026
**Mode:** P0 research only; this report does not revise the published article
**Underlying evidence authority:** [`COMMUNITIES-EVIDENCE-LEDGER.csv`](../recovered/COMMUNITIES-EVIDENCE-LEDGER.csv)
**Full technical synthesis:** [`COMMUNITIES-FINAL-SYNTHESIS-REPORT.md`](../recovered/COMMUNITIES-FINAL-SYNTHESIS-REPORT.md)

This page is the public-readable map of a much larger audit. It is meant to make the research behind Joel Rosenblum's article *Building Healing Community “Escuelitas” to Save the World* inspectable without forcing readers to reconstruct the project from hundreds of source notes and machine-readable rows.

It is not a substitute for the evidence ledger. When this report compresses a case or a conclusion, the ledger remains the place to check what the source actually said, what it did **not** establish, what alternative interpretation remains live, the confidence assigned to the finding, and whether additional external verification is required before publication-facing reliance.

## The thesis and the research are different things

Joel's thesis is:

> **communal living is a return to our evolved ancestral pattern; large societies breed anomie and capture by psychopaths.**

That is the author's thesis. This research program was **not designed to test ancestral evolution, comparative anomie, the prevalence of psychopathy, or whether large societies are more capturable than small societies**. Historical actors are not retrospectively diagnosed here from conduct descriptions.

The audit asked a narrower empirical question: what do intentional communities, communal traditions, and selected adjacent systems show about dangerous or exploitative conduct, capture of authority, correction, children, care, exclusion, exit, property, succession, autonomy, and the ability of communal practices to reproduce?

The strongest relevant result is therefore a corollary, not a proof of the larger thesis: **small scale does not automatically eliminate capture**. It can relocate power into housing, money, care, confession, work, sex, family contact, childrearing, records, membership, and exit. A community trying to escape large-system domination still needs ways to keep its own authority bounded, reviewable, and survivable by the person who challenges it.

The primary corpus consists of the 984 journal PDFs and eight standalone sources. Adjacent records were added only through finite, named research units where the primary corpus left a functional question unresolved: child response, structured assessment and review, durable treatment and transition, official correction, fair separation and pooled risk, planned fission, autonomous Indigenous governance and legal pluralism, and Escuelita diffusion.

The research is historically rich, but it is **not a representative sample of all intentional communities**. Finding counts are coded coverage, not prevalence estimates, effect sizes, or votes. Twenty findings from different parts of one institutional history are not twenty independent replications, and one bounded-search finding may summarize a very large search space.

## The recurring failure mechanism

Across very different cases, one mechanism kept reappearing: legitimate authority in one domain could be converted into practical control over unrelated domains, including the channels through which that authority could be questioned.

The recurring sequence was:

`legitimate function or dependency -> role fusion -> evidence and remedy capture -> stigmatizing classification or retaliation -> costly exit -> outward cohesion or survival misread as success -> crisis and partial correction`

A spiritual teacher, therapist, founder, property custodian, financial expert, childcare authority, productive specialist, or security role may begin with a real function. The danger rises when the same person or aligned faction can also define the problem, control records, classify dissent, determine consequences, restrict outside contact, control necessities, and review complaints about itself.

This is why a formal council, consensus meeting, election, or equality principle is not enough by itself. The operational questions are more concrete: Who controls appointments? Who controls the agenda and records? Who can impose an emergency condition? Who can remove the founder? Can someone appeal without permission from the person being challenged? Can the reviewer obtain evidence the challenged authority cannot edit or suppress?

The audit therefore supports **decomposing practical power by function**, not merely asking whether a community is democratic in form.

## Participation is not the same as an override

Several communities had abundant speech, meetings, confession, feedback, criticism, or consultation while still lacking a protected way to contradict the dominant authority.

The evidence repeatedly distinguishes **being allowed to speak** from having an effective route to create and preserve a record, challenge an official interpretation, obtain independent review, correct false information, appeal, and reach another competent forum when the local chain is implicated.

That makes evidence itself a governance function. A community that cares about correction needs to know who can receive a complaint, who owns the original record, what confidentiality means, who can inspect competing evidence, when recusal is mandatory, who can reopen a conclusion, and whether a dissenter or leaver can retain records after departure.

## Communities need real separation authority—but not arbitrary expulsion

The evidence does not support either pole: “never exclude anyone” or “the group can remove whomever it decides is bad.”

Open access without usable boundary authority was sometimes associated with chronic conflict, sanitation breakdown, violence, or the practical rule of whoever controlled necessities. At the other extreme, informal or status-driven exclusion could become conformity enforcement, retaliation, humiliation, property loss, or banishment without a reliable evidentiary process.

The supported direction is **bounded separation authority**: conduct-specific thresholds, immediate lawful protection when necessary, evidence preservation, notice, recusal, reply, independent review, proportional and time-limited interim conditions, appeal, protection of necessities and family contact, and a neutral process for material settlement.

Mediation can be valuable. It is not a substitute for determining contested facts or protecting a nonconsenting person's rights.

## There is no validated “good person” or “dangerous person” shortcut

The corpus did not validate contribution, productivity, charisma, therapeutic fluency, spiritual reputation, long residence, conformity, criminal history, poverty, distress, or an administrator's suitability label as a general danger screen.

This cuts in both directions. Communities need not pretend that all applicants should have identical access immediately. They can make **role-specific, staged decisions** about access to children, money, medicine, vehicles, records, housing authority, or emergency powers. But the evidence supports tying those decisions to relevant conduct, competence, supervision, legal constraints, and review—not to a claim that the community has discovered a person's moral essence.

Professional structured-risk tools in the adjacent evidence did not solve this problem either. They showed group-level and outcome-specific predictive value with material error and implementation limits; they did not supply individual certainty or a lay membership screen.

## Community care is valuable; fused care and government are not the same thing

The research strongly supports the ordinary value of community: attention, meals, transport, belonging, companionship, observation, mutual aid, practice, and voluntary peer support.

It does **not** support treating “the community is the therapist” as a license to fuse care with discipline, custody, romance, employment, housing, membership, evidence control, or medical authority. The historical record includes cases where therapeutic or confessional systems became part of the chain through which dissent, family contact, medical decisions, sexuality, or membership were controlled.

The practical boundary is a firewall: peer care can remain communal while clinical authority, serious safeguarding, custody, coercive decisions, and review of alleged harm remain in competent and sufficiently independent hands.

## Children need rights that do not depend on adult voluntariness

Adults can decide to join a community. Children do not make the same founding choice, and their practical ability to exit is different.

The evidence supports direct child protections around food, sleep, healthcare, education, privacy, bodily safety, family and outside contact, communication assistance, independent advocacy and reporting, refusal and reply, portable records and learning, and transition support.

The hardest question in this research remains unresolved: **what should a community do when a child remains gravely dangerous despite ordinary care and accountability?**

Across the completed intentional-community and standalone searches, the project did not locate a case with the complete sequence:

`persistent dangerous conduct by a child -> conduct-specific allegation -> assessment -> intervention -> review -> later outcome`

That is a **bounded null**, not evidence that no community ever faced or managed such a child. Privacy, euphemism, destroyed records, aggregate reporting, or routing into family, school, disability, juvenile, medical, or child-protection systems could conceal relevant cases.

The adjacent evidence helps separate functions—assessment, immediate safety, de-escalation, safeguarding, review, treatment, family coordination, transition, and later outcomes—but does not demonstrate a reliable internal intentional-community solution or transfer professional/statutory powers to lay communal authorities.

## Exit has to work when leaving is inconvenient

A right to leave on paper is not enough if the person loses the cash, records, housing, transport, healthcare, family contact, portable benefits, advice, or time needed to use it.

The research therefore distinguishes four different things:

1. formal permission to leave;
2. material usability;
3. relational continuity and nonretaliation; and
4. procedural vindication, including valuation, records, advice, review, and enforceable payment.

A serious exit design should be tested against a hostile or financially inconvenient departure, not only the amicable case.

This is also why property and accounting turned out to be part of the practical constitution. Title, debt, reserves, account authority, appointments, removal, benefit vesting, liquidity, records, succession, and audit can determine actual power more decisively than an inspirational governance statement.

## Independent correction does not mean “the state”

One of the most important corrections in the research came from Joel's objection to an earlier state-centric synthesis.

The initial evidence base was dominated by voluntary communities inside nation-state jurisdiction, plus courts, regulators, licensed care, child-welfare systems, and official inquiries. That supported the need for routes outside an implicated private authority, but it did **not** justify treating “independent” as synonymous with “state-operated.”

The Zapatista record is a counterexample. In the 2003 structure, autonomous municipalities retained broad local functions while regional Good Government Councils handled intermunicipal mediation, complaints against autonomous councils, investigation, correction orders, and compliance monitoring. That is review outside the implicated municipality but inside the autonomous political order. The 2023 reorganization further demonstrates that governance scale itself was treated as revisable through evaluation and self-criticism.

Cherán supplies a hybrid case: communal government and security alongside selective serious-crime referral, public-resource dependence, and use of a federal court to protect Indigenous election rules. CRAC-PC shows why isolated village autonomy can be insufficient when regional federation is needed for information, solidarity, justice, and resistance to capture. UNDRIP supplies a rights boundary: Indigenous peoples may maintain institutions and juridical systems, while human-rights standards remain applicable.

The corrected principle is therefore relational:

**put each function at the lowest competent layer that is not reviewing itself, has enough scale and enforceability for the problem, preserves direct individual rights and bypass routes, and is itself accountable.**

Depending on the function and the actual political environment, that may be an immediate community, neighboring community, autonomous municipality, regional Indigenous council, movement federation, professional body, public agency, court, state, or international forum. A layer may be skipped when urgency, conflict, rights, competence, or enforceability requires it.

Indigenous territorial and juridical authority is its own evidence lane. It must not be collapsed into a generic claim that any voluntary commune has equivalent jurisdiction.

For the full correction, see [`COMMUNITIES-AUTONOMY-LEGAL-PLURALISM-REPORT.md`](../recovered/COMMUNITIES-AUTONOMY-LEGAL-PLURALISM-REPORT.md).

## Did the Zapatista Escuelita actually reproduce communities?

The answer depends on what counts as reproduction.

The project uses a six-level lineage ladder:

1. encounter;
2. continued alumni relation;
3. practice transfer;
4. creation of a new organization;
5. a durable shared-life or governing institution; and
6. measured later human outcomes.

The evidence is strong for continued alumni relations and meaningful practice transfer. Alumni appeared as collective actors in multiple places after the school, and qualitative follow-up describes participants carrying principles into organizing, care, academic, political, and NGO work.

**Espacio de Coordinación Grietas en el Muro** is the strongest organization-level candidate found. A 2016 record explicitly includes Escuelita students within a mixed group creating an alternate CompArte site, and the named coordination space remained publicly visible in later organizing records through a 2024 CNI statement.

But the causal lineage is mixed, internal governance is not documented, residential life is not established, and later human outcomes are unmeasured. Pre-2013 evidence also matters: transnational activists were already “taking Zapatismo home,” and projects such as Casa del Pueblo and Universidad de la Tierra Califas existed before the 2013–2014 Escuelita. Later resemblance can be older roots, reinforcement, or grafting rather than descent from the school.

The defensible conclusion is therefore strong but narrower than “replication engine”: **Zapatismo is well supported here as a movement school and network generator. The located evidence does not yet demonstrate that the Escuelita generated a population of durable new residential or autonomous-governing communities with measured human outcomes.**

That is an evidence ceiling, not proof that no such descendants exist.

For the complete descendant audit, see [`COMMUNITIES-ESCUELITA-SEED-OUTCOMES-REPORT.md`](../recovered/COMMUNITIES-ESCUELITA-SEED-OUTCOMES-REPORT.md).

## Success is a vector, not a score

Longevity, cohesion, size, member satisfaction, ecological performance, founder removal, a conviction, a license revocation, asset preservation, process completion, or short-term in-program improvement are different endpoints. They can conflict.

The research therefore treats success as a non-nettable vector that includes at least safety, child wellbeing, autonomy and dissent, access to remedy, material security, exit usability, family continuity, mission performance, institutional viability, founder replaceability, adverse effects, and post-exit/post-treatment/post-fission/post-correction outcomes.

A community can be loving, productive, ecological, durable, or spiritually meaningful while still failing a rights or safety floor. Conversely, dissolution can sometimes be adaptive rather than proof that the project failed.

## Continuity and replication are not the same as preserving one institution forever

Communal purpose can persist through successor institutions, migration, parallel organizations, alumni networks, teaching, conversion of legal form, leadership succession, voluntary forks, and daughter communities.

That makes reproduction a movement-level question. A successful flagship that never teaches or reproduces may be less relevant to Joel's mission than a community whose members routinely form viable daughters or carry useful practices into other settings.

But diffusion needs lineage controls. Inspiration is not the same as a new organization; a new organization is not automatically a durable communal institution; durability is not automatically good human outcomes. Preexisting influences, mixed parentage, failed descendants, and daughter outcomes all matter.

## The combined design is a synthesis, not a proven blueprint

The cross-corpus synthesis assembles a recurring counterdesign from separately supported components: practical power mapping; direct rights; role-specific staged access; fair separation; care firewalls; independent evidence channels; usable exit; fiduciary controls; capacity planning; non-nettable outcome measurement; succession and fission rules; and named jurisdictional interfaces.

The full architecture is labeled **S-15, model-assisted synthesis**. **No source in this corpus validates that entire package as one tested system.** Its components have different evidentiary status and should remain separately testable.

The newer operational lessons extend this into testable governance engineering—such as checking whether appeal rights remain reachable under recusal and vacancies, measuring how quickly authority is actually revoked after role changes, defining deadlock defaults before conflict, and auditing whether formal procedures are followed in practice. Those are practical or provisional design lessons, not findings that the communities corpus has already proven as a package. See [`COMMUNITY-DEVELOPMENT-LESSONS.md`](../COMMUNITY-DEVELOPMENT-LESSONS.md).

## What the research still does not know

The completed program leaves thirteen explicit boundaries:

1. No intentional-community case supplies the complete persistent-dangerous-child sequence with later outcome.
2. No source tests the combined S-15 architecture as a package.
3. The corpus cannot estimate how common each failure or protective component is across communities.
4. It does not compare intentional communities with nuclear families, ordinary neighborhoods, institutions, or states on one common outcome panel.
5. Many positive components lack adoption, fidelity, adverse-effect, leaver, child, survivor, or long-term outcome data.
6. Many official corrections lack implementation and recovery follow-up.
7. Many exit, pooled-risk, and fission instruments lack executed agreements, audited reserves, actual payouts, refusals, appeals, and household outcomes.
8. Historical communal-wave evidence does not establish a present-day 2024–2026 surge in communal formation or interest.
9. Reporting-system performance is difficult to measure because unseen, destroyed, inaccessible, or institutionally filtered cases have no denominator.
10. The minimum set of safeguards that gives the most protection for the least bureaucracy, cost, false-positive exclusion, or loss of local autonomy remains unknown.
11. The 2023 Zapatista reorganization lacks an independent later-outcome evaluation in this unit, and there is no common outcome panel comparing autonomous, hybrid, and state-centered arrangements.
12. No public Escuelita alumni registry or longitudinal cohort gives an adoption, project-formation, abandonment, or failure rate.
13. No located directly descended residential commune or autonomous federation has a complete durability and human-outcome panel.

These are boundaries around the conclusions, not reasons to erase what did converge. They are also **not an authorization for an unbounded new research sweep**. Any future research should start with a finite question whose answer could materially change a decision or claim.

## Verification status: why 133 findings are still flagged

The ledger currently marks 133 findings as needing some external verification before certain publication-facing uses and 65 as not requiring it at this checkpoint.

That flag is deliberately conservative. It does not mean 133 findings are probably wrong. Depending on the row, it can mean the research relied on a review-level account where the underlying primary record should be checked before naming an allegation; a historical legal proposition needs current-law verification; a clinical framework needs a current authoritative source; an implementation claim needs a later record; or a publication-facing sentence would benefit from tracing the exact original evidence.

The correct next verification strategy is **claim-selective**: first decide which findings and named examples the article or public report actually relies on, then verify those load-bearing claims against the best available primary/current sources. Rechecking all 133 indiscriminately would mix necessary publication hardening with low-value work on findings that may never appear in prose.

## Bottom line

The research does not hand us a proven model community. It does something more useful for design: it identifies where very different communities repeatedly became hard to correct, which safeguards have some bounded support, which apparent success signals are unreliable, and where the evidence still stops.

The central design lesson is not “trust the community” or “trust the state.” It is: **do not let the authority whose conduct is at issue control the evidence, the review, the necessities that make dissent possible, and the only path to remedy. Put each function at a competent layer, preserve direct rights and bypass routes, and measure what happened to people later.**

That conclusion can inform the next version of the article. It should not be mistaken for proof of Joel's larger thesis—or weakened into a denial of that thesis merely because this particular evidence program was designed to answer different questions.
