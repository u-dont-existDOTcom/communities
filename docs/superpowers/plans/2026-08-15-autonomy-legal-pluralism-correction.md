# Autonomy and legal-pluralism correction

Date: 2026-08-15
Mode: P0 research correction; no article prose revision
Status: accepted from owner correction and in progress

## Decision

The final synthesis made a sampling-frame error. It drew mainly from intentional communities operating inside nation-state jurisdiction, then generalized the adjacent court, regulator, clinical, and inquiry evidence into a state-centric rule.

The correction will add a bounded autonomous-governance evidence lane rather than reinterpret the existing sources. It will test Zapatista governance against two comparative patterns: Cheran's hybrid autonomous/state interface and the CRAC-PC's regional Indigenous policing and justice system. UNDRIP supplies a normative rights boundary, not outcome evidence.

## Question

Does safe correction require an institution outside the community or the nation-state, or does it require functional independence from the implicated decision-maker at a competent scale?

## Hypotheses to distinguish

1. **State-monopoly hypothesis:** police, courts, licensed systems, and state agencies must retain the relevant functions in every community form.
2. **Functional-independence hypothesis:** the necessary property is that the implicated authority cannot control evidence, review, appeal, or remedy; the competent layer may be autonomous, Indigenous, federated, professional, public, or state-run.
3. **Local-sufficiency hypothesis:** a single village or residential community can safely retain all functions without a translocal appeal, capacity, or defense layer.

The evidence may reject more than one hypothesis. A Zapatista counterexample can reject state monopoly without validating local sufficiency.

## Evidence unit

Promote six findings, F-187 through F-192:

- the 2003 Zapatista municipal and Good Government Council architecture, including internal regional correction;
- the 2023 GAL/CGAZ/ACGAZ reorganization and its contested-territory boundary;
- Zapatista outward pedagogy and adaptation beyond the territorial core;
- Cheran's assembly government, community security and justice, selective state referral, legal recognition, and incomplete economic autonomy;
- the CRAC-PC comparison showing that village custom alone was insufficient while regional translocal institutions supplied accountability and defense; and
- UNDRIP Article 34's recognition of Indigenous juridical systems subject to international human-rights standards.

## Claim correction

The old universal claim is replaced by two claims:

- **S-16:** Independent correction is a relationship, not a geography. The reviewer must be independent of the implicated authority; it need not be outside the movement or operated by the nation-state.
- **S-17:** Jurisdictional placement is contingent on territorial control, competence, scale, spillover, coercive environment, enforceability, and direct rights. Translocal federation can be the protective layer; state and professional interfaces remain contingent tools and backstops rather than a universal design premise.

S-15 remains the combined synthesis but changes from a three-box state-centered table to a jurisdictional ladder with direct individual bypass:

`person -> immediate community -> neighboring/autonomous review -> regional federation -> public/state/international backstop`

No step is automatically superior. Each function goes to the lowest competent layer that is not reviewing itself and cannot disable the person's direct rights.

## What is falsified, qualified, and retained

- **Falsified:** independent correction universally equals outside state/legal correction.
- **Qualified:** state, licensed, and statutory systems may remain necessary where an autonomous order lacks competence, territorial reach, enforceability, or a rights-protecting appeal.
- **Retained:** the accused local authority cannot be the final judge of its own evidence or conduct; serious harms need a competent route beyond it.
- **Added:** federation and translocal solidarity are safety and anti-capture infrastructure, not merely cultural networks.

## Transfer boundaries

- Zapatista autonomy is de facto, territorially rooted, armed and politically organized; it is not a direct template for an ordinary voluntary commune.
- The uprising created leverage and autonomous institutions, not uncontested sovereignty. Mexican public authority, neighboring non-Zapatista communities, cartels, paramilitaries, markets, and state programs remain part of the environment.
- Primary communiques establish intended architecture, not implementation quality or later human outcomes.
- Cheran is a hybrid, not a no-state case: serious crimes were referred to a state prosecutor and some programs depended on state subsidies, while communal organization preceded and drove legal recognition.
- CRAC-PC evidence is observational and strongest for regional accountability and anti-capture mechanisms, not proof that every Indigenous justice outcome is fair.
- UNDRIP is normative and expressly retains an international-human-rights boundary.
- Indigenous or communal authority is not inherently rights-protecting. Direct individual routes, child rights, gender equality, evidence controls, and appeal remain necessary questions at every layer.
