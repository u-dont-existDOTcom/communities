## Empirical findings

### 1. The old LEX design explicitly treated intercommunity labor as a reciprocal balance

Twin Oaks' historical Labor Exchange (LEX) policy describes visiting labor as outside its annual labor plan because the community expected to get the labor back during the same planning period.

The rule was balance-sensitive:

- outgoing LEX was generally approved when Twin Oaks owed labor to the destination, or when the destination did not owe Twin Oaks a large labor debt and seemed likely to repay;
- if the destination already owed Twin Oaks a large labor debt, a Twin Oaks member generally could not claim LEX credit for working there.

This is a reasonable routine-exchange rule. But it means the same ledger cannot automatically function as an asymmetric solidarity mechanism: the community that has already received more labor is precisely the one whose access to additional credited labor is restricted.

Historical source: Twin Oaks Labor System / Labor Policy, Labor Exchange section.

### 2. In an actual community stress episode, FEC used a separate direct-aid channel

A 2010 FEC Assembly report described East Wind as the community most in need after several member deaths in a short period plus business and regulatory pressures. Delegates discussed special LEX opportunities for East Wind members, but later created a **special FEC budget for direct aid to East Wind**.

This is important because it is not merely a theoretical objection to reciprocal accounting. In a concrete asymmetric shock, the federation used a distinct budgetary support channel rather than treating all help as ordinary reciprocal labor debt.

Historical source: `FEC Assembly Report by Drew Acorn` (2010).

### 3. The June 2026 FEC Exchange Program explicitly rejects the old parity assumption

The current FEC Exchange Program Policy was approved in January 2026 and last amended in June 2026. Its purpose statement says it replaces the old LEX and LETS programs because they were `somewhat confusing, under-developed, and based on a transactional system that falsely assumed parity between communities`.

The replacement design is explicitly non-transactional:

- there is **no formal labor debt or tracking** inside the Exchange Program;
- trips may be approved for skill-sharing, mutual aid, relationship-building, cultural cross-pollination, communard renewal, and exploration;
- mutual-aid trips receive priority when a host community has a time-sensitive need for extra support;
- travelers receive full labor credit/compensation from their home community for the exchange;
- travelers are normally expected to contribute about three hours of labor per day at the host community;
- the policy says this simplified expectation is partly intended to avoid the logistical complications of matching different community labor systems;
- the FEC Exchange Fund covers travel, while host communities provide basic accommodations and traveling communities retain specified responsibilities for their members;
- delegates are told to seek balanced distribution over time while taking differing community population sizes into account.

This is unusually direct institutional learning: the federation itself identified **false parity + transactional accounting + cross-system complexity** as defects in the old exchange architecture and replaced them with a bounded interface.

Current source: `FEC Exchange Program Policy`, approved January 2026 / amended June 2026, linked from the official FEC policy page.

### 4. This supplies an important correction to G-026: interoperability does not require system harmonization

Batch 65's G-026 identified the administrative friction in historical dual membership: tax, housing, labor, accounting, health, PEACH, and status coordination could make mobility cumbersome enough that switching frequency might be limited.

The 2026 Exchange Program shows a second solution for **temporary intercommunity movement**: do not reconcile the full internal systems at all. Standardize only the boundary protocol.

The current interface specifies:

- consent by sending and host communities;
- coordinator and host roles;
- travel funding;
- host accommodation expectations;
- a simple host-labor expectation;
- full home-community labor credit;
- responsibility for emergencies and unexpected costs;
- incident/problem records and future-approval consequences.

The underlying community labor systems remain autonomous.

So G-026 should not be read as `standardize everybody's labor/benefit system`. The more precise rule is:

> **standardize the minimum interface necessary to make movement usable; preserve local systems unless deeper portability actually requires harmonization.**

This also preserves local selective membership and capacity constraints. Exchange participation requires consent; it is not a right to enter another community, and full residential/member migration remains a different problem from a temporary exchange trip.

### 5. Federation dormancy has a digital-identity/decommissioning failure mode

The current official FEC site explicitly says `thefec.org` is the old website, is out of date, and is no longer maintained by the FEC.

In August 2026, the old domain was observed serving unrelated current political content under the page title/header of the Federation of Egalitarian Communities. The provenance/cause of that content was not established in this research; do **not** infer hacking, sale, or a specific takeover mechanism from this observation alone.

The operational failure is narrower and well supported: once an institution abandons a formerly official domain without controlling what later appears there, search engines and old links can continue to present unrelated material with the institution's historical identity attached.

Disposition: fold into G-023 federation anti-starvation / degraded-mode duties rather than minting a separate article gap.

A federation maintenance/decommissioning checklist should therefore include:

- registrar/domain ownership and renewal;
- DNS and redirect control;
- website/admin credential succession;
- canonical-current-site notices;
- durable read-only archive snapshots;
- retirement of obsolete forms/contact routes;
- preservation of policy provenance/version dates;
- monitoring of formerly official domains and high-ranking stale pages long enough to detect misleading reuse.

## Candidate dispositions after hostile veto + collision

### Survives as article gap: B66-1 / G-027 — Do not force asymmetric intercommunity support into a parity ledger

The article already supports federation, mutual-aid agreements, intercommunity movement, and shared infrastructure. What it does not yet distinguish is **the accounting semantics of different kinds of intercommunity help**.

A single reciprocal ledger works poorly when it is asked to cover several different purposes:

- ordinary labor exchange where return is actually expected;
- emergency support after an asymmetric shock;
- skill-sharing whose benefit may be indirect or delayed;
- exploratory movement and communard renewal;
- relationship-building whose value is deliberately not bilateral or immediately measurable.

Historical FEC LEX required reciprocal labor balancing. The 2010 East Wind stress episode used a separate direct-aid budget. The 2026 FEC redesign then explicitly removed labor-debt tracking and rejected the assumption of parity between communities.

Operational rule:

1. **Classify the transfer before accounting for it.** Is it reciprocal exchange, mutual aid/grant, shared-service contribution, training/skill transfer, or exploratory/relationship travel?
2. Use a debt/balance ledger only when repayment is genuinely part of the bargain.
3. Do not silently convert emergency aid or federation-funded exploration into bilateral debt.
4. For non-transactional exchange, preassign who covers travel, accommodations, home labor credit, host labor burden, emergencies, and coordination.
5. Preserve local labor/economic systems where a small shared interface is enough.
6. Track distribution across communities and normalize by relevant capacity/population so `non-transactional` does not become invisible permanent extraction from one node.
7. If a grant/aid relationship later becomes reciprocal, make that a new explicit agreement rather than retroactively reclassifying past aid as debt.

**Why this is not just `be generous`:** the failure concerns ledger design. A federation can become less capable of helping a distressed or structurally smaller member if every supportive flow is forced to balance bilaterally. The FEC's own 2026 policy identifies false parity and transactionality as defects of the prior design.

**Relationship to G-026:** G-026 is about making movement administratively usable across autonomous systems. G-027 is about deciding what intercommunity transfers **mean economically** and therefore whether they should create reciprocal obligations at all. The 2026 Exchange Program is evidence for both, but the design questions are distinct.

Classification: **B**. Mutual-aid agreements and federation continuity already exist in the article/corpus, but the reciprocal-vs-nontransactional accounting distinction is a substantial missing operational layer.

Strict novelty status: **not promoted**. Adjacent mutual-credit/cooperative-finance literature already contains countercyclical and non-bilateral accounting ideas, and mandatory Parallel Task is unavailable in this runtime.

### Fold into G-026, not a new gap — interoperability via adapters/interfaces

Temporary movement can sometimes be enabled by a federation protocol that translates only the boundary conditions instead of unifying internal systems. Add this as an explicit correction to G-026.

### Fold into G-023, not a new gap — digital decommissioning / identity continuity

The old FEC domain now demonstrating unrelated content after being officially retired is a concrete federation-maintenance failure mode. Treat domains, credentials, redirects, archives, and policy provenance as part of institutional succession/degraded mode.

### Rejected as strict novelty / generic theory

Do not promote the following as Creative-Tail discoveries:

- reciprocity can fail under asymmetric capacity;
- mutual aid is not ordinary barter;
- countercyclical support is useful;
- multilateral debt clearing can reduce bilateral gridlock;
- emergency reserves reduce shock exposure;
- larger members can subsidize smaller members.

These are established elsewhere. Their value here is the **specific FEC operating failure/redesign and article application**, not theoretical novelty.
