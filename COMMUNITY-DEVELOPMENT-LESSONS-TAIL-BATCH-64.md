# Community development lessons — FEC federation failure / rebuild lane

Date: 2026-08-16
Disposition: post-corpus empirical/design addendum; no article prose revised

## Question

What failure modes emerge at the federation layer even when member communes themselves remain viable?

## Primary empirical leads

### 1. General FEC near-collapse, 2024

A September 2024 Commune Life account by Raven, an FEC participant/observer, described the Federation of Egalitarian Communities as `pretty much functionally gone` while emphasizing that the member communities themselves were not the thing failing. The account reports:

- legal/financial trouble attributed largely to neglected organizational paperwork;
- disputes among member communities;
- delegates leaving their home communities without clear replacements;
- an individual taking on legal/financial repair outside the normal delegate role;
- later scheduled calls with only one participant;
- people at member communes being absorbed by local work/crises and giving little attention to FEC maintenance.

This is a participant account, not an independent audit, but it supplies a concrete mechanism-level lead.

Source: Commune Life, `Not With a Bang but a Whisper`, 2024-09-23.

### 2. FEC rebuilding, 2025–2026

A May 2025 update attributed to FEC delegates said the organization had spent the prior year recovering from inactivity/disorganization. Named priorities included:

- corporate registration and tax filing;
- policy review/update;
- regaining control of the old website.

The current official FEC site at `egalitariancommunities.org` states that the organization is in an active rebuilding phase and that the old `thefec.org` site is obsolete.

These records support the existence of a real federation-maintenance failure/rebuild episode; they do not independently adjudicate every causal claim in the 2024 participant account.

### 3. Re-forming Community status

FEC's 2017 `Re-forming Communities Status` policy was created for full-member communities that no longer met full-membership conditions. The background gives three distinct triggers:

- effectively complete membership turnover at Sapling, later renamed Mimosa;
- Sandhill potentially shrinking to a three-member family unit, creating concern about inegalitarian power over later joiners;
- The Midden having abandoned full income sharing well before finally leaving FEC.

The policy explicitly notes that major turnover can make re-forming closer to starting a new community than simply returning to alignment.

Source: FEC, `Re-forming Communities Status`, adopted 2017-12-08.

### 4. PEACH governance separation

The current official FEC initiatives page says PEACH is governed independently from the FEC by representatives from participating member communities. Older FEC documentation likewise distinguishes PEACH's governing body from FEC delegates and states that FEC delegates have no authority over PEACH.

This is an important architecture lead but **not causal proof** that separation allowed PEACH to survive the general federation's 2024 dysfunction. No claim of uninterrupted PEACH performance has been established in this lane.

## Practical lessons

### B64-1 — Federation anti-starvation

Treat translocal maintenance as productive capacity, not leftover volunteer labor. Reserve time/money for delegate succession, compliance, records, finances, communications, meetings, and restart capacity.

The stronger residual is temporal: local crises can simultaneously pull attention away from the federation precisely when translocal coordination is most useful. Build backup ownership and minimum-operating-state checks rather than assuming formal delegate obligations guarantee actual liveness.

### B64-2 — Community-level accreditation must expire or requalify after material change

Do not attach federation trust permanently to a name, land parcel, or corporation. Track people, site, legal control, governance/economic rules, mission, and accreditation separately.

Material change should trigger review rather than punishment. Useful lifecycle states include forming, in-dialogue, full, re-forming, dormant, and dissolved.

### B64-3 — Modularize critical shared services

Where a federation provides critical functions, consider separate bounded governance for high-consequence services so failure of the general coordination layer does not automatically kill every service. Preserve explicit interfaces, direct standing, accounts, succession, and restart rules.

PEACH is a design analogue only; the current evidence does not establish the causal effectiveness of this modularity.
