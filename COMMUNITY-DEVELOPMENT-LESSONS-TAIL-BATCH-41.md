# Main lesson — Current-member happiness is not the effect of a community design

**Status:** FOUNDATIONAL RESEARCH-CONTROL / KNOWN

A community practice partly determines who remains available to answer later surveys. If dissatisfied residents leave faster under one design, the remaining residents can look unusually happy even when the design did not improve the entrant population.

Therefore current-resident outcomes are descriptive and useful, but they are not automatically causal measures of design success.

---

## B41-L01 — Follow fixed-entry cohorts through exit

Define cohorts at clear events such as:
- provisional residence start;
- full membership;
- household/child entry;
- start of a major labor/governance/family intervention.

Follow the cohort for a fixed period whether members stay or leave.

## B41-L02 — Treat leaving as an outcome and an observation transition

Record where possible:
- exit timing;
- voluntary / pressured / expelled / practical;
- stated reasons;
- housing/material effects;
- family/relationship continuity;
- later wellbeing;
- later view of the community;
- whether exit itself obstructed follow-up.

Do not code exit simply as missing data.

## B41-L03 — Report three outcome layers separately

1. **Current-resident state** — how current members experience the community now.
2. **Fixed-entry cohort outcome** — what happened to everyone entering over a defined horizon.
3. **Leaver trajectory** — outcomes/reasons after departure.

## B41-L04 — Analyze retention and satisfaction jointly

Possible patterns:
- high satisfaction + high retention;
- high satisfaction + low retention — possible strong selection;
- low satisfaction + high retention — possible exit barriers;
- low satisfaction + low retention.

Neither satisfaction nor retention alone defines success.

## B41-L05 — Reconcile changing denominators

For repeated outcome surveys track:
- entrants;
- departures;
- expulsions;
- deaths;
- nonresponse;
- children aging in/out;
- household splits/mergers.

A rising average in a changing population is not necessarily within-person improvement.

## B41-L06 — Do not automatically penalize turnover

Exit can represent:
- harm/mismatch;
- healthy self-selection;
- successful temporary participation;
- graduation;
- work/family opportunity;
- adaptive migration/fission.

Interpret type and later outcomes.

## B41-L07 — Maintain independent opt-in leaver research contact where appropriate

When ethical/legal:
- keep research contact independent of community leadership;
- allow confidential post-exit participation;
- keep claims/benefits separate from favorable responses;
- preserve long-term contact only with consent.

## B41-L08 — Children/families require trajectory outcomes

A community cannot infer child/family success only from families still present if dissatisfied or harmed households leave disproportionately.

Where feasible follow education, health, family contact and wellbeing across transition.

## B41-L09 — Admission, retention and expulsion are part of the outcome model

Different communities select different populations through:
- admissions;
- probation;
- role access;
- social pressure;
- exit affordability;
- expulsion;
- research response.

Outcome comparisons should include these selection mechanisms.

## B41-L10 — Keep current-resident surveys; label them correctly

They answer:

> How do people currently choosing/able to remain experience the community?

That is important. It is simply a different estimand from entrant-cohort success or causal effect.

# Combined research-center gates after Batches 38–41

Before a strong comparative claim, ask:

1. **Measurement:** are outcomes classified comparably? — C022.
2. **Valuation:** is there a legitimate shared value/aggregation rule? — Batch 39.
3. **Diffusion:** did research/recommendation alter the institutional variation being studied? — Batch 40.
4. **Selection:** did the practice alter who remained observable? — Batch 41.

These should become explicit fields in any future communal research protocol.
