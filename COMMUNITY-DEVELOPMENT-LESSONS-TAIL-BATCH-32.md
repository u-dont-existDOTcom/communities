# Community Development Lessons — Creative Tail Batch 32

Updated: 2026-08-15  
Status: append-only practical supplement; no originality claim

## Main lesson — Every “best practice” needs a scaling regime
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + RESEARCH-CONTROL

A community research center should not publish merely:

> Practice X worked well in Community Y.

It should also say what is expected to happen if many more communities adopt X.

Classify at least:

- **approximately scale-invariant** — effect expected to remain similar;
- **frequency-dependent** — effect changes as adoption/prevalence changes;
- **shared-capacity limited** — benefit depends on finite specialists, land, finance, markets, federation staff, etc.;
- **wider-system dependent / not scale-closed** — success imports critical inputs from outside the movement;
- **positive network-effect** — effect improves as movement scale creates mobility, pooled services, insurance, markets, bargaining, etc.

This prevents a successful niche from being mislabeled a universal model.

---

## B32-L01 — Separate local efficacy from movement-wide equilibrium effect
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

A practice can help one community while creating spillovers that change outcomes once many communities use it.

Before broad replication, ask:

- what does adoption do to prices/markets?;
- applicant/recruit pools?;
- shared specialists?;
- land/sites?;
- grants/donors?;
- federation queues?;
- neighboring communities?;
- political/legal environment?

Do not assume the observed small-scale effect is constant.

---

## B32-L02 — Run a scale-closure matrix for critical inputs
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

For each important input to a community design, record:

1. current source;
2. whether source is inside or outside the movement;
3. current quantity/capacity;
4. what happens if movement adoption becomes 10%, 50%, 90% of the target population;
5. what internal/federated production could substitute;
6. transition time/capital needed to create that substitute.

Inputs can include:

- trained adults and specialists;
- education/apprenticeship;
- land;
- construction capacity;
- capital/credit;
- health care;
- tools/technology;
- markets/customers;
- energy;
- food inputs;
- transport;
- legal/public infrastructure.

---

## B32-L03 — Measure imported-skill dependence
**Status:** PRACTICAL / RESEARCH-CONTROL

A community can appear capable because it recruits adults whose education and professional skills were produced elsewhere.

Track, for critical roles:

- where members acquired the skill;
- whether the community/movement could train replacements itself;
- training time and cost;
- whether children/young adults have a pathway into the role;
- whether federation-level training could supply it.

This matters to claims of reproducibility and worldwide scaling.

---

## B32-L04 — Distinguish demonstration value from universalizability
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

A community may be extremely valuable even if its complete model cannot be copied everywhere.

Possible roles include:

- demonstration project;
- experimental laboratory;
- training center;
- niche provider;
- sanctuary/refuge;
- source of one transferable practice;
- fully replicable living system.

State which role the evidence supports rather than forcing all successes into “universal model.”

---

## B32-L05 — Record whether a practice depends on being rare
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Examples:

- a niche business profitable because few competitors use it;
- a recruitment pitch drawing from a limited subculture;
- a grant strategy using a finite funding pool;
- a political strategy that succeeds because it is unusual;
- a land strategy dependent on a limited class of cheap sites.

If rarity contributes to the benefit, the practice should not be disseminated as though payoff were adoption-invariant.

---

## B32-L06 — Estimate an adoption-response curve when frequency dependence is plausible
**Status:** PRACTICAL / RESEARCH-CONTROL

Instead of one effect estimate, ask what the expected effect looks like at different saturation levels.

Even rough bands can help:

- low adoption;
- moderate adoption;
- widespread adoption;
- near-universal adoption.

State the mechanism and uncertainty when data are insufficient.

---

## B32-L07 — Do not create a movement monoculture from one observed winner
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

If scaling effects are unknown, retain enough diversity that the movement continues learning across different community designs and contexts.

This is not an argument against shared lessons or standards, especially for basic rights/safety. It concerns context-sensitive or equilibrium-sensitive practices whose payoff may depend on adoption patterns.

---

## B32-L08 — Compare portfolios when practices interact
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

If different community types complement or compete with each other, the relevant question may be:

> What mixture produces the strongest movement?

rather than:

> Which single type is best?

Examples could include different economic niches, training functions, geographic contexts, care capacities, or experimentation roles.

Avoid turning this into central planning; autonomous communities still choose their own form. The federation/research center can report interaction effects.

---

## B32-L09 — Track positive network effects too
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Scaling can improve a practice when more communities create:

- intercommunity migration options;
- specialist pools;
- schools/training;
- pooled insurance/reserves;
- shared purchasing/markets;
- political leverage;
- emergency mutual aid;
- more viable daughter-community matches.

A scaling audit should not assume only dilution or congestion.

---

## B32-L10 — Put wider-system dependence into public research reports
**Status:** PRACTICAL / RESEARCH-CONTROL

When presenting a successful community, state explicitly which capacities remain supplied by mainstream/public/external systems.

This makes it possible to distinguish:

- local autonomy;
- resilience;
- niche success;
- movement-level self-provision;
- actual scale closure.

Do not use “self-sufficient” as a loose synonym for successful communal coordination.

# Batch 32 disposition

No Creative Tail survivor. Policy-scaling/general-equilibrium research already owns the core mechanism, and ecovillage research already recognizes contextual scaling and wider-system dependence.

The status-quo improvement is methodological:

> **Before the movement research center calls something a best practice, state what happens to its payoff and its input sources as adoption rises.**
