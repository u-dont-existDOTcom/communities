# Main lesson — Separate fission composition from destination allocation

**Status:** HIGH-VALUE PRACTICAL / KNOWN TARGET PRACTICE

Hutterite branching practice provides a strong communal precedent: form two viable groups first, then decide by lot which group remains at the mother colony and which moves to the daughter.

The important mechanism is **destination blindness**, not random person assignment.

---

## B48-L01 — Build two viable groups before either knows which gets the incumbent site

Balance relevant capacities first:
- leadership/succession;
- work/business/farming skills;
- childcare/care;
- age structure;
- family units;
- health/support needs;
- maintenance/trades;
- social continuity.

Then assign mother/daughter destination under a precommitted process.

## B48-L02 — Randomize destination, not necessarily people

Do not split families/care relationships arbitrarily in the name of fairness.

Stronger structure:
1. protect defined relational units;
2. construct two viable groups;
3. assign destination only after composition is fixed.

## B48-L03 — Destination blindness reduces manipulation

If everyone knows who gets the established site while groups are being formed:
- people can lobby to stay;
- leaders can retain allies/preferred operators;
- unpopular members can be pushed toward the daughter;
- daughter status can become a `loser group` marker.

Destination-blind composition reduces those channels.

## B48-L04 — Define protected household/care units before partition

Examples:
- nuclear households;
- coparenting structures;
- dependent elders/caregivers;
- critical medical support links.

## B48-L05 — Separate skill balance from social preference

A purely self-selected group can be comfortable but functionally fragile. A centrally balanced group can be viable but coercive.

Set viability constraints, then allow bounded preference negotiation where possible.

## B48-L06 — Make both groups transition-ready before destination assignment

The Lehrerleut pack-before-lot tradition is a strong commitment device.

Modern equivalents:
- both groups complete staffing plans;
- both have budgets/assets mapped;
- both have records/transport ready;
- both demonstrate operating capacity before site assignment.

## B48-L07 — Compare functional capacity, not only asset book value

Equal money/assets do not guarantee equal viability when land, infrastructure, market access, debt or experienced operators differ.

## B48-L08 — Plan transitional mother→daughter support explicitly

Define:
- goods/services;
- mentoring;
- duration;
- repayment if any;
- independence/sunset criteria.

Record this support in C005 reproduction studies so subsidized survival is not mistaken for autonomous robustness.

## B48-L09 — Lot legitimacy does not guarantee good outcomes

Randomness can reduce favoritism but does not prove:
- good composition;
- consent;
- child/family welfare;
- fair land/assets;
- long-term viability.

Follow outcomes.

## B48-L10 — Compare lot-based and mutual-consent fission empirically

Hutterite branches now provide different branching practices: Lehrerleut often retain lot-based branching while other Leuts more often use discussion/mutual consent.

This is a potentially valuable natural comparison, but many branch-level confounders remain.

## B48-L11 — Add fission-process covariates to C005

Future lineage data should record:
- lot / volunteer / mutual-consent mechanism;
- whether group composition was fixed before destination was known;
- family-unit constraints;
- leadership/skill balance;
- asset/function balance;
- transitional subsidy;
- daughter time-to-reproduction;
- later transfers/conflict/failure.

## B48-L12 — Use destination blindness only where destination status could contaminate composition

The generic mechanism can apply beyond colony fission, but it is most useful when one destination/site/role is predictably more desirable and composition decisions could be manipulated around that advantage.
