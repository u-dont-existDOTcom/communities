## B25-L01 — Propagate retired-rule tombstones with replicated governance templates
**Status:** TAIL-PROVISIONAL  
**Creative Tail:** C018

A movement can preserve old policies perfectly and still reintroduce a failed rule if daughter communities, starter kits, old handbooks, or forked constitutions replicate the positive template separately from the record that says why a pattern was retired.

For high-consequence governance patterns, create a durable **tombstone** containing:

1. the retired rule/pattern and its semantic scope;
2. version/date and context;
3. why it was retired;
4. evidence/counterexample/failure mode;
5. replacement or safer alternative, if any;
6. whether the failure is universal or context-specific;
7. conditions for legitimate reconsideration;
8. the process/authority required to override the tombstone.

When a daughter community, fork, federation starter template, restored archive, or imported policy set is created, propagate/check relevant tombstones alongside current rules.

The tombstone is not an eternal ban. Its purpose is **forced review before resurrection**.

### Semantic rather than literal matching

Record the failed authority relationship or mechanism, not only the exact old wording. If a founder-veto rule was retired because the founder controlled both accusation and appeal, a renamed clause recreating the same conflict should trigger review.

### Why this may improve current practice

Intentional-community archives and some policy documents already preserve amendment/replacement history. The additional operational rule is to make important negative knowledge travel through the same replication channel as the governance templates that could otherwise reintroduce it.

## B25-L02 — Preserve superseded decision rationale, not only the old text
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

When a consequential policy changes, preserve:

- what problem the old rule attempted to solve;
- why it was adopted;
- what evidence later challenged it;
- what consequences appeared;
- why the replacement was chosen;
- which alternatives were rejected.

This reduces institutional forgetting and prevents newcomers from interpreting an apparently odd current safeguard as arbitrary bureaucracy.

Use concise decision records rather than requiring future members to reconstruct rationale from years of meeting minutes.

## B25-L03 — Map overlapping local/federation authority before conflicting decisions occur
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Polycentric/federated governance creates real value but also overlapping jurisdiction.

For each consequential function, specify:

- local exclusive authority;
- federation exclusive authority;
- concurrent authority;
- rights/floors the local unit cannot waive;
- who wins or suspends action when decisions conflict;
- which actions are reversible while jurisdiction is disputed;
- appeal/arbitration route;
- emergency rule and expiration.

Do not assume autonomous bodies will always communicate before acting.

## B25-L04 — Define a federation-disconnect / governance-partition mode
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

If a community depends on federation review, shared records, pooled funds, specialist staff, or appellate bodies, decide in advance what happens when those links are unavailable.

Classify functions into:

- **continue locally:** ordinary reversible operations within existing authority;
- **continue under temporary bounded authority:** urgent necessities with clear limits/sunset;
- **freeze:** major irreversible changes whose legitimacy depends on unavailable review;
- **alternate route:** designated backup community/body/provider;
- **automatic escalation:** when connection is not restored within a defined time.

A written right whose only reviewer is offline can become practically dead even when its text remains unchanged.

## B25-L05 — Predefine graceful degradation: protect necessities and rights before convenience
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

A severe shortage of money, labor, housing, energy, transport, or leadership attention should not force the community to invent priorities during panic.

Define a degradation order that protects at minimum:

- food, water, shelter and essential utilities;
- urgent health access;
- child necessities and safeguarding;
- direct reporting/bypass routes;
- essential records and identity/property documents;
- usable exit and emergency relocation;
- core financial controls.

Then identify functions that can be reduced, deferred, simplified, or temporarily suspended first.

The point is not to prescribe one universal ordering; it is to prevent rights and necessities from disappearing accidentally because less important activities have stronger internal constituencies.

## B25-L06 — Stagger major movement-wide reforms when simultaneous adoption would create common-mode risk
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

If a federation wants to change governance, accounting, education, admission, technology, or another system across many communities, avoid synchronizing every unit onto a materially untested design unless there is a compelling reason.

Where ethically and operationally appropriate:

1. begin with willing pilot communities;
2. predefine outcome and failure measures;
3. retain some unaffected comparison/reference experience;
4. analyze spillovers;
5. expand, revise, or stop based on evidence.

Do not create involuntary control groups for rights or safety protections that are already clearly required.

## B25-L07 — Treat cross-community experiments as interfering systems, not independent observations
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + RESEARCH-CONTROL

Communities in one movement can exchange:

- members;
- teachers/facilitators;
- money and rescue support;
- governance ideas;
- markets and suppliers;
- reputation/recruitment channels;
- federation staff.

Therefore a reform in Community A can affect Community B even if B did not formally adopt it.

When comparing daughter communities or pilots, record these spillovers. Do not call one community an independent control simply because its constitution lacks the new rule.

## B25-L08 — Treat major rule changes as migrations of existing state
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Editing constitutional text is not enough when a rule change affects existing members, offices, benefits, debts, records, sanctions, appeals, or pending cases.

For each major change, ask:

- what existing states are now invalid or ambiguous?;
- who is grandfathered and why?;
- what pending cases use old versus new procedure?;
- which records/forms/accounts need conversion?;
- what authority must be revoked or reissued?;
- which external contracts or legal filings must change?;
- what rollback/compensation exists if migration fails?;
- which steps are irreversible and therefore require stronger preconditions?

A new constitution running on unmigrated old state can produce contradictory rights and authorities.

## B25-L09 — Use tamper-evident precommitment for high-stakes records when later review depends on local originals
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Where a local community controls records that may later be reviewed by a federation or independent body, consider proportionate tamper-evident methods for high-stakes material:

- timestamped signed records;
- cryptographic hashes/commitments;
- append-only audit trails;
- duplicate custody with an independent body.

The goal is not public surveillance. Sensitive content can remain private while its existence/version is committed.

Use only where the evidence-integrity benefit justifies privacy, technical, and administrative cost.

## B25-L10 — Version the definitions of success and safety metrics
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + RESEARCH-CONTROL

If a federation changes how it defines `member departure`, `conflict`, `safety incident`, `successful admission`, `child wellbeing`, `financial distress`, or `daughter community`, old and new observations may no longer measure the same construct.

Preserve metric/schema versions and document transformations. Do not silently compare historical values under incompatible definitions.

This matters especially for the movement research center envisioned in the article: better measurement can otherwise create a fake trend merely by changing the measurement rule.

---

## B25-L11 — Check imported governance documents for staleness before adoption
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN; companion to C018

Every reusable policy/template should carry enough metadata to answer:

- current version?;
- superseded by what?;
- deprecated for what reason?;
- applicable to which legal/community context?;
- known incompatible later rules?;
- associated tombstones/incident lessons?

A document being in the federation archive means it is historically available, not that it is current or recommended.

---

## B25-L12 — Under uncertain authority, default carefully on irreversible action
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

When two bodies plausibly claim authority and the conflict cannot immediately be resolved, the default should not simply be "whoever acts first wins."

For high-consequence actions, predefine whether uncertainty should:

- preserve the status quo;
- allow only reversible temporary action;
- freeze coercive/property changes;
- permit narrowly defined emergency protection;
- invoke a neutral/federation conflict route.

Different functions need different defaults. The lesson is to choose the default before the authority conflict occurs.

# Batch 25 practical disposition

### Most distinctive operational lesson

**B25-L01 / C018:** negative governance knowledge must replicate with positive templates. A movement can have excellent archives and still suffer institutional "zombie rules" if retired-pattern metadata does not travel to daughters, forks and starter packets.

### Strong useful-but-known additions

The remaining Batch 25 lessons strengthen:

- jurisdictional clarity;
- federation fault tolerance;
- graceful degradation;
- safe movement experimentation;
- research validity under spillovers;
- rule-change migration discipline;
- evidence integrity;
- longitudinal measurement integrity.

None of those is being claimed as a new general theory.
