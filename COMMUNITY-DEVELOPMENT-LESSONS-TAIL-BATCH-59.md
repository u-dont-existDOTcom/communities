# Main lesson — Adult membership choice needs a live material non-membership option

**Status:** TAIL-PROVISIONAL NARROW RESIDUAL  
**Creative Tail:** C026 — Adulthood option grant / neutral transition package

The communities research corpus already owns:
- non-inherited adult membership;
- child exit capacity;
- usable rather than paper exit;
- transition support;
- leaving-fund evidence;
- later-outcome and family-contact concerns.

Therefore the broad idea `young people raised in a commune should choose adult membership rather than inherit it automatically` is **not new**.

The surviving practical addition is narrower:

> **The portable resources that make adult non-membership a real option should be available before the young person opts into the adult property/work/governance regime, rather than requiring them to join first and later qualify for exit help.**

---

## B59-L01 — Define an explicit adulthood transition

Possible paths:
- opt/apply into full adult membership;
- bounded transition/associate status;
- explore sister communities;
- live/work/study outside for a defined period;
- choose non-membership/leave.

Exact legal forms vary.

## B59-L02 — Put transition resources before the membership decision

Avoid a sequence where a young person must:
1. accept the adult communal regime;
2. surrender/merge relevant property or obligations;
3. later resign;
4. only then receive leaving assistance.

That sequence biases the initial consent.

## B59-L03 — Minimum portability domains

Depending on resources/jurisdiction:
- identity/citizenship documents;
- health and education records;
- transcripts/skills/work-history references;
- personal possessions;
- private banking/payment access;
- transport;
- temporary housing;
- work/training support;
- independent advice;
- private external communication;
- starter funds or equivalent transition support where feasible.

No universal cash amount is proposed.

## B59-L04 — Maintain portable records throughout childhood/adolescence

A community-raised young adult may have real skills that outsiders cannot verify.

Keep portable records of:
- education;
- apprenticeships;
- work roles;
- licenses/certifications;
- training;
- references not controlled by one leader;
- health records.

## B59-L05 — Use an independent transition adviser

The adviser should not be solely the person/community office that benefits from retaining the young adult's labor or membership.

Federation-level or external advice can reduce this conflict.

## B59-L06 — Do not require hostility or abuse claims to qualify for transition help

A young adult may choose a different life for:
- education;
- work;
- romance;
- curiosity;
- different values;
- independence.

Basic option capacity should not depend on proving wrongdoing.

## B59-L07 — Keep family contact distinct from membership status

Where consistent with safety and individual choice, choosing non-membership should not automatically mean loss of ordinary parent/sibling contact.

Make religious/social consequences explicit rather than hidden in the membership choice.

## B59-L08 — Consider bounded outside-life exploration

Where feasible, allow a defined period to:
- study/work outside;
- live in another community;
- compare alternatives;
- later decide whether to apply for adult membership.

This need not create indefinite entitlement to commune resources.

## B59-L09 — Do not condition portable education/assets on promising to stay

If credentials, savings, licenses, or training become forfeitable when a young adult declines adult membership, they function as retention leverage.

Audit portability explicitly.

## B59-L10 — Transition capacity is not a claim to a proportional share of communal assets

A community can retain collective ownership while providing a practical adulthood option package.

Do not conflate exit capability with individual ownership of land/business assets.

## B59-L11 — Measure choice symmetry

At adulthood record:
- housing/support available on join versus non-join paths;
- portable records/credentials;
- independent advice;
- financial/transport capacity;
- family/social consequences;
- later outcomes.

This makes `voluntary adult membership` more empirically inspectable.

## B59-L12 — High youth retention is not self-validating evidence

High retention can reflect:
- genuine preference;
- high community quality;
- family bonds;
- poor outside options;
- material exit barriers;
- religious/social pressure.

Interpret retention together with option quality and later trajectories.

## B59-L13 — Federation infrastructure can support the adulthood option

Possible shared resources:
- transition housing;
- career/education advice;
- transport grants;
- credential conversion;
- sister-community placements;
- Batch 46 private transfer exploration.

## B59-L14 — Follow all adulthood paths

Research:
- young adults who join;
- leave;
- move to sister communities;
- defer/explore and later return.

Measure wellbeing, work/education, financial stability, family contact, later evaluation of choice freedom, and later movement participation.
