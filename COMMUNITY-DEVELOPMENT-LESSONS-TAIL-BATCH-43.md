# Main lesson — Member self-report is an incentive-sensitive evidence channel

**Status:** FOUNDATIONAL RESEARCH-CONTROL / KNOWN

In communal and especially high-control settings, a respondent may understand a survey question perfectly and still have reasons to give a favorable answer because criticism could threaten belonging, relationships, housing, status, identity, leader relations, or the group’s reputation.

Therefore survey validity depends not only on wording but on the **reporting environment**.

Do not infer that commune members systematically lie. The design must be robust to both genuine satisfaction and strategic/identity-protective positive reporting.

---

## B43-L01 — Add an explicit disclosure-incentive gate

Before interpreting member self-report, document whether unfavorable answers could plausibly create:
- social sanction;
- reputational cost;
- leader disapproval;
- housing/work/benefit risk;
- relationship/family consequences;
- perceived harm to the community’s standing;
- fear of being identified as disloyal.

If yes, treat the response channel as potentially incentive-sensitive.

## B43-L02 — Keep identifiable raw data outside the local community

For sensitive research:
- use federation/external independent data custody;
- separate participation tokens/identity from answer storage;
- prevent local leaders/committees from seeing individual raw responses;
- suppress small cells and metadata that make reidentification easy;
- publish only appropriately aggregated outputs.

`Confidential` is weak if members reasonably believe local leadership can identify them.

## B43-L03 — Make survey completion genuinely private

Do not collect sensitive answers:
- during group meetings;
- on supervised/shared devices;
- with leaders present;
- with required post-survey discussion;
- through visible channels that reveal who gave which response.

Offer private self-administered/off-site options where feasible.

## B43-L04 — Anonymity is a perceived condition, not a checkbox

In a small commune, a unique incident or phrase can identify the respondent even if names are removed.

Audit:
- sample/cell size;
- unique event details;
- timestamps;
- quotations;
- device/account metadata;
- who knows who participated.

Let participants separately consent to use of narrative quotations.

## B43-L05 — Randomize privacy/mode where sample size permits

For federation-scale samples, compare equivalent sensitive questions under different conditions such as:
- confidential vs stronger anonymity;
- interviewer vs self-administered;
- direct vs indirect sensitive-question method.

If reported outcomes shift materially, record a **mode effect** rather than assuming one mode is perfectly truthful.

## B43-L06 — Use indirect sensitive-question techniques cautiously

List experiments, randomized response and similar methods can reduce individual disclosure risk but are inefficient and assumption-sensitive.

A single commune will often be too small for useful inference. Pool across sufficiently comparable federation samples when justified.

## B43-L07 — Separate participation incentives from favorable answers

Do not make grants, status, federation approval, membership renewal, or reputation depend directly on looking happy/safe in survey outcomes unless the gaming problem is explicitly modeled.

Reward data completeness/participation separately, if at all.

## B43-L08 — Maintain independent leaver/former-member channels

Current members and former members have different incentives and biases.

Neither should be treated automatically as truth.

Track separately where feasible:
- current members;
- recent voluntary leavers;
- pressured/expelled members;
- former children/households;
- applicants who declined membership.

## B43-L09 — Combine self-report with non-self-report outcomes

Examples where lawful/ethical:
- exit delay/liquidity;
- labor/work records;
- turnover;
- child education/health continuity;
- injuries/medical utilization;
- remedy/appeal completion;
- financial solvency;
- verified procedural records;
- post-exit trajectories;
- reproduction/fission outcomes.

Objective records have their own missingness/gaming problems and measure different constructs; use them as independent channels, not replacements for lived experience.

## B43-L10 — Ask concrete behavior before loaded identity labels

A respondent may reject `I was coerced` while truthfully endorsing concrete facts such as:
- dissent could cost housing;
- leaving would trigger shunning;
- family contact was restricted;
- one leader controlled money/access;
- refusal brought sanctions.

Record concrete event/constraint items separately from the respondent’s own interpretive label.

## B43-L11 — Use face-saving wording where appropriate

Reduce needless moral threat:
- normalize ordinary difficulties;
- ask frequency/degree before moral labels;
- offer uncertainty options;
- avoid wording that clearly signals the `good member` answer.

Do not euphemize serious harm away.

## B43-L12 — Measure perceived disclosure consequences

Possible diagnostic items:
- confidence that local leaders cannot see answers;
- whether another member could infer the respondent from context;
- whether criticism would carry social/material cost;
- whether members discussed how to answer;
- whether the community framed the research as reputationally important.

These describe the reporting environment; they do not mechanically correct bias.

## B43-L13 — Do not treat social-desirability/lie scales as truth detectors

Such scales can be useful diagnostics but can reflect:
- actual strategic self-presentation;
- genuinely internalized moral/religious norms;
- cultural response style;
- the stakes of the specific survey.

Never convert them into a simple arithmetic correction of happiness/safety scores.

## B43-L14 — Preserve an independent route to the research center

Members should be able to participate/contact researchers without routing through local leadership.

This is especially important for sensitive safety, exit, child/family and governance research.

## B43-L15 — Repeated low-stakes measurement can be better than one ceremonial evaluation

A highly publicized annual `community evaluation` can make face-saving pressure intense.

Repeated independent measurement can reduce the symbolic stakes of any one response and improve longitudinal interpretation.

## B43-L16 — Triangulate disagreements; do not average them away

When current members, leavers and external records disagree, retain the disagreement and ask:
- what facts are disputed?;
- what each channel could observe;
- what incentives affect each source;
- whether accounts changed after exit;
- whether C021 scrutiny-conditioned evidence affects the record.

# Updated federation research-center gates

Before a strong federation-level claim, ask:

1. **Measurement gate — C022:** are the constructs/categories commensurate?
2. **Valuation gate — Batch 39:** is there a legitimate common evaluative rule?
3. **Diffusion gate — Batch 40:** has research/recommendation changed institutional variation?
4. **Selection gate — Batch 41:** has the design changed who remains observable?
5. **Constituency gate — Batch 42:** has the design changed who controls future review/definition?
6. **Disclosure-incentive gate — Batch 43:** do members have incentives/fears that systematically alter self-report?

Perfect questionnaire wording does not solve gate 6.
