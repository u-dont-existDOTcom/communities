# Main lesson — Embed reporting-bias diagnostics into communal surveys

**Status:** FOUNDATIONAL RESEARCH-CONTROL / KNOWN METHODS, HIGH PRACTICAL VALUE

The owner proposed using basic questions with expected variation to detect whether commune members are face-saving. The strongest version of this idea is to embed a **multi-method reporting-pressure battery** inside federation surveys.

Important correction:

> **Do not infer face-saving mainly from unusually low variance on ordinary questions.** A commune may genuinely be selected/homogeneous. Prefer diagnostics with known truth, randomized comparison conditions, or independent anchors.

---

## B44-L01 — Use a reporting-pressure profile, not an individual lie score

Combine several diagnostics and summarize them at community/sample level.

Use the profile to decide:
- how much uncertainty to attach to self-report;
- whether stronger privacy/indirect methods are needed;
- whether cross-community comparisons are credible;
- how much non-self-report triangulation is required.

Do **not** label an individual as dishonest from one battery score or mechanically subtract points from wellbeing/safety outcomes.

## B44-L02 — Embed validated improbable-virtue / social-desirability items

A small validated battery can identify unusually strong socially desirable responding.

Caveat: high scores can reflect genuine internalized norms/cultural style as well as strategic self-presentation. Treat this as one diagnostic only.

## B44-L03 — Add neutral overclaiming foils with known truth

The Over-Claiming Technique asks about real and nonexistent items; false familiarity with foils provides an objectively known false-positive channel and can separate response bias from knowledge.

Use neutral foils unrelated to actual community history.

Do not fabricate local incidents or memories; that risks confusing respondents and damaging research trust.

## B44-L04 — Randomize direct versus indirect measurement for a few critical sensitive outcomes

At federation scale where sample size is adequate, randomly ask some respondents a direct sensitive question and others a validated indirect version such as a list/item-count or randomized-response design.

The group-level discrepancy estimates **disclosure sensitivity** for that topic.

This is especially useful for outcomes such as:
- coercive control;
- fear of criticism;
- hidden dissatisfaction;
- unsafe behavior;
- pressure to stay;
- severe conflict.

Do not infer which individual concealed the sensitive trait.

## B44-L05 — Randomize credible privacy/mode conditions

Where ethical and operationally feasible, compare equivalent items under:
- standard confidential custody;
- stronger independent/anonymized custody;
- interviewer versus private self-administration.

A response shift is evidence of **privacy/mode sensitivity**, not proof that one condition is perfectly truthful.

Never falsely promise anonymity.

## B44-L06 — Use ingroup-label mirror vignettes to detect group-protective evaluation

Randomize otherwise identical synthetic cases framed as:
- occurring in `your community`;
- occurring in another/unlabeled community.

Compare:
- severity;
- blame;
- reportability;
- whether a formal process should trigger.

Systematic leniency toward the ingroup indicates identity-sensitive evaluation of identical described facts.

Caveat: respondents may legitimately import contextual knowledge about their own community. Keep facts explicit and interpret the differential as identity/context sensitivity, not an automatic lie measure.

## B44-L07 — Use independent-record anchors where consent and record quality permit

Include a small number of factual items for which an independent record exists, such as:
- attendance;
- credited labor;
- dates/use of formal procedures;
- other low-stakes administrative facts.

Compare self-report error on neutral versus reputation-relevant facts.

Do not assume leadership-controlled records are infallible ground truth.

## B44-L08 — Keep self-versus-peer estimates as a weak diagnostic only

Asking `How common is X among people here?` can reduce personal self-presentation pressure, but it also measures perceived norms and projection.

Never treat peer estimates as concealed self-report truth.

## B44-L09 — Repeated/reverse-coded items detect inconsistency, not face-saving itself

Use them for quality/acquiescence checks.

A respondent can give a perfectly consistent favorable story, so consistency is not honesty.

## B44-L10 — Avoid using response time as a deception detector

Response time/paradata can identify unusual answering patterns but is affected by reflection, literacy, device, disability and many other factors.

Use only as weak data-quality context.

## B44-L11 — Expected-variance compression is a secondary anomaly flag, not calibration truth

Comparing commune variance to general-population variance is dangerous because communes self-select and may genuinely be unusually homogeneous.

Only use low variance as supporting evidence when stronger diagnostics also indicate social-desirability pressure.

## B44-L12 — Use crosswise/randomized-response designs only when respondents understand them and N is adequate

Privacy-preserving indirect methods can reduce disclosure pressure and some extended designs can diagnose systematic response bias.

But they are statistically inefficient and can fail if respondents misunderstand or ignore the protocol. Small communes generally require federation-level pooling.

## B44-L13 — Longitudinal resident-to-leaver change is informative but not a truth oracle

If the same person reports much more negatively after departure, possibilities include:
- reduced fear/face-saving;
- new negative experiences;
- grievance;
- information gained after exit;
- retrospective reinterpretation.

Treat change as evidence requiring explanation, not proof that the later answer is automatically true.

# Recommended embedded survey battery

## Tier A — routine communal survey

- 4–8 validated social-desirability/improbable-virtue items;
- perceived disclosure-consequence items from Batch 43;
- repeated/reverse-coded quality items;
- C022 measurement canaries;
- entry/leaver cohort identifiers for Batch 41.

## Tier B — federation-wide research with sufficient N

- randomized direct-versus-indirect sensitive questions;
- privacy/mode randomization;
- ingroup-label mirror vignettes;
- neutral overclaiming foils;
- independent-record validation subsample.

## Tier C — high-risk methodological study

- randomized-response / extended-crosswise designs;
- repeated resident→leaver follow-up;
- independent standardized interviews/observations.

# Suggested reporting-pressure outputs

Do not produce `person X lies 37%`.

Instead report community/sample-level diagnostics such as:
- social-desirability score distribution;
- foil false-positive/overclaiming bias;
- direct–indirect disclosure gap + uncertainty;
- privacy-mode response shift;
- ingroup-vs-neutral vignette leniency;
- independent-record self-report error;
- perceived disclosure-risk distribution;
- resident-vs-leaver change.

Possible overall qualitative profile:
- low detected reporting pressure;
- moderate mode-sensitive reporting;
- strong group-protective response pattern;
- contradictory diagnostics / high uncertainty.

Use this profile to qualify inference, not to rank moral character.

---

# Why known/randomized anchors are better than assumed variance

The strongest diagnostics manufacture or know the comparison benchmark:

- nonexistent foils have known truth;
- random assignment creates exchangeable groups;
- independent records provide external anchors;
- identical vignettes hold described facts fixed.

That is much stronger than assuming commune members should have the same ordinary response distribution as outsiders.
