# Community Development Lessons — Creative Tail Batch 33

Updated: 2026-08-15  
Status: append-only practical supplement; no originality claim

## Main lesson — Intercommunity transfer should not launder unresolved outcomes
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + RESEARCH-CONTROL

A community can look better after a difficult member or household leaves even when nobody's life improved and the burden merely moved to another community or outside institution.

For serious conflict, safety, care, housing, child/family, or high-support cases, track separately:

1. origin-community outcome;
2. transfer/exit process;
3. destination-community outcome where applicable;
4. person/household later outcome;
5. movement-wide resource/risk effect;
6. reason for transfer.

A supported move to a better-fitting community can be a genuine success. Expulsion, abandonment, or hidden burden shifting is not.

---

## B33-L01 — Do not mark a case resolved merely because the person leaves
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Use statuses such as:

- resolved locally;
- transferred with support;
- voluntary move, later outcome pending;
- separation/expulsion, later outcome pending;
- moved outside movement;
- unresolved/lost to follow-up.

This is especially important for child, survivor, health, housing, family-contact and severe-conflict outcomes.

---

## B33-L02 — Preserve leaver follow-up in the research architecture
**Status:** EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED + PRACTICAL

Where ethical and feasible, follow leavers through confidential/independent routes rather than relying only on current-member surveys.

Do not require former members to engage with the institution they left. Independent evaluators, alumni networks, or federation-level research may be safer routes.

---

## B33-L03 — Record baseline state at admission or transfer
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN + RESEARCH-CONTROL

When comparing community outcomes, distinguish conditions that were already present from those that emerged after entry.

Relevant baseline information can include, only where appropriate and non-discriminatory:

- material needs;
- family/child situation;
- health/care needs;
- skill/training level;
- unresolved outside obligations;
- current housing/employment stability;
- prior community/federation support.

Do not turn baseline context into a global danger label or exclusion score.

---

## B33-L04 — Avoid raw outcome league tables across radically different case mix
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

A community serving people with greater support needs can have worse raw numbers while doing excellent work.

Prefer:

- stratified outcomes;
- baseline-to-later change;
- transparent context;
- uncertainty;
- direct rights/safety floors that are not risk-adjusted away.

Opaque risk adjustment can itself hide unfairness, so publish assumptions rather than one supposedly objective ranking.

---

## B33-L05 — Track whether burden decreased or merely moved
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

When a transfer or exclusion improves local conditions, ask:

- did the person's wellbeing improve?;
- did risk/harm decrease?;
- did support need decrease?;
- did another community inherit the same or greater burden?;
- did public/family systems absorb the cost?;
- did the movement supply enough support to the receiving community?

Local relief can be real and necessary while total burden remains unchanged.

---

## B33-L06 — Successful transfer is a legitimate positive outcome category
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

Do not create metrics that incentivize communities to retain everyone forever simply to avoid a departure penalty.

A move can be successful when:

- the person prefers the destination;
- fit/resources are better;
- housing/care/relationships remain safe;
- material entitlements and records travel;
- family/child rights are protected;
- the destination actually remains viable later.

The research center should distinguish supported mobility from institutional failure.

---

## B33-L07 — Map transfer networks and net receiving communities
**Status:** PRACTICAL / RESEARCH-CONTROL

At federation scale, record which communities become frequent:

- senders;
- receivers;
- transition/landing communities;
- specialist-care/support communities;
- founder/training communities.

If some communities systematically absorb high-support cases, federation funding/labor/support should recognize the function rather than letting their local metrics make them appear worse.

---

## B33-L08 — Keep denominators visible after attrition
**Status:** PRACTICAL / KNOWN

For satisfaction, safety, retention, education, child wellbeing and other outcomes, report:

- starting cohort;
- current cohort;
- number/reasons for exits;
- transferred versus lost follow-up;
- outcome availability among leavers.

An improving average among whoever remains may reflect improvement, selection, or both.

---

## B33-L09 — Standardize transfer/exit reason coding without pretending motives are uncontested
**Status:** PRACTICAL / RESEARCH-CONTROL

Use consistent categories but allow multiple perspectives:

- member-stated reason;
- community-stated reason;
- independent/federation classification if one exists;
- unresolved disagreement.

Do not let the community unilaterally code every difficult departure as `voluntary` or every leaver unilaterally determine a contested factual finding.

---

## B33-L10 — Evaluate movement outcomes at the person/household level as well as the community level
**Status:** PRACTICAL / RESEARCH-CONTROL

A federation can improve average community metrics while failing the people who move between communities.

For major research questions, maintain both:

- community/institution trajectories;
- person/household trajectories across institutions.

This is especially relevant to the project's goal of helping people live better rather than merely maximizing institutional survival.

# Batch 33 disposition

No Creative Tail survivor. Healthcare/provider-performance literatures already establish cream skimming, patient-transfer selection and case-mix/risk-adjustment problems, while the existing communities synthesis already emphasizes leaver and later outcomes.

The status-quo improvement is:

> **Do not let a community improve its report card merely by moving the hard outcome out of its denominator. Follow the person and the burden across the movement.**
